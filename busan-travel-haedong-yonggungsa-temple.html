Haedong Yonggungsa Temple: Busan’s Seaside Sanctuary

Published 8 October 2026

At a glance

  • Where: 86 Yonggung-gil, Gijang, Busan
  • What to expect: A Buddhist temple complex set beside the sea, with coastal views and paths that include stairs.
  • Google Maps: 4.5/5 from 27,862 reviews, according to the current listing supplied for this guide.
  • Best for: Visitors looking for a temple visit with a striking seaside setting.

Many of Busan’s best-known sights are associated with beaches, markets and city neighborhoods. Haedong Yonggungsa offers a different kind of coastal stop: a Buddhist temple beside the water, where traditional buildings and devotional spaces meet open sea views. Its setting is the main draw. Rather than standing in a quiet mountain landscape, the temple occupies a rocky stretch of coast in Gijang, northeast of central Busan.

The combination makes this a memorable visit, whether you are interested in Korean Buddhism, architecture, photography or simply seeing another side of the city. It is also a place of worship, not only a sightseeing attraction, so a little consideration for people praying helps make the visit more comfortable for everyone.

What makes the temple worth visiting?

Haedong Yonggungsa is especially distinctive for its location. From within the temple grounds, you can look out toward the sea and hear the waves below. The coastal setting gives the visit a very different atmosphere from temples surrounded by forest or mountains. The contrast between the temple’s buildings, the rocky shoreline and the water is also what makes it a popular place for photographs.

Take time to explore at an unhurried pace rather than treating the temple as a single viewpoint. The approach and grounds include steps and walking paths, with different perspectives along the way. Pause to look at the buildings and their details, but be mindful of signs and any areas set aside for worship. If you are visiting primarily for the scenery, allow time to enjoy the view without blocking narrow paths or gathering around people who are praying.

How to plan your visit

The temple is at 86 Yonggung-gil, Gijang, Busan. Since the visit involves walking and stairs, wear comfortable shoes and bring water if you expect to spend time exploring the grounds. The coastal setting can feel exposed to sun or wind, so dressing for the day’s weather is sensible. If mobility or step-free access is important to your group, check current site information before setting out rather than assuming every part of the grounds will be accessible.

Haedong Yonggungsa is outside Busan’s central districts, so plan it as a dedicated outing or combine it with another stop in the Gijang area. For your route, use a current map or transport app and check the journey from your starting point on the day. Bus routes, traffic and travel times can change, and the most convenient option depends on where you are staying. The official website is another useful place to check for visitor information before you go.

There is no need to rush through the grounds. If you want quieter photographs, arriving earlier in the day may help, although conditions and visitor numbers vary. Weather also changes the experience: clear skies can make the sea views more vivid, while cloudy or windy conditions give the coast a different mood. Check the forecast and any current notices before travelling.

Temple etiquette and photography

Visitors are welcome to appreciate the site, but remember that it remains a religious place. Keep your voice low around prayer spaces, avoid interrupting worship, and follow posted instructions. Ask permission before photographing people, especially anyone engaged in prayer. If a space is marked as restricted, respect the boundary even if it appears to offer a good photo angle.

For photos of the coastline and temple architecture, take care near steps and uneven ground. Keep pathways clear, and do not climb on walls or rocks for a better view. These small courtesies help protect both the site and the experience of other visitors.

Good to know

  • Allow time to walk: The visit involves moving through a temple complex rather than viewing everything from one spot; expect stairs and paths.
  • Check details before departure: Confirm current visitor guidance and route information using the official site and a live map.
  • Dress for the coast: Comfortable footwear is useful, and weather-appropriate layers can make time outdoors more pleasant.
  • Be considerate: Keep noise down near worship areas and follow signs about access and photography.
  • Use the rating as a reference: Google Maps currently lists the temple at 4.5/5 from 27,862 reviews; ratings and review totals can change.

FAQ

Is Haedong Yonggungsa a good choice for first-time visitors to Busan?
Yes, if you want to see a temple in a memorable coastal setting. Allow extra time for the journey from your starting point and for walking around the grounds.

Is the temple suitable for visitors with limited mobility?
The grounds include stairs and walking paths, so access may be challenging for some visitors. Check current accessibility information before your visit.

Can I take photographs inside the temple grounds?
Photography is part of many visitors’ experience, but follow posted rules, avoid restricted areas and do not photograph people praying without permission.

ABOUT KOREA PLAINLY

Korea,
made easy.

Korea Plainly is a practical English-language guide to everyday Korea. We turn confusing travel details, local habits, transportation, food, apps and neighborhoods into simple information that visitors can actually use.

Who runs Korea Plainly

Korea Plainly is run by Jungsuk Kim (김중석), a Korean living in Korea. I started the site because I want to introduce visitors to the many beautiful places across Korea, from the big cities to the quieter regions that first-time travellers often miss, and to make the practical side of a trip feel simple.

Questions, corrections and suggestions are always welcome: return0508@naver.com.

What we cover

From Seoul's subway and T-money cards to convenience stores, Korean menus, cafes, restaurants, payments, useful Korean phrases and neighborhood guides, Korea Plainly focuses on practical questions that come up when exploring Korea.

Our approach

Clear first. Useful second. No unnecessary jargon. Each guide is designed to be easy to scan on a phone and useful before, during or after a trip to Korea.

How our guides are made

Our general guides explain how everyday things work in Korea: transport, money, phones, food and etiquette. Our regional articles introduce specific places, using public information such as Google Maps and Naver listings, official websites and opening details.

We use AI tools to help research and draft articles. Every article shows the date it was published, and anything that changes often, such as fares, prices and entry rules, is written as approximate with the year, or linked to the official source so you can confirm it.

Checking and corrections

The site is checked every day for broken pages, off-topic photos and information that looks wrong, and older guides are expanded and updated over time. Places do change, though: opening hours, prices and rules can differ from what you read here.

If you spot something outdated or incorrect, please email return0508@naver.com with the article title and what should change. We fix confirmed errors as quickly as we can.

Photos

Most photos are our own images or freely licensed photos from Wikimedia Commons, credited under the image with the author, licence and a link to the original. Newer regional articles may use a photo from the place’s Google Maps listing, credited to its author. If you own a photo on this site and want it credited differently or removed, please email us.

Advertising and independence

Korea Plainly may show ads served by Google AdSense to keep the site free. Advertisers do not choose, review or pay for what we write, and we do not accept payment for recommendations. Read more in our privacy policy.

How Korean Convenience Stores Compare

By Korea Plainly · Published 10 October 2026

A practical guide to CU, GS25, 7-Eleven and emart24, including what to expect, how to choose a store and tips for buying food and everyday essentials.

Photo: lazy fri13th, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Korean convenience stores are useful places to pick up a quick meal, a drink, toiletries or a small travel essential. You will see familiar chains such as CU, GS25, 7-Eleven and emart24 in cities and towns. Their basic services and everyday products are often similar, but each store has its own mix of snacks, prepared food and promotions.

For visitors, the most important point is that the branch matters as much as the brand. A large store near a station may have seats and a broad food selection, while a small shop on a side street may focus on drinks and packaged snacks. Use the chain names as a guide, not as a guarantee of what a particular location will carry.

What the main chains are like

CU

CU is one of the familiar names you are likely to encounter. Its stores carry the usual convenience-store basics, including drinks, packaged snacks and ready-to-eat food. Some branches have a wider selection of meal items or seating, while smaller ones may have limited space. Look at the refrigerators and food shelves to see what is available that day; a product you have seen at another CU may not be stocked in this branch.

GS25

GS25 is another widely recognized chain with a similar mix of essentials and quick meals. It is worth checking when you want a particular kind of snack or prepared item, but there is no reliable rule that it will have a better selection than a nearby competitor. Store size, neighbourhood and delivery schedules can all affect what is on the shelves.

7-Eleven

7-Eleven offers the familiar convenience-store format: packaged food, drinks and useful everyday items. Its selection can overlap heavily with other chains, and an individual branch may also carry products or meal options that are not available at another location. If you already know the brand from home, expect a Korean product range rather than an exact copy of the stores you know elsewhere.

emart24

emart24 is another option to look for. As with the other chains, you can expect basic snacks and drinks, with the range of ready-to-eat food depending on the branch. If you are comparing nearby stores, check the actual shelves rather than choosing by name alone. A smaller or less busy shop may have fewer meal choices regardless of its chain.

Food and drinks

Convenience stores are handy for a quick breakfast, a late snack or a simple meal between activities. Common choices include packaged sandwiches, rice-based meals, instant noodles, baked goods, milk, bottled drinks and snacks. The selection changes, so check the display and packaging instead of relying on a particular product being available everywhere.

Some branches provide a microwave, hot water for instant noodles or a place to sit. These facilities are not guaranteed, and seating may be limited or unavailable. If you buy a chilled prepared meal, check the package for heating instructions. You can ask staff for help with the microwave, using a short question such as “Can I heat this?” Pointing to the food is also useful if you are unsure how to explain.

Many Korean packaged foods have labels in Korean, and ingredients or allergen information may be difficult to identify quickly. If you have an allergy or dietary restriction, do not guess from the pictures. Use a translation app to read the label, and consider carrying a written Korean translation of important food restrictions.

Comparing promotions and products

Chains often run product promotions, but the offers and participating items can change. A deal at one branch may not apply to every product or store. Read the shelf label carefully and check the terms before relying on a promotion. Some offers require buying more than one item, and the products included may be marked with a small sign.

Each chain also has its own branded products. If you see a snack or drink recommended online, note the chain and product name, but be ready for it to be sold out or discontinued. When you simply want something to eat, comparing the visible options is usually faster than searching for one specific item.

Payment and useful services

Major payment cards are commonly used in Korean shops, but a foreign card may not work at every terminal. Keep a backup payment method, such as another card or some cash, especially if you are away from busy areas. If a card is declined, try another card or ask whether cash is accepted rather than assuming the item cannot be purchased.

Some convenience stores can help with transit-card purchases or top-ups, but availability and the cards supported can vary. Ask before counting on a particular service, and be prepared to use cash for a top-up. The staff can also tell you whether the branch provides parcel services or other local options, though these are not essential features of every store.

How to choose a store

  • For a quick meal: Choose a branch with visible refrigerated meal shelves, and check whether it has a microwave and somewhere to sit.
  • For a specific product: Check more than one nearby chain. Stock differs from branch to branch.
  • For travel basics: Small stores usually have everyday items, but a larger branch is more likely to offer a broader choice.
  • For payment or transit services: Ask the staff whether the service is available before relying on it.

Before you go

Do not assume every convenience store is open all night, even though the name may suggest it. Check the listed hours when you need a late-night stop. Restrooms are also not a guaranteed customer facility, and seating can be scarce at busy times. If you use a store’s microwave or hot-water station, follow the posted instructions and leave the area tidy for the next person.

In short, CU, GS25, 7-Eleven and emart24 all serve much the same everyday purpose. The best choice is usually the closest branch that has the food, facilities or service you need. For a visitor, looking at the shelves, checking signs and asking a simple question is more dependable than expecting one chain to be best for everything.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Korean convenience store chains very different from one another?

The main chains often offer similar basics, such as drinks, snacks, ready-to-eat food, and everyday essentials. The selection, promotions, and available services can differ by branch, so the closest store may be just as useful as a particular brand. If you want a specific item, check a few nearby locations.

Which convenience store should I choose for a quick meal?

Compare the food on display at nearby stores, since ready-to-eat choices can vary by location and time. You may find meals, snacks, or drinks that suit your needs, but no chain is guaranteed to have the same selection at every branch. Check labels if you need to avoid particular ingredients.

Can I rely on every branch to offer the same services?

No. A chain’s branches may differ in the services they provide, as well as in their food and product selection. If you need a particular service, look for information at the location or ask staff before relying on it. For a simple snack or drink, most nearby branches are easy to compare.

Umjine in Gangneung: A Seafood Stop for Cockle-Rice Fans

Published 12 October 2026

At a glance: Umjine is a well-known seafood restaurant in Gangneung, associated with cockle dishes and Korean-style seafood meals. It is at 21 Gyeonggang-ro 2255beon-gil. Google currently lists it at 4.2 stars from 4,682 reviews; ratings and review totals can change.

Best for: Visitors curious about Gangneung’s seafood dining and cockle-based dishes.

Gangneung’s food scene draws on its position on Korea’s east coast, and Umjine is a name that regularly appears in local meal plans. The restaurant is associated with cockles—bivalve shellfish often served seasoned or mixed with rice—as well as other seafood dishes. For travelers looking to try a meal beyond familiar Korean barbecue or noodle options, it offers a chance to explore a distinctly coastal style of Korean food.

It is worth setting expectations before you go: this is a Korean restaurant, and English menu support or English-speaking service should not be assumed. A little preparation—knowing the dish names you want to ask about and keeping the restaurant’s address handy—can make ordering easier.

What to eat at Umjine

Cockles are the food most closely associated with Umjine. In Korean, cockles are kkomaek (꼬막). They may be seasoned and served with rice, or combined with rice as a mixed dish. One familiar style is to eat seasoned cockles alongside rice and then mix the two together; another is cockle fried rice. These preparations bring together the shellfish’s briny flavor with rice and a savory seasoning.

Local search results also describe Umjine as a seafood restaurant and mention options such as mulhoe (물회), a chilled, seasoned raw-seafood dish; sea-urchin bibimbap; and seafood rice bowls. Menus can change, and search listings do not confirm what is available on a particular day, so check the restaurant’s official website or ask staff before making a special trip for one specific dish.

If you are unfamiliar with cockles, think of them as a shellfish with a pleasantly chewy texture and a stronger, more oceanic taste than mild white fish. If you have a shellfish allergy, avoid cockle and seafood dishes and tell the staff clearly. For other dietary restrictions, do not assume a dish is free of ingredients such as chili, fish sauce, or seafood-based seasoning; ask before ordering.

How to make the most of your visit

Consider Umjine when you want to make a seafood meal part of your Gangneung itinerary, rather than treating it as a quick snack. The cockle-and-rice combination is a substantial style of meal, and rice-based dishes can be easier to share than individually plated items—but confirm serving sizes and ordering options with the restaurant. A high review count suggests the restaurant is frequently visited, so it may be sensible to allow flexibility in your schedule.

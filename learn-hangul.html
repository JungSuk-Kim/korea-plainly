Learn to Read Hangul in an Hour: Korean Alphabet for Travellers

By Korea Plainly · Published 15 October 2026

Learn to read the Korean alphabet before your trip: every letter with its sound, how syllable blocks work, a read-the-sign quiz and a memory game, with real words from Korean signs and menus.

Photo: Pinterpandai, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Hangul, the Korean alphabet, was designed in the 15th century to be easy to learn, and it really is: most people can sound out simple words after an hour of practice. You do not need to speak Korean for this to be useful. Being able to read 지하철 as "jihacheol" (subway), 출구 as "chulgu" (exit) or 김밥 as "gimbap" makes signs, menus and maps far less mysterious. Work through the letters below, then test yourself with the sign quiz and the memory game.

How Hangul works

Hangul has 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels, plus a few doubled and combined ones. Letters are not written in a line like English; they are grouped into square blocks, one block per syllable. Each block starts with a consonant, then a vowel, and sometimes ends with another consonant:

서 = ㅅ (s) + ㅓ (eo) = "seo"
울 = ㅇ (silent) + ㅜ (u) + ㄹ (l) = "ul"

Put them together and you get 서울, Seoul. That is the whole system. Vertical vowels (like ㅏ, ㅓ, ㅣ) sit to the right of the first consonant; horizontal ones (like ㅗ, ㅜ, ㅡ) sit underneath it.

The consonants

Tap a letter to see an example syllable.

A few tips: ㅇ is silent at the start of a syllable (it just holds the place before a vowel) but sounds like "ng" at the end. ㄱ, ㄷ, ㅂ and ㅈ sound closer to k, t, p and ch at the start of a word, and closer to g, d, b and j between vowels, which is why you see both "g" and "k" in English spellings. ㄹ is between an "r" and an "l".

The vowels

Notice the pattern: adding a second short stroke adds a "y" sound (ㅏ a, ㅑ ya; ㅗ o, ㅛ yo). ㅓ "eo" is like the "u" in "sun", and ㅡ "eu" is like the "oo" in "good" said with flat lips. ㅐ and ㅔ sound almost the same today.

Practise on the trip

  • Read station names on the subway map; they are written in Hangul and English side by side, so you can check yourself.
  • Sound out menus. Many dish names are simple: 김치찌개 is "kimchi jjigae", kimchi stew. See how to read a Korean menu and restaurant words.
  • Spot loanwords. Lots of signs are English written in Hangul: 커피 (keopi, coffee), 버스 (beoseu, bus), 호텔 (hotel). Once you can read them, you understand them.

FAQ

How long does it take to learn Hangul?

Most people can learn the letters and sound out simple words in one to three hours. Reading quickly takes more practice, but even slow reading is useful on a trip.

Do I need to read Korean to travel in Korea?

No. Signs in cities and on transport are usually in English too. But reading Hangul helps with menus, small shops and map apps, and locals appreciate the effort.

Why are there two spellings, like "gimbap" and "kimbap"?

Some Korean sounds sit between two English letters, so older and newer romanisation systems spell them differently. Korea's official system today uses "gimbap", but you will see both.

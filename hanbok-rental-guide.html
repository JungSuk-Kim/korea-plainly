Hanbok Rental in Seoul Near Gyeongbokgung Palace: Prices, Free Entry and Tips

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 17 October 2026 · Updated 1 November 2026

Hanbok rental near Gyeongbokgung Palace: where the shops are, 2026 prices, how free palace entry in hanbok works, closing days and what to wear underneath.

Photo: Republic of Korea from Seoul, Republic of Korea, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Putting on a hanbok, the traditional Korean dress, and walking through Gyeongbokgung Palace is one of the best-loved things to do in Seoul, for Korean visitors as much as foreign ones. Hanbok rental is cheap, quick and easy to book, the photos are lovely, and wearing one gets you into the royal palaces free. This guide covers where the rental shops near Gyeongbokgung are, what a rental costs, how the free entry works and how to plan the day so you are not rushing back to the shop.

Free palace entry in hanbok

Visitors wearing hanbok enter Seoul's royal palaces free: Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung, plus Jongmyo Shrine and the Joseon royal tombs. Normal adult tickets are small anyway (about ₩3,000 for Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung in 2026), so treat the free entry as a bonus rather than the reason to rent.

Two things are worth knowing. First, special programmes are not covered: the Secret Garden (Huwon) tour at Changdeokgung and the seasonal night openings need their own tickets. Second, the rules are under review. A planned rise in palace fees from 2027 has been put on hold, and the heritage authorities have said they will look again at which outfits count as hanbok for free entry. A standard rental set, traditional or modern-style, has been accepted at the gate so far, but check the official palace websites before your trip.

Closing days

Each palace closes one day a week: Gyeongbokgung usually on Tuesdays, and Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung usually on Mondays. Rental shops stay open on those days, so it is easy to dress up and then find the gate shut. Check before you book, and see our best times for Seoul attractions for the quietest hours.

Hanbok rental near Gyeongbokgung Palace

Most visitors rent within a ten-minute walk of the palace, so they spend their rental time inside the gates rather than on the pavement:

  • Gyeongbokgung Station (Line 3): exits 4 and 5 bring you out by the palace's west side and the Seochon streets, where many small shops sit above cafés and convenience stores.
  • Anguk Station (Line 3): the streets towards Bukchon and Insadong have the largest cluster. This side is handy if you also want Changdeokgung or the hanok alleys of Bukchon Hanok Village.
  • Gwanghwamun Station (Line 5): a few shops near the main gate, useful if you are coming from City Hall or Myeongdong.

Search 한복대여 (hanbok rental) on Naver Map or KakaoMap, which show shop photos, recent reviews and walking routes (our map app guide explains which to use). Google Maps listings are thinner. Booking a day or two ahead is wise for spring and autumn weekends, when the palace is at its busiest.

Outside Seoul, the same thing works in Jeonju Hanok Village and Gyeongju's old town, where the streets of traditional houses suit a hanbok just as well.

What a rental costs

Prices vary by shop, style and length of rental. As a rough guide for 2026:

  • Basic hanbok, 2-4 hours: roughly ₩15,000-30,000.
  • Premium or embroidered "royal" styles: often ₩30,000-50,000, and more for designer pieces.
  • Full day: roughly ₩30,000-60,000.
  • Extras: hairstyling, a hair ribbon (daenggi), a traditional hat for men, or a second fitting may cost a few thousand won each, though many shops include a simple hairstyle.

Shops usually ask for ID or a small deposit. Prices online are sometimes lower than walk-in prices, so compare the shop's own booking page, Naver reservations and travel booking apps.

How a rental works, step by step

  1. Arrive on time. Your rental time usually starts when you leave the shop, not when you arrive, but slots fill up and late arrivals wait.
  2. Choose your hanbok. Women pick a jeogori (short jacket) and a long chima (skirt); men pick a jacket, trousers and often a vest. Staff help match colours and dress you in a fitting room. If you are a man, ask for men's styles directly; some shops show women's racks first.
  3. Add hair and accessories. Braids, a bun with a pin, a small bag and a ribbon complete the look.
  4. Leave your things. The shop keeps your bag and coat. Take your phone, a card and a little cash in the small hanbok bag.
  5. Return on time. Late returns are charged, often per hour. Two hours is enough for Gyeongbokgung alone; choose four if you also want Bukchon or a café stop.
What to wear underneath: a thin T-shirt and leggings or shorts, since the hanbok goes over your clothes, and flat shoes you can walk in on gravel. In summer it is hot, so go early. From November to February ask for a padded jacket, fur-trimmed shawl or inner layer; most shops have winter sets.

Planning your palace visit

  • Go early. The first hour after opening is quieter and the light is softer for photos.
  • See the guard ceremony. The royal guard changing ceremony at Gwanghwamun Gate usually takes place mid-morning and early afternoon; times change with season and weather, so check on the day.
  • Good photo spots: Geunjeongjeon throne hall, the pond pavilion of Gyeonghoeru, and Hyangwonjeong pavilion at the back of the grounds, which is quieter.
  • Bukchon after the palace: parts of Bukchon limit tourist visits to daytime hours, and residents live in these houses, so keep your voice down and do not step into doorways.

Etiquette and photo tips

  • Visitors in hanbok are welcomed, not seen as disrespectful. Locals often smile and some may ask for a photo.
  • Lift the skirt slightly on stairs and gravel, and watch it near puddles and food.
  • Do not climb on buildings, sit on stone platforms marked off, or step over barriers for a shot. Palace staff will stop you.
  • Tripods and drones have their own rules at the palaces; a phone is simplest. Many shops offer a photographer for hire.

FAQ

Do you really get into Seoul's palaces free in hanbok?

Yes, at the time of writing. Visitors wearing hanbok enter the royal palaces, Jongmyo Shrine and the royal tombs free, but special tours and night openings still need a ticket. The rules on what counts as hanbok are under review, so check the palace website before you go.

How much is hanbok rental near Gyeongbokgung Palace?

A basic hanbok for two to four hours is roughly ₩15,000-30,000 in 2026, with premium styles and full-day plans costing more. Hairstyling and accessories are sometimes extra, so ask what the price includes.

How long does a hanbok rental last?

Most plans run for two or four hours, with full-day plans at many shops. Two hours suits Gyeongbokgung alone; four hours lets you add Bukchon or Changdeokgung without watching the clock.

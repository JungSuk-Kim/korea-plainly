Renting a Hanbok in Seoul: Free Palace Entry and What to Expect

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 17 October 2026

How hanbok rental works in Seoul: where to rent, what it costs, how long it takes, free entry to the royal palaces in hanbok, closing days and photo tips.

Photo: Republic of Korea from Seoul, Republic of Korea, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Walking through a royal palace in a hanbok, the traditional Korean dress, is one of the most popular things visitors do in Seoul, and Koreans do it too. It is easy to arrange, it looks wonderful in photos, and it gets you into the palaces for free. Here is how it works.

Free palace entry in hanbok

If you wear a hanbok, you can enter Seoul's royal palaces free of charge: Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung, as well as Jongmyo Shrine and the Joseon royal tombs. Normal tickets cost only a few thousand won, so the saving is small, but it is a nice bonus. Some special programmes, such as the Secret Garden tour at Changdeokgung and night openings, are not included, and the heritage authorities have been reviewing palace fees, so check the official palace websites before you go.

Staff decide what counts as hanbok at the gate. A standard rental hanbok, traditional or modern-style, is normally fine.

Closing days

Each palace closes one day a week: Gyeongbokgung usually on Tuesdays, and Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung usually on Mondays. Check before you rent, so you do not dress up for a closed gate. Our Gyeongbokgung guide and best times for Seoul attractions help you plan the visit.

Where to rent

Rental shops cluster around the palace you plan to visit:

  • Near Gyeongbokgung: dozens of shops around Anguk and Gyeongbokgung stations, and in the streets of Seochon and Bukchon.
  • Bukchon and Insadong: handy for Changdeokgung and the hanok alleys of Bukchon Hanok Village.
  • Other cities: shops also gather near Jeonju Hanok Village and Gyeongju's old town, where hanbok fits the setting just as well.

Search 한복대여 (hanbok rental) on Naver Map or KakaoMap to see shops, reviews and photos. Many take online bookings, which is worth doing at weekends and in spring and autumn.

How it works

  1. Choose your hanbok. Racks of skirts and jackets in every colour. Staff help you match them and dress you in a fitting room.
  2. Add extras. A hairstyling service, hair ornaments, a small bag or a traditional-style hat for men often cost a little more.
  3. Leave your things. Most shops keep your bag and clothes while you are out. Take your phone, cards and a small amount of cash.
  4. Return on time. Plans usually run for two to four hours or a full day, with late fees after that.

Prices change and vary with the style and the length of rental. Basic plans are usually reasonable, and premium hanbok with embroidery or a "royal" look cost more. Many shops ask for ID or a deposit.

Tip: Wear a thin top and leggings or shorts underneath; the hanbok goes over your clothes. In summer it can be hot, and in winter ask for a padded jacket or a fur-trimmed shawl, which many shops offer.

Etiquette and photo tips

  • Wearing hanbok as a visitor is welcomed, not seen as disrespectful. Locals often smile and may ask for a photo.
  • Go early. Palaces are quieter and the light is softer in the first hour after opening.
  • The skirt is long: lift it slightly on stairs and gravel, and watch it near puddles.
  • Do not climb on palace buildings or step over barriers for a shot. Palace staff will stop you.
  • Some shops offer a photographer for hire if you want proper portraits.

FAQ

Do you really get into Seoul's palaces free in hanbok?

Yes. Visitors wearing hanbok enter the royal palaces, Jongmyo Shrine and the royal tombs free. Special tours and night openings may still need a ticket.

How long does a hanbok rental last?

Most plans run for two to four hours, with longer or full-day plans available. Late returns usually cost extra.

Can foreigners wear hanbok?

Yes. Rental shops cater to visitors, and wearing hanbok to the palaces is seen as a friendly way to enjoy Korean culture.

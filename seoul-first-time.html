SEOUL · FIRST VISIT

7 Things First-Time Visitors Should Know About Seoul

By Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

The seven things worth sorting out before your first day in Seoul: a transport card, the right map app, a little cash and a realistic plan.

Photo: USAGI_POST, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Seoul is one of the easiest big cities in Asia for a first visit: it is safe, clean and very well connected. These seven tips cover what to sort out before you land and what to expect on the ground.

1. Get a transport card on day one

Seoul's subway and buses run on rechargeable tap cards. Single tickets cost more and must be bought for every ride, so get a card on your first trip.

  • T-money is the standard card. Buy it at a convenience store or a station machine, then top it up with cash.
  • Tourist cards such as WOWPASS combine a transit card with a prepaid payment card.
  • Unlimited-ride passes for short stays have been sold at Seoul stations in recent years. Check the current options when you arrive, as these schemes change.

Tap in and tap out on buses as well as the subway, because transfers are only discounted when you tap out. Our transit card guide compares the cards in detail.

2. Download Naver Map or KakaoMap

Google Maps handles public transport in Korea, but walking directions and shop listings have long been limited.

  • Naver Map has an English interface, accurate walking routes, live bus arrivals and the exit number to use at each station.
  • KakaoMap is a good second option and pairs with the Kakao T taxi app.
  • Search in English first; if a small café or restaurant does not appear, paste its Korean name or address.

Set it up before you fly. See Naver Map vs Google Maps for how they compare.

3. Cards work almost everywhere, but carry some cash

Most shops, cafés, restaurants and taxis in Seoul accept foreign Visa and Mastercard cards. Cash is still useful in a few places:

  • street food stalls and some traditional market vendors
  • topping up a T-money card, which usually needs cash
  • small, older restaurants, especially outside the main tourist areas

Withdraw won from an ATM marked "Global" or "Foreign Card", which you will find in convenience stores and bank branches. The cash, cards and payments guide explains fees and which machines to look for.

4. The subway is easy once you know the exits

Lines and stations are numbered and colour-coded, and signs and announcements are in English. What surprises visitors is the size of the stations.

  • Check the exit number before you arrive. Large stations have more than ten exits, and the wrong one can leave you a long walk from where you meant to be.
  • Allow time for transfers. Changing lines at busy hubs can mean several minutes of corridors and stairs.
  • Avoid rush hour with luggage, roughly 7:30 to 9:00 and 18:00 to 19:30 on weekdays.

Read how to use the Seoul subway for transfers, last trains and etiquette.

Quick tip: Naver Map shows the best carriage to board for a quick transfer or exit. It saves a lot of walking at big stations.

5. Convenience stores will solve half your problems

You are rarely more than a few minutes from a CU, GS25 or 7-Eleven, and many are open around the clock. They sell and top up transit cards, often have a global ATM, and stock cheap breakfasts, umbrellas, chargers and hot meals you can heat in the store.

6. Small etiquette habits that matter

A few habits go a long way:

  • Keep your voice down on the subway and buses, and take phone calls quietly.
  • Leave priority seats free, even when the carriage is busy; they are kept for older and pregnant passengers.
  • Queue in line for buses and let people off the train before you get on.
  • Take your shoes off where you see shoe racks or a raised floor, common in some restaurants and guesthouses.
  • Do not tip; it is not expected in restaurants or taxis.

7. Do not try to see everything

Crossing Seoul can take an hour, so group each day by area: say, Gyeongbokgung and Bukchon in the morning, Insadong in the afternoon, and Hongdae or the Han River in the evening. Leave gaps for cafés and side streets. Our Seoul neighbourhoods guide helps you decide where to spend your time.

Bottom line: A transport card, a Korean map app, a little cash and an unhurried plan will cover most of what a first visit to Seoul needs.

FAQ

Do I need to speak Korean to get around Seoul?

No. Subway signs, announcements and most tourist sites have English, and map apps work in English. A translation app helps with menus and small shops.

Is Google Maps enough in Seoul?

It works for subway and bus routes, but it is weaker for walking directions and small businesses. Most visitors keep Google Maps and add Naver Map or KakaoMap for walking routes and station exits.

How many days do I need for a first visit to Seoul?

Three to four full days lets you see the main palaces, a few neighbourhoods and a market without rushing. With more time, add a day trip outside the city.

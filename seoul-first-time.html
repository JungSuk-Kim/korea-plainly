SEOUL · FIRST TRIP

7 Things First-Time Visitors Should Know About Seoul.

Seoul is easy to love — and much easier when you know a few basics before you arrive. Here are seven practical things that can save you time, money and confusion.

01. Get a transportation card on day one.

For most visitors, a rechargeable transportation card is the easiest way to use Seoul's buses and subway. T-money cards can be bought at convenience stores and subway-related locations, then topped up for everyday travel.

PLAINLY PUT

Don't wait until your first busy transfer to figure out tickets. Get your transit setup early and keep the card somewhere easy to reach.

Seoul's official travel guide currently lists the adult subway base fare at ₩1,400 with a transportation card. Fares can increase with distance or on certain lines, so treat the base fare as a starting point rather than a flat price for every ride.

02. Use Korean map apps alongside Google Maps.

Google Maps is useful for browsing and saving places, but local map services are often more practical for detailed Korean addresses, walking routes and public-transit directions. Naver Map and KakaoMap are two names worth knowing before your trip.

Save your hotel and a few important destinations before leaving the airport. Having the Korean name of a place can also make it much easier to show a driver or ask someone nearby.

03. Keep a little cash just in case.

Cards are widely used in Seoul, but carrying some Korean won is still sensible. It can be useful for topping up certain transit cards, small purchases, markets or situations where a payment terminal is unavailable.

You don't need to carry a large amount. A small emergency amount is enough for most travelers.

04. Seoul's subway is incredibly useful — but don't assume it runs all night.

The subway is one of the easiest ways to move around Seoul. Seoul's official travel guide says regular subway service operates from approximately 5:30 a.m. to midnight, although the exact hours vary by station and route.

Stations use color-coded lines and signs in Korean, English and Chinese, which makes the system much less intimidating than it first looks.

GOOD HABIT

Check the last train before a late-night outing. “I'll just take the subway back” is not a plan you want to discover is impossible at 12:10 a.m.

05. Korean convenience stores are more useful than they look.

GS25, CU, 7-Eleven and other convenience stores are part of everyday Korean life. They are useful for drinks, snacks, quick meals, coffee and small travel emergencies.

Many stores also have microwaves and seating areas, and some offer services beyond simply selling food. For a traveler, they can become a surprisingly convenient backup when you're tired, hungry or between plans.

06. Learn a few small etiquette habits.

You don't need to memorize a long rulebook. A few basics go a long way: keep your voice down on public transport, queue where others are queuing, avoid blocking subway doors, and follow the signs around temples, markets and other shared spaces.

When eating with others, notice what people around you are doing. Korean dining can be communal, and some restaurants have their own ordering or seating routines.

07. Don't try to see all of Seoul in one day.

Seoul is huge. Neighborhoods can feel like completely different cities, and moving between them takes longer than a map can make it look.

Instead of crossing the city repeatedly, group your day by area. Spend a morning and afternoon around one cluster of neighborhoods, then move somewhere else for the evening. You'll spend less time underground and more time actually experiencing the city.

THE KOREA PLAINLY RULE

When in doubt, make the plan simpler. Seoul has enough to discover without turning every hour into a checklist.

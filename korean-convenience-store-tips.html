What to Know Before Using a Korean Convenience Store

Daily Korea

Korean convenience stores are handy places to buy drinks, snacks, simple meals, toiletries, and other everyday items. You will see familiar chains such as CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, and emart24 in many towns and cities. Some stores are open around the clock, but opening hours vary, so check the sign or map listing if you need a store late at night.

Stores can differ in size and services. A larger branch may have tables, a microwave, a ramen station, or an ATM, while a smaller one may offer only basic shopping. Treat each extra service as a possibility, not a guarantee.

Paying for your purchases

Most stores have a staffed checkout, and some also have self-checkout machines. You can usually pay with cash or a bank card. Foreign cards work at many shops, but acceptance can depend on the card, payment network, and terminal. Keep another payment method available in case your card is declined. If you use a self-checkout and cannot follow the Korean prompts, ask a staff member for help.

At a staffed checkout, place your items on the counter and wait for the cashier to scan them. You may be asked whether you want a receipt. If you need a shopping bag, ask for one; a bag may cost extra. Carrying a reusable bag is a useful option.

Some convenience stores sell and reload transit cards, including T-money, but availability varies by location. You can ask, “T-money 충전해 주세요” (T-money chungjeonhae juseyo), meaning “Please top up my T-money.” Reloading is commonly done with cash at the counter or a machine, so bring cash if you plan to add money. Not every store has an ATM, and an ATM may not accept your foreign card. Look for a machine marked for international cards, and check any fees shown on its screen before continuing.

Buying and heating food

Convenience stores offer ready-to-eat meals such as rice dishes, kimbap, sandwiches, noodles, and packaged snacks. The selection changes by store and time of day. Check the package for a date and storage instructions, especially for chilled food. Ingredient and allergy information is often printed in Korean, so use a translation app if you need to check a particular ingredient. Do not assume a product is vegetarian, halal, or free of an allergen based only on its appearance.

Many stores have a microwave customers can use, but the setup differs. Some food should be heated only after removing a lid or film; follow the package directions and do not microwave packaging unless it says it is safe. If you are unsure how to use the microwave, ask the cashier. A useful phrase is “전자레인지 어디 있어요?” (jeonjareinji eodi isseoyo?), meaning “Where is the microwave?”

Instant cup noodles are common. A store with a ramen station may have hot water and a place to eat, but not every branch has these facilities. Follow the instructions on the cup, and take care when carrying hot water. If there are tables, they may be shared with other customers. Clear your place when you finish and leave the area tidy.

Some stores have a small dining area, but it is not a full-service café. Seating, utensils, hot water, and heating equipment are not guaranteed. Check what is available before choosing a meal that depends on a particular facility.

Useful services and small details

Depending on the branch, you may find services such as parcel drop-off, bill payment, or a cash machine. These can involve Korean-language instructions or eligibility requirements, and some may not be convenient for a short-term visitor. Check the signs or ask the staff before relying on a service. A translation app can help you show a short question on your phone.

Useful phrases include “이거 데워 주세요” (igeo dewo juseyo), meaning “Please heat this,” and “봉투 주세요” (bongtu juseyo), meaning “Please give me a bag.” You can also point to an item if you do not know its name. Staff may ask a question in Korean while checking out; a polite smile and a simple “네” (ne, “yes”) or “아니요” (aniyo, “no”) can help, but it is fine to ask them to repeat or use a translation app.

Be considerate and plan ahead

Pay for food before eating or opening it. If you use a microwave or hot-water station, follow the posted directions and leave the area clean for the next customer. Dispose of rubbish using the bins provided, if there are any. Waste and recycling arrangements vary, so do not assume every store has a bin for every type of packaging.

Convenience stores are useful for quick purchases, but they may not carry the full range of products you would find in a supermarket. If you need a specific medicine, dietary product, or personal item, check the package carefully or look for a more specialized shop. For a smooth visit, keep a payment backup, bring a reusable bag, and use a translation app when a label or machine is unclear.

What to Know Before Using a Korean Convenience Store

A simple guide to shopping, paying, heating food, using common services, and avoiding small surprises at Korean convenience stores.

Korean convenience stores are much more than places to grab a drink. Branches such as CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, and emart24 are useful for quick meals, snacks, drinks, toiletries, phone-related services, and everyday travel needs.

The easiest way to think about them is as small neighborhood shops with different levels of service. A large branch may have seats, a microwave, hot water, or other facilities, while a smaller branch may offer only the basics.

How payment works

At a normal checkout, put your items on the counter and let the cashier scan them. Cash and cards are commonly accepted, although a foreign card can occasionally be declined depending on the card network or terminal.

If your card does not work, try another payment method rather than assuming the store does not accept cards. If you need a shopping bag, ask for one; bags can cost extra.

A useful phrase is 봉투 주세요 (bongtu juseyo), meaning “A bag, please.”

Buying and heating ready-made food

Convenience stores are popular for quick meals such as triangle kimbap, lunch boxes, sandwiches, snacks, and instant noodles. Selection varies by branch and time of day.

Many ready-made meals can be heated at the checkout. If you want the staff to heat something, you can say 이거 데워 주세요 (igeo dewo juseyo), meaning “Please heat this.”

Some stores also have a customer microwave. Follow the instructions on the package, especially when a plastic film or lid needs to be removed before heating.

Eating inside the store

Some branches have a small seating area, ramen station, hot-water dispenser, or sink. These facilities are not guaranteed, so check what the particular branch provides.

If you use a table, microwave, or hot-water station, clean up after yourself and leave the space ready for the next customer. A convenience-store seating area is generally a quick-use space rather than a full café.

T-money and other services

Some convenience stores sell or top up transportation cards such as T-money. Availability and the exact service can vary by branch.

If you need a T-money top-up, you can say T-money 충전해 주세요 (T-money chungjeonhae juseyo), meaning “Please top up my T-money.” Carrying some Korean won is useful when you are relying on a service that may not accept your preferred payment method.

Depending on the branch, you may also see ATMs, parcel services, printing, or other convenience services. Do not assume every branch offers the same options.

Small things visitors often wonder about

  • Receipts: You may be asked whether you want one. Take it if you need proof of purchase.
  • Toilets: Availability varies. A store with a toilet may have restrictions on customer use.
  • Trash: Do not assume every branch has a public bin for all rubbish. Follow the store's setup.
  • Allergies: Check Korean labels carefully or use a translation app. Do not judge ingredients by appearance alone.
  • Late-night shopping: Many stores stay open late or around the clock, but opening hours vary by branch.

Three phrases worth saving

  • 봉투 주세요 — A bag, please.
  • 이거 데워 주세요 — Please heat this.
  • 이거 얼마예요? — How much is this?
Bottom line: Pick what you need, pay at the counter, check whether food needs heating, and use the facilities only when the branch provides them. A translation app and a little Korean cash can make the experience even easier.

FAQ

Can I use a foreign card at a Korean convenience store?

Often yes, but acceptance can vary by card and terminal. Keeping another payment method available is a good idea.

Can I heat convenience-store food myself?

Some branches have customer microwaves, while others heat food at the counter. Follow the package instructions and ask staff if you are unsure.

Can I top up T-money at a convenience store?

Many branches provide T-money services, but availability can vary. Ask the cashier before relying on a particular branch.

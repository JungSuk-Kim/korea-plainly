Doing Laundry in Korea: Coin Laundries and Hotel Options

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 11 November 2026

How to find and use a Korean self-service laundry, what to expect from hotel laundry, and how to avoid common washing and payment problems.

Running out of clean clothes in Korea is usually easy to fix. Self-service laundries are common in residential neighborhoods, and many hotels offer either guest laundry machines or a paid laundry service. The main things to plan for are payment, drying time and the difference between washing clothes yourself and handing them to hotel staff.

Find a self-service laundry

Search Naver Map for 셀프빨래방 (self-service laundry) or 코인세탁 (coin laundry). These shops are often called 빨래방 on signs. Search near your accommodation rather than assuming one will be beside a major tourist sight. Hongdae and Yeonnam-dong, around Hongik University Station on Line 2 and the AREX, have many small residential streets where you can look. In Seoul, the area around Jongno 3-ga Station, served by Lines 1, 3 and 5, is another useful search area if you are staying near Insadong or Ikseon-dong.

Check the map listing for recent reviews and photos of the machines. Google Maps can be less useful for local business listings, so Naver Map is a good first choice. Look for a photo showing payment instructions before you set off. A shop described as 코인세탁 may not accept coins only; the name is widely used even when machines also take cards or use a shop payment terminal.

How to use the machines

Take your clothes to the washer marked 세탁기 (washing machine), and use 건조기 for the dryer. Instructions may be in Korean, but machine screens often show a cycle number, price and remaining time. The basic process is to put clothes in, choose a cycle, pay, and press start. Some washers add detergent automatically; others have a vending machine for 세제 (detergent) and 섬유유연제 (fabric softener). Check before buying a separate packet.

Payment differs from shop to shop. Some machines take bills or coins, while others use a card reader or a central kiosk. Carry a ₩10,000 note and a bank card as backups, and be ready to follow the instructions on the payment screen. If it asks you to select a machine number, note the number printed on the washer. A translation app can help with a screen, but machine diagrams are often the quickest guide.

Wash cold or use a gentle cycle for clothes you are unsure about. Korean dryers can shrink delicate items, so check the care label and use a lower heat setting if available. Put bras, socks and other small items in a 세탁망 (laundry net), which you can buy at a Daiso store. Do not mix clothes that need very different temperatures or drying times.

Budget enough time and money

As a rough 2026 guide, a wash may cost around ₩4,000–₩7,000 and a dryer cycle around ₩4,000–₩6,000. Prices vary by machine size and neighborhood, and a large dryer may need another payment to finish a full load. Allow roughly an hour or more for washing and drying, rather than planning laundry between two tightly scheduled activities. Use the machine’s displayed timer as your guide.

Set a phone alarm so you can return when the cycle ends. If the shop is busy, remove your finished clothes promptly so the next customer can use the machine. Avoid leaving valuables in a laundry basket while you wait. Many self-service laundries in residential areas stay open late or around the clock and have folding tables, but bring a small bag to carry clean clothes back to your hotel.

Ask your hotel before you book

Hotels handle laundry in two different ways. A guest laundry room lets you operate a washer and dryer yourself; a hotel laundry service collects items and charges by the piece. Ask which is available, whether detergent is supplied, and what payment method the machines take. A hotel’s phrase 세탁 서비스 usually means staff service, not necessarily a self-service room.

Business hotels and guesthouses are worth checking if laundry access matters. For example, Toyoko Inn branches in Seoul and Busan are popular with budget-conscious travelers, but facilities can differ by property, so confirm the details for the exact branch. In Busan, guests staying around Seomyeon Station on Lines 1 and 2 can also search the surrounding blocks for 빨래방 if their hotel has no machines.

Hotel laundry service is convenient when you need clean work clothes or do not want to spend time waiting, but it can cost much more than a self-service wash. At larger hotels a single shirt can cost more than a whole self-service load, and prices vary widely by garment and property. Check the laundry form in your room for the current item prices and collection times. Dry cleaning is listed as 드라이클리닝 and is separate from ordinary washing.

Small details that make laundry easier

  • Keep a few detergent sheets or a small laundry packet if you are moving between cities, but first check whether your hotel or laundry shop supplies detergent.
  • Separate light and dark clothes before you leave your room; most small laundries do not offer a staffed sorting service.
  • Do not put a waterproof jacket, wool sweater or delicate item in a hot dryer unless its care label allows it.
  • If a machine stops or payment fails, look for a phone number or help notice near the kiosk. Use a photo of the screen when asking your hotel desk or a nearby shop worker for help.
Bottom line: Search Naver Map for 셀프빨래방 near your accommodation, bring cash and a card, and allow time for both washing and drying. Ask your hotel whether it has guest machines or paid laundry service, since the two options are different.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I bring detergent and coins to a coin laundry?

It is sensible to bring detergent and some cash, but check the machines and signs when you arrive. Payment methods and detergent availability can differ from one laundry to another. If you are unsure which product or amount to use, follow the machine instructions or ask for help.

Can my hotel wash my clothes for me?

Many hotels offer some form of laundry service, but the options depend on the property. Ask the front desk whether it provides washing, dry cleaning, or access to a self-service laundry room. Check the expected return time and any charges before handing over your clothes.

What if I cannot understand the washing machine instructions?

Use a translation app to read the machine labels, and check the clothing care label before choosing a cycle. If the controls are still unclear, ask another customer or contact the laundry attendant if one is available. Avoid guessing at settings that could damage delicate clothing.

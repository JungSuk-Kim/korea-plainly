3 Days in Jeju: An Island Itinerary for First-Timers

By Korea Plainly · Published 13 October 2026 · Updated 17 October 2026

A three-day Jeju plan: the west coast and Hyeopjae Beach, Sunrise Peak and Udo on the east, then Hallasan or Seogwipo's waterfalls, with car and bus tips.

Photo: Asaf Zebulon, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Jeju is a volcanic island off Korea's south coast, about an hour's flight from Seoul, with black-rock beaches, crater hills, waterfalls and Korea's highest mountain, Hallasan. It is bigger than it looks, so the best way to see it in three days is one side of the island per day: the west coast, the east coast and the south. Every stop links to a fuller guide where we have one.

Getting around: most visitors rent a car, which makes this plan easy. You need an International Driving Permit (or the type of licence Korea accepts from your country) and your passport. Without a car, Jeju's buses reach every stop below but take much longer, so drop one stop per day, or book a taxi or day tour for the east coast.

Where to stay

For a short trip, stay in Jeju City, close to the airport, where most restaurants and buses start. It works as a base for all three days. Maison GLAD Jeju is a convenient city hotel. If you prefer the coast, Aewol on the west or Seogwipo on the south are good alternatives.

Day 1: the west coast

Morning: Aewol coast

Drive west along the coast road from Jeju City to Aewol, where the shoreline is lined with lava rock and cafes with big sea views. Haejigae Cafe is a relaxed stop with views of the water.

Midday: Hyeopjae Beach

Continue to Hyeopjae Beach, famous for its clear, turquoise water and views of Biyangdo, a small island just offshore. Walk the sand and the coastal path, and eat lunch in Hallim nearby. In summer it is a swimming beach; the rest of the year it is perfect for a walk.

Afternoon: Arte Museum or a crater walk

Head inland to Arte Museum Jeju, a large immersive digital art space, which is also a good plan for a rainy day. On a clear day, swap it for a short climb up one of the oreum, the small volcanic cones scattered across the island, for a view over the fields.

Evening: sunset on the west coast

The west coast faces the sunset. Stay for it before driving back to Jeju City.

Day 2: the east coast

Early morning: Seongsan Ilchulbong

Seongsan Ilchulbong, "Sunrise Peak", is a huge volcanic crater rising from the sea on the east tip of the island and part of Jeju's UNESCO World Natural Heritage. The climb to the crater rim takes about 20 to 30 minutes on steps. Come for sunrise or early in the morning to avoid the tour groups.

Late morning: Udo Island

Ferries run from Seongsan Port, near Sunrise Peak, to Udo, a small island with white sand beaches, peanut ice cream and quiet coastal roads. Rent a bicycle, e-bike or scooter on the island to get around. Ferries can be cancelled in bad weather or strong wind; check on the day.

Afternoon: Seopjikoji and the coast road

Walk the clifftop path at Seopjikoji, just south of Seongsan, with views back to Sunrise Peak. On the way back to Jeju City, families and adventure lovers can stop at Jeju Leports Land in Jocheon for outdoor activities.

Day 3: Hallasan or the south coast

Option A: hike Hallasan

Hallasan, at 1,947 metres, is South Korea's highest mountain. The two trails to the summit, Seongpanak and Gwaneumsa, need an online reservation in advance for the final section to the summit and take most of a day, with strict cut-off times for starting the final climb. If you want a shorter hike, the Eoseungsaengak trail takes about an hour up and down and gives a taste of the mountain. Start early, bring water and warm layers, and check the weather: the mountain is much colder and windier than the coast.

Option B: Seogwipo and the waterfalls

Cross the island to Seogwipo on the south coast. See Jeongbang Falls, which drops straight into the sea, and Cheonjiyeon Falls, which is lit in the evening. Walk a section of the Jeju Olle Trail along the coast and finish at Seogwipo Maeil Olle Market for snacks and Jeju tangerines.

Before you fly home

If your dates fall on the 2nd or 7th of the month (2, 7, 12, 17, 22, 27), visit the Jeju Traditional Five-Day Market in Jeju City. For a last seafood meal near the airport, see Gozip Dol Wooluck.

What to eat on Jeju

  • Heuk-dwaeji: Jeju's famous black pork, grilled at the table.
  • Seafood: raw fish, abalone porridge and hairtail (galchi) dishes.
  • Tangerines: in season from late autumn to winter, and in juices, chocolates and desserts all year. Visiting then? See our Jeju in winter guide.

Tips for Jeju

  • The weather changes fast, and wind is common. Bring a windproof layer whatever the season.
  • Book flights from Seoul early for weekends and holidays; it is one of the busiest air routes in the world.
  • Many places outside Jeju City close early in the evening. Plan dinner near your hotel.
  • Use Naver Map or KakaoMap for driving and bus routes. See Naver Map vs Google Maps.

FAQ

Is three days enough for Jeju?

Three days is enough for the highlights of the west coast, the east coast and either Hallasan or the south. With more time, slow down and add more beaches, oreum and coastal walks. To fit Jeju into a longer trip, see our 10 days in Korea plan.

Do I need a car on Jeju?

It makes things much easier. Buses reach the main sights but take longer, so plan fewer stops per day if you rely on them.

Do I need to book a Hallasan hike?

Yes, for the summit sections of the two summit trails (Seongpanak and Gwaneumsa). Shorter trails don't need a reservation. Check the official Hallasan website, as the rules change.

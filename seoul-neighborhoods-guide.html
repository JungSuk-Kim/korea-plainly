SEOUL · FIRST TRIP

Where to Stay in Seoul: Neighborhoods Compared for First-Time Visitors

By Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 17 October 2026

Myeongdong, Jongno, Hongdae, Gangnam, Itaewon, Seongsu and Dongdaemun compared: what each area is like, who it suits, and how to choose a hotel base.

Korea Plainly · 8 min read

Photo: Bgag, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Seoul is huge, but most first-time visitors spend nearly all their time in a handful of neighbourhoods. Each one has its own character, and choosing the right base can save you an hour or more of travel every day. Here is how the main areas compare, with a short checklist for picking a hotel.

Myeongdong

The classic first-timer base. Myeongdong is a grid of shopping streets packed with cosmetics shops, fashion chains and, from late afternoon, street food stalls. It is busy and very commercial, but it is central: Namsan Seoul Tower, Namdaemun Market and City Hall are all within walking distance, and the palaces are a short subway or taxi ride away.

  • Getting there: Myeong-dong station (Line 4) and Euljiro 1-ga station (Line 2). Airport limousine buses also stop in the area.
  • Watch out for: crowds in the evening and small hotel rooms for the price.

Best for: a first visit, short stays, shopping.

Jongno, Insadong and Bukchon

This is old Seoul. Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung palaces, the hanok houses of Bukchon, the tea houses and craft shops of Insadong, and the food stalls of Gwangjang Market are all close together, so you can spend whole days on foot.

Bukchon is a lived-in residential area, not a theme park. Parts of it now limit tourist visits to daytime hours, so check the signs at the entrance to each lane, keep your voice down and do not open gates or doors.

Best for: history, traditional culture, walking.

Hongdae and Yeonnam-dong

Hongdae grew up around Hongik University and is the centre of Seoul's youth culture: street buskers, indie shops, karaoke rooms, clubs and cafes open until late. Yeonnam-dong, next door, is calmer, with small restaurants along Gyeongui Line Forest Park, a long strip of green built over an old railway line. K-pop fans will find album shops and fan cafés here; see our K-pop guide to Seoul.

  • Getting there: Hongik University station (Line 2, the Gyeongui-Jungang Line and the AREX airport railway).
  • Watch out for: noise on Friday and Saturday nights if your room faces the main streets.

Best for: nightlife, cafes, budget stays, easy airport access.

Gangnam and Jamsil

Modern Seoul south of the Han River: office towers, wide roads, COEX Mall and its Starfield Library, upmarket shopping in Apgujeong and Cheongdam, and Lotte World Tower and Lotte World in Jamsil. Hotels are often newer and larger, but the big sights of old Seoul are 30 to 45 minutes away by subway, and the area is less pleasant to explore on foot.

Best for: business trips, malls, modern city views, families heading to Lotte World.

Itaewon and Hannam-dong

Seoul's most international neighbourhood, with restaurants from around the world, halal food near the Seoul Central Mosque, bars and rooftop terraces. Hannam-dong next door is quieter and more polished, known for design shops, galleries and the Leeum Museum.

Best for: international and halal food, nightlife, visitors who want an easy start.

Seongsu-dong

Former shoe factories and warehouses turned into cafes, pop-up stores and small Korean brands. It is very popular with young locals, especially at weekends, and sits next to Seoul Forest park. There are fewer hotels than in other areas, so most visitors come for an afternoon rather than stay.

Best for: cafes, design shops, a local weekend feel.

Dongdaemun

Fashion malls and wholesale markets that stay open late into the night, plus the curved, silver Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP). Several subway lines meet here and airport buses stop nearby, and hotels tend to be a little cheaper than in Myeongdong, which makes it a practical, central base.

Best for: shopping, a central and affordable base.

Choosing a base

For a first trip of three to five days, Myeongdong, Jongno or Dongdaemun keep you close to the palaces and old town. Pick Hongdae if nightlife and cafes matter more, and Gangnam if you are in Seoul for work. Then check these points before you book:

  • Distance to the station: look up the walk on Naver Map or KakaoMap rather than trusting "near the station". Five minutes is ideal, especially with luggage.
  • The right line: Line 2 loops around the centre, and a hotel near an AREX station or an airport bus stop makes arrival day much easier. Our Incheon Airport to Seoul guide compares the options.
  • Hills: parts of Seoul, including the streets around Namsan and Bukchon, are steep. Check the street view before booking.
  • One base is enough: the subway reaches every area above, so stay in one hotel rather than moving every two nights.
Tip: Load a transit card on your first day. It works on the subway, buses and many taxis, and saves buying single tickets for every ride.

FAQ

Which area is best for a first trip to Seoul?

Myeongdong is the easiest choice for most first-time visitors: it is central, well connected and has hotels at every level. If you prefer quieter streets and history, Jongno near Insadong is a good alternative.

Is it better to stay north or south of the Han River?

For sightseeing, north of the river is usually better, because the palaces, markets and old town are there. South of the river (Gangnam and Jamsil) suits business trips and shopping malls.

How many days do I need to see these neighbourhoods?

Three days is enough to get a feel for the old town, one shopping area and one nightlife area. Our 3-day Seoul itinerary shows how to fit them together.

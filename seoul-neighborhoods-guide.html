SEOUL · FIRST TRIP

Seoul Neighborhoods: A Simple Guide for First-Time Visitors

By Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026

What each popular Seoul neighbourhood is like, what to do there, and which one suits you as a base.

Korea Plainly · 8 min read

Photo: Bgag, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Seoul is big, but most first-time visitors spend their time in a handful of neighbourhoods. Each has a different character, and picking the right base saves a lot of travel time.

On this page

  1. Myeongdong
  2. Jongno, Insadong and Bukchon
  3. Hongdae and Yeonnam-dong
  4. Gangnam and Jamsil
  5. Itaewon and Hannam-dong
  6. Seongsu-dong
  7. Dongdaemun
  8. Choosing a base

Myeongdong

The classic first-timer base: shopping streets, cosmetics shops, street food stalls in the evening and easy subway access. It is busy and commercial, and close to Namsan Seoul Tower and Namdaemun Market.

Best for: a first visit, short stays, shopping.

Jongno, Insadong and Bukchon

Old Seoul. Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung palaces, the hanok houses of Bukchon, tea houses and craft shops in Insadong, and the market food of Gwangjang Market are all close together. Bukchon is a residential area, so visit quietly and within the posted visiting hours.

Best for: history, traditional culture, walking.

Hongdae and Yeonnam-dong

A university area with a young crowd, busking, indie shops, cafes and nightlife. Yeonnam-dong next door is calmer, with a long park built on an old railway line. Direct trains run from Incheon Airport.

Best for: nightlife, cafes, budget stays.

Gangnam and Jamsil

Modern Seoul south of the Han River: office towers, COEX Mall and its library, upmarket shopping, and Lotte World Tower in Jamsil. Distances are larger and streets less walkable than north of the river.

Best for: business trips, malls, modern city views.

Itaewon and Hannam-dong

Seoul's most international area, with food from around the world, bars and rooftop restaurants. Hannam-dong next door is known for design shops and galleries.

Best for: international food, nightlife.

Seongsu-dong

Former factories and warehouses turned into cafes, pop-up stores and small brands. Popular with locals at weekends and close to Seoul Forest park.

Best for: cafes, design, a local weekend feel.

Dongdaemun

Late-night fashion malls and wholesale markets, plus the futuristic Dongdaemun Design Plaza. Good subway and airport bus links make it a practical base.

Best for: shopping, a central and affordable base.

Choosing a base

  • Stay within a few minutes' walk of a subway station, ideally on Line 2 (the loop) or a line with a direct airport connection.
  • Myeongdong, Jongno and Dongdaemun put you close to the palaces and old town; Hongdae is better for nightlife; Gangnam suits business and shopping.
  • Check how far the hotel is from the station on Naver Map or KakaoMap rather than trusting "near the station" descriptions.
Tip: Seoul's neighbourhoods are well connected by subway. Choose one base and take day trips across the city rather than changing hotels.

Chimaek: Korean Fried Chicken and Beer for Beginners

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 8 November 2026

A practical guide to ordering chimaek in Korea, from chicken styles and useful Korean phrases to riverside picnics in Seoul and Busan.

In Korea, fried chicken and beer are a familiar pairing called chimaek (치맥), combining the words for chicken, chikin (치킨), and beer, maekju (맥주). You can enjoy it at a restaurant, order it to your accommodation, or take it to a riverside picnic. For a first visit, the main choices are what kind of chicken to get, whether you want bones, and how spicy you like it.

Choose a chicken style

Huraideu (후라이드) is plain fried chicken, usually crisp and savory. Yangnyeom (양념) is coated in a sweet, sticky, often mildly spicy sauce. Ganjang (간장) means soy-sauce chicken and is usually savory-sweet. If you cannot decide, ask for banban (반반), half one flavor and half another. It is a common way to sample two styles.

Chicken with bones is the classic choice. For less mess, look for sun-sal (순살), boneless chicken. Small, bite-size fried pieces may be listed as dakgangjeong (닭강정); they are handy for sharing, though the sauce can be quite sweet. Spice levels vary by restaurant, so ask maepge haeyo? (맵게 해요?), meaning “Is it spicy?” before ordering.

How much to order

A whole order is often enough for two people sharing other dishes, but portions differ. In many Seoul shops, a chicken order is roughly around ₩20,000–₩30,000 (2026); boneless options and special flavors may cost more. Check the menu for set or combo meals, which may include a drink or side dish. Some restaurants require each person to order a drink, so look at the menu before settling in.

Chicken usually comes with pickled radish, called chikin-mu (치킨무). Its crisp, sour taste cuts through the fried coating. You may also see cabbage salad or a small bowl of seasoned peanuts. These sides are commonly included, but do not assume every refill is free.

Where to try it in Seoul

Hongdae, around Hongik University Station on subway Line 2, has plenty of casual restaurants where a group can share chicken and beer after browsing shops and street performances. It is a convenient area for first-timers because the station connects Line 2, the Airport Railroad and the Gyeongui-Jungang Line. Look for menus with pictures if you do not read Korean; point to the flavor and say banban juseyo (반반 주세요), “Half and half, please.”

For a picnic, Yeouido Hangang Park is a classic choice. It is close to Yeouinaru Station on Line 5, and the riverside paths are popular for sitting outdoors. Buy takeaway chicken nearby or arrange delivery to a clearly described meeting point; delivery drivers cannot always find a blanket or an exact spot in a large park. Bring tissues and a rubbish bag, and use the park’s bins where available.

Try the waterfront in Busan

In Busan, Gwangalli Beach is a memorable place to eat takeaway chicken with a view of the lit Gwangan Bridge. Gwangan Station on subway Line 2 is a useful stop for reaching the beach area. Pick up your food from a nearby shop and find a place where eating is allowed; keep containers tidy and avoid blocking the walkway. The sea breeze can cool food quickly, so eat soon after you collect it.

Ordering in a restaurant

Menus may show a whole chicken as han mari (한 마리), literally “one bird.” To ask for two flavors, say banban isseoyo? (반반 있어요?), “Do you have half-and-half?” To order beer, say maekju han byeong juseyo (맥주 한 병 주세요), “One bottle of beer, please.” You can show the phrase on your phone if pronunciation feels difficult.

Many chicken shops serve food in large portions, so share rather than ordering one chicken per person. If you want a quieter meal, go before the late-evening rush. Use Naver Map or KakaoMap to find a nearby shop and see the menu or recent visitor photos; these local map apps are often more useful than international map services for walking directions in Korea.

Delivery and basic etiquette

Delivery is part of chimaek culture, but it can be tricky if you do not have a Korean phone number or a local address format. In Seoul, Shuttle is an English-language delivery app that visitors may find easier to use; service and restaurant choices depend on location. If ordering to a hotel, ask the front desk whether the driver should leave the food at reception or call you. Do not rely on a delivery driver to enter a hotel or meet you inside a busy station.

At the table, chicken is commonly shared from the center. Use the serving tongs if provided, and take pieces onto your own plate. Beer is often served cold in bottles or glasses; a simple toast is geonbae (건배), “Cheers.” If you do not drink alcohol, it is fine to order soda or another non-alcoholic drink and still enjoy the chicken.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does “chimaek” mean?

“Chimaek” combines the Korean words for chicken and beer. It usually refers to enjoying fried chicken with beer, whether at a restaurant or as a casual meal with friends. You can still order the chicken without beer; the name describes a popular pairing, not a requirement.

How do I order fried chicken if I’m not sure what the menu means?

Look for pictures or ask the staff to explain the chicken styles and sauces. Menus may offer different cuts, seasonings, or levels of spice, and the choices can vary by restaurant. If you have a preference, say whether you want plain or seasoned chicken and whether you want it spicy.

Do I need to drink beer with chimaek?

No. Beer is the traditional pairing, but you can choose another drink or simply order chicken. If you are dining with a group, people can order different drinks to suit their preferences. You can also ask what nonalcoholic options the restaurant has.

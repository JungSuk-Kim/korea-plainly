Seoraksan Mountain Hikes: Easy to Full-Day Trails from Sokcho

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 30 October 2026 · Updated 28 November 2026

Seoraksan Mountain from Sokcho: bus 7 to Sogongwon, Biseondae, Biryong Falls, the Ulsanbawi stairs, Daecheongbong, the cable car and closure dates.

Photo: Olga Lipunova, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Seoraksan (설악산) is the mountain most Koreans picture when they think of granite peaks and autumn colour, and it is easy to reach from Sokcho on the east coast. Almost every first visit starts at one place, Sogongwon, where you choose between a flat valley walk, a waterfall, a steel staircase up Ulsanbawi or, with a full day, the summit.

Getting to the trails

From Seoul, express buses to Sokcho leave from Seoul Express Bus Terminal (Gangnam) and Dong Seoul Terminal and take about two and a half hours on a clear road. Sokcho has two terminals: the express terminal (속초고속버스터미널) near the beach and the intercity terminal (속초시외버스터미널) in the centre. City buses 7 and 7-1 run from both to the end of the line at Seorak-dong; sit tight until the last stop, roughly 30 to 40 minutes.

The bus drops you at the 소공원 (Sogongwon) area. There is no entry fee (the temple charge was dropped in 2023), and within ten minutes you reach Sinheungsa Temple and its large seated bronze Buddha. Every trail below except Daecheongbong from Osaek starts here, and the path forks just past the temple: left for Ulsanbawi, straight on for Biseondae, and back towards the car park for Biryong Falls.

On signs, 입산통제 means the trail is closed. Buy water and gimbap in Sokcho or at the shops by the bus stop; there is nowhere to buy food on the trails themselves.

The cable car to Gwongeumseong

If you have little time or do not want to hike, the Seoraksan cable car (설악케이블카) runs from Sogongwon to the rocky top of Gwongeumseong. From the upper station a ten-minute scramble over bare granite leads to a view of the peaks and the sea. A return ticket was about ₩16,000 for adults in late 2026, sold on the day at the station. Autumn weekend queues can pass an hour, and it stops in strong wind, so go early.

Easy: Biseondae Valley

About 3 km each way, 2 to 3 hours return. The gentlest walk follows a stream through a wide valley to Biseondae (비선대), where the water runs over pale slabs beneath a wall of cliffs. Trainers are fine. Biseondae is the natural turning point: beyond it the trail climbs steeply to Geumganggul Cave and the high ridges, which is a different kind of hike. This is the best pick for families, an afternoon arrival or a cold winter day.

Easy to moderate: Biryong Falls

About 2.5 km each way, 2 to 3 hours return. The route to Biryong Falls (비룡폭포, "Flying Dragon Falls") follows a forested gorge past Yukdam Falls, with suspension bridges and a fair number of stairs near the end. The falls drop into a deep green pool; they are best after summer rain. Wear shoes with grip, because the stone steps stay wet, and do not climb onto the rocks by the pool.

Moderate to strenuous: Ulsanbawi Rock

About 3.8 km each way, 4 to 5 hours return. Ulsanbawi (울산바위), the granite ridge you see from all over Sokcho, is the classic Seoraksan half-day. The first hour is easy forest path past Heundeulbawi, the "rocking boulder" that visitors try to push, and the small Gyejoam hermitage built into a cave. Then come the stairs: hundreds of steel steps bolted to the rock, steep enough that you will use the handrail. The top is exposed and windy. Start by late morning, and remember the descent is harder on the knees.

Full day: Daecheongbong from Osaek

About 5 km each way, 8 to 10 hours return. Daecheongbong (대청봉, 1,708 m) is the highest point of Seoraksan and the third-highest peak in South Korea. The shortest approach starts at Osaek (오색) on the south side, in Yangyang County, not at Sogongwon. It is steep almost all the way, mostly stone steps through forest, and Korean hikers often start before dawn. Buses to Osaek run from Yangyang and Sokcho, but not often, so check the times on Naver Map or KakaoMap the evening before and know how you will get back.

Seasons and trail closures

Mid to late October is peak season, when the colour reaches Sogongwon and the road into Seorak-dong jams at weekends. July and August rain can close trails for a day or two.

  • Wildfire closures: the high ridge and summit trails close twice a year, in recent years from mid-November to mid-December (15 November to 15 December in 2026) and from early March to mid-May. The routes to Daecheongbong are always among them. The valley walks to Biseondae and Biryong Falls, and Ulsanbawi, have usually stayed open.
  • Ice: from late November the stairs to Ulsanbawi ice over. Slip-on crampons (아이젠, aijen) are sold cheaply at the outdoor shops in Seorak-dong, and most Korean hikers carry them all winter.
  • Short days: in December it is dark by about 5:30 pm.

Trail status is posted on the Korea National Park Service website (english.knps.or.kr); check it before you go, and see our Korea winter travel guide for clothing.

After the hike: food in Seorak-dong and Sokcho

The restaurants by the bus stop serve hikers' food: sanchae bibimbap (rice with mountain greens), pajeon (savoury pancake) and dotorimuk (acorn jelly), usually with a bowl of dongdongju, a milky rice wine. Back in town, Sokcho Jungang Market (속초관광수산시장) is the place for dakgangjeong (sweet crispy fried chicken, sold in boxes) and ojingeo sundae (stuffed squid). Our Sokcho guide covers where to stay, and 7 days in Korea shows how to fit the east coast into a first trip.

Bottom line: Take bus 7 or 7-1 to Sogongwon, walk to Biseondae or Biryong Falls for an easy first visit, climb Ulsanbawi if you like a challenge, and save Daecheongbong for a full, early day when the trail is open.

FAQ

Can I visit Seoraksan as a day trip from Seoul?

Yes, but it is a long day: about five hours on buses there and back, plus the local bus to Sogongwon. That leaves time for the cable car and Biseondae, not much more. For Ulsanbawi, spend a night in Sokcho.

Which Seoraksan trail has the best views?

For the effort, Ulsanbawi: the top looks out over the Seorak peaks on one side and Sokcho and the sea on the other. If you cannot manage the stairs, the cable car to Gwongeumseong gives a similar view in a few minutes.

Are Seoraksan trails closed in November?

Partly. The high ridge and summit trails, including every route to Daecheongbong, close from mid-November to mid-December to prevent wildfires (15 November to 15 December in 2026). Biseondae, Biryong Falls and Ulsanbawi have usually stayed open.

Wi-Fi, SIM or eSIM: Staying Connected in Korea

Published 7 October 2026

eSIM, a Korean SIM card or a pocket Wi-Fi router: how each one works in Korea, who it suits, and what to sort out before you land.

Korea Plainly · 7 min read

Mobile data is not optional in Korea. You will use it for maps, translation, taxi apps and booking restaurants, and many places expect you to show something on your phone. The good news: coverage is excellent across cities, subways and most of the countryside, and there are three easy ways to get online.

Option 1: eSIM

An eSIM is a digital SIM you install by scanning a QR code or through an app. You can buy it at home and switch it on when you land, so you have data the moment you leave the plane.

  • Good for: solo travellers and couples whose phones support eSIM and are not locked to their home carrier.
  • Keep in mind: many tourist eSIMs are data-only, with no Korean phone number. That is fine for most travel apps, but a few local services send verification codes by text to a Korean number.
  • Check first: in your phone settings, look for an option such as "Add eSIM" or "Add cellular plan". If it is missing, your phone may not support eSIM.

Option 2: Korean SIM card

Physical tourist SIM cards are sold at airport telecom counters and online for airport pick-up. Some plans include a Korean phone number for calls and texts.

  • Good for: longer stays, travellers who want a local number, and older phones without eSIM.
  • Keep in mind: you need your passport to buy one, and you will have to keep your home SIM somewhere safe while you travel.

Option 3: Pocket Wi-Fi

A portable router that you rent, usually collected and returned at the airport. Several phones and laptops can share one connection.

  • Good for: families and groups who stay together all day.
  • Keep in mind: it is one more device to charge and carry, and whoever is away from the router has no data.

Which one should you choose?

  • Phone supports eSIM and you only need data: eSIM.
  • You want a Korean number, or your phone has no eSIM: Korean SIM card.
  • Three or more people who stay together: pocket Wi-Fi.

Before you fly

  • Check that your phone is unlocked if you plan to use a SIM or eSIM.
  • Download offline maps and a translation app at home, in case setup takes longer than expected.
  • Turn off data roaming on your home SIM to avoid surprise charges.
  • Save your accommodation address in Korean; it is useful for taxis and map searches.
Tip: Free Wi-Fi is common in cafes, subway stations and on many buses, but it is no substitute for your own data when you are lost or need a taxi.

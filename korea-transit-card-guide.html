Korea Transportation Card: How to Buy, Top Up and Use T-money

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 24 November 2026

Where to buy a T-money card, how to top it up with cash or Apple Wallet, Seoul fares and transfers, the Climate Card pass and refunds.

In Korea the transportation card (교통카드, gyotong kadeu) is a small prepaid card you tap on subway gates and bus readers. T-money is the brand you will see most, and one card covers the subway and city buses in Seoul, Busan, Daegu, Jeju and most other cities. You buy it for a few thousand won, top it up with cash and tap your way around. Here is how to do each step without fuss.

FROM MY OWN TRIPS

The first thing I tell friends to do after landing is buy a T-money card and top it up. Transfers between the subway and buses just work, you skip the ticket machines entirely, and you can use the same card in convenience stores and taxis. It saves a surprising amount of hassle from day one.

— Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly

T-money, Cashbee and what you actually need

T-money (written tmoney on its own website and app) and Cashbee are the two big card brands. Cashbee is more common around Busan, but both work on the same readers almost everywhere, so you never need two. There is no registration, no passport check and no deposit: the card is anonymous and the balance stays on it for years, so keep it for your next trip.

Plain cards sit next to character versions (Kakao Friends, Line Friends, K-pop groups) and key-ring or phone-strap cards that cost more. They all hold a balance in exactly the same way.

Where to buy a T-money card

  • Convenience stores. CU, GS25, 7-Eleven and emart24 keep cards by the till. Say "T-money card, please" or point at the rack. The cashier can top it up in the same transaction.
  • Incheon and Gimpo airports. The convenience stores in the arrivals halls and on the way to the AREX airport railway station all sell cards, so buy one before your first train. Our Incheon Airport to Seoul guide covers the routes into the city.
  • Subway stations. Many stations have card machines beside the ticket machines with an English button.

A plain card costs roughly ₩3,000 to ₩5,000 (2026), and it comes with a zero balance.

Topping up (충전, chungjeon)

Bring cash. At a convenience store, hand over the card with the notes and say "chungjeon hae juseyo" (top up, please) plus the amount, for example "man won" for ₩10,000. In a station, look for the machine labelled 교통카드 충전 (Transportation Card Top-up): lay the card on the tray, choose the amount and feed in ₩1,000, ₩5,000 or ₩10,000 notes. Machines do not take foreign credit cards for a plastic T-money card.

For three or four days in Seoul, ₩20,000 to ₩30,000 is a good first top-up. The gate screen shows your remaining balance each time you tap, so you will see when it runs low. Our money exchange guide explains where to get won notes.

Fares and the transfer discount in Seoul

With a card, a subway ride in the Seoul area starts at about ₩1,550 and a blue or green city bus at about ₩1,500 (2026). Longer subway trips add a little for distance, which is why you tap out at the end.

The real saving is 환승 (hwanseung, transfer). If you change between subway and bus within 30 minutes of tapping off (60 minutes late at night), you pay only for the extra distance rather than a new base fare, up to four transfers in one trip. You will hear the bus reader say "hwanseung-imnida" ("transfer") when it works. For the detailed rules see our public transport transfers guide.

Using it on the subway, buses and taxis

  • Subway. Tap the round reader on the gate going in and coming out. If the gate stays shut and the screen shows 잔액이 부족합니다 (balance too low), top up at the machine inside the gates and tap again. Our Seoul subway guide covers lines and exits.
  • City buses. Board at the front and tap by the driver; tap again by the rear door as you get off. The reader says "hacha-imnida" ("getting off"). Skip the tap-off and your next ride may charge a full fare. Many Seoul buses no longer take cash at all. See our Seoul bus guide.
  • Taxis. Nearly all Seoul taxis have a reader. Say "T-money" when the fare shows and tap.
  • Shops. Convenience stores, many vending machines and some cafés take it, which is the easiest way to spend a small balance.

One card per person: a gate lets one traveller through per tap, and the transfer discount follows each card's own journey. Keep the card apart from other contactless cards in your wallet too, or the gate may say 카드를 한 장만 대 주세요 (touch only one card).

What it does not cover

A T-money card does not pay for the KTX or other long-distance trains, and most express and intercity buses need a separate ticket (see our intercity bus guide). On the airport railway it covers the all-stop AREX train, but the non-stop Express train needs its own ticket.

Climate Card, WOWPASS and Apple Wallet

  • Climate Card short-term pass (기후동행카드). Seoul's unlimited-ride pass comes in 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7-day versions for visitors; a 3-day pass is around ₩10,000 plus the card (2026). It covers Seoul subway lines and Seoul city buses, but not AREX, airport buses or buses from Gyeonggi Province. It pays off if you take three or more separate rides a day.
  • WOWPASS. A prepaid payment card for foreigners, loaded with foreign currency at kiosks in Myeongdong, Hongdae and the airport. It has a separate T-money balance for fares that you top up in won. See our WOWPASS guide.
  • Apple Wallet. Since 2026 iPhone users can add a T-money card in Wallet and top it up with a Mastercard issued abroad, then tap the phone like a card. Keep a plastic card as a back-up for a flat battery.

Tapping a contactless Visa or Mastercard from home on a gate does not work in Seoul yet. Our cash and cards guide covers paying in shops.

Getting leftover money back

Convenience stores such as GS25 and CU refund small balances, roughly up to ₩20,000, for a fee of about ₩500. Larger balances go through T-money's own refund points. The price of the card itself is never refunded. Most visitors simply spend the last few thousand won on banana milk and gimbap at the airport. Fares and refund rules change from time to time, so check tmoney.co.kr before you go.

Busan, Jeju and other cities

The same card works on the Busan Metro, the Daegu and Gwangju subways, Jeju's island buses and city buses in most towns, so you only need one for the whole trip. Transfer windows and fares differ by city; Naver Map and KakaoMap show the fare for each route when you plan.

Quick checklist: buy a card at the airport convenience store, add ₩20,000 in cash, tap on and off every ride, and spend or refund what is left before you fly home.

FAQ

What is the best transportation card for tourists in Korea?

A plain T-money card from any convenience store suits most visitors: it works on subways and buses nationwide. Add a Climate Card pass only if you will ride many times a day within Seoul.

Can I top up a T-money card with a credit card?

Not a plastic one: shops and station machines take cash. A T-money card in Apple Wallet can be topped up with a Mastercard issued abroad.

Can two people share one T-money card?

No. A gate lets one person through per tap and the transfer discount only follows one journey, so give each traveller their own card.

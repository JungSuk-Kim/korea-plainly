T-money Card in Korea: How Tourists Buy, Top Up and Use a Transportation Card

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 18 October 2026

How to buy a T-money card, top it up with cash and use it on the Seoul subway, buses and taxis, plus refunds and the tourist passes worth knowing about.

A T-money card is the small prepaid transportation card most people in Korea tap to pay for the subway and city buses. You load money onto it, tap it on a reader as you travel, and the fare comes off the balance. For a first trip it is the simplest way to get around Seoul: no single tickets, no counting coins, and cheaper transfers between buses and the subway.

This guide explains what the card is, where to buy it, how to top it up, how to use it on each kind of transport, how to get leftover money back, and when a tourist pass might suit you better.

What T-money and Cashbee are

T-money (written tmoney on the company's website and app) is the best-known transportation card brand in Korea. Cashbee is the other big one; it is common around Busan and is sold in many of the same shops. For a visitor the two work the same way: both are prepaid cards you top up and tap. You do not need to register them, show a passport or link them to a bank account.

The cards come in plain designs and in character versions, key rings and phone straps, which cost more. All of them hold a balance in the same way, so choose whichever you like.

Where to buy a T-money card

  • Convenience stores. CU, GS25, 7-Eleven and emart24 usually keep cards by the till. Ask for a "T-money card". This is the easiest place to buy one in Seoul.
  • Subway stations. Many stations have card vending machines next to the ticket machines, with an English menu.
  • Incheon Airport. Convenience stores in the arrivals halls and at the airport railway station sell cards, so you can have one before your first train or bus into the city. See our Incheon Airport to Seoul guide for the routes.

A basic card usually costs a few thousand won (roughly ₩3,000 to ₩5,000 depending on the design and the shop). That price buys the card only; the card starts with no balance, so top it up straight away.

How to top up

Plan on topping up with cash. At a convenience store, hand over the card and the cash and say how much you want to add. At a subway station, use the machine marked for card recharging: put the card on the reader, choose an amount, then insert notes. Machines take ₩1,000, ₩5,000 and ₩10,000 notes; some also take ₩50,000.

Card machines and shops generally won't let you top up a physical T-money card with a foreign credit or debit card, so keep some won notes with you. Our money exchange guide explains where to get them. For a few days in Seoul, ₩20,000 to ₩30,000 is a sensible first top-up; the balance shows on the gate screen each time you tap.

Using it on the subway

Tap the card on the round reader at the ticket gate as you enter, and tap again at the gate as you leave. The fare depends on distance, so the exit tap matters. If the gate beeps and stays shut, the balance may be too low: top up at the machine just inside the gates. Our Seoul subway guide covers the map, lines and exits.

Using it on buses

Board at the front and tap the reader by the driver. When you get off, tap the reader by the rear door as well. Tapping off is easy to forget, but if you skip it you may miss the transfer discount on your next ride and pay a full fare again. Many city buses no longer take cash at all, which makes the card even more useful. See our Seoul bus guide for the colours and routes.

Transfers between bus and subway

The biggest saving from a T-money card is the transfer discount. In Seoul and the surrounding area, if you change between buses and the subway within a short window (about 30 minutes, longer late at night), you pay only the extra distance instead of a new base fare. You must use the same card and tap on and off every time. Transfer rules differ between cities, so in other regions treat it as a bonus rather than something to count on. Our transfers guide has more detail.

Taxis and shops

Most Seoul taxis have a card reader that takes T-money: tell the driver "T-money" and tap when the fare shows. Convenience stores and some vending machines and cafés accept it too, which is handy for using up a small leftover balance. A T-money card does not pay for KTX or other long-distance trains, and most intercity and express buses need a separate ticket.

Getting leftover money back

You can get unused balance back before you fly home. Small balances can usually be refunded at participating convenience stores, and larger amounts at refund machines in some subway stations or through T-money's own customer service. A small handling fee (around ₩500) is normally taken, and the price of the card itself is not refunded. The rules change from time to time, so check the current conditions on the official T-money website (tmoney.co.kr) or ask the cashier. Many visitors simply spend the last few thousand won on snacks at the airport.

Tourist alternatives to a T-money card

  • WOWPASS. A prepaid card for foreign visitors that you can load with foreign currency at kiosks; it works as a payment card in shops and has a separate transport (T-money) balance that you still top up for fares.
  • Korea Tour Card. A transportation card for visitors that works like T-money and includes discounts at some attractions and shops.
  • Climate Card short-term pass. Seoul's Climate Card (기후동행카드) sells 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7-day passes that tourists can buy. They give unlimited rides on the Seoul subway and Seoul city buses within the covered area, but not the Airport Railroad (AREX) or airport and intercity buses. At the time of writing a 3-day pass was around ₩10,000 plus a separate card fee, but check the current options and prices on official Seoul sources before you go. The 30-day version is being changed into a new monthly scheme for residents, which tourists cannot use.

For most short city breaks a plain T-money card is enough. A Climate Card pass is worth it if you will ride the subway or buses many times a day within Seoul.

Phones and foreign credit cards

Paying for buses and the subway directly with a contactless card from home is not yet possible in Seoul; foreign card payments on Seoul city buses are planned to start in 2027. Mobile T-money is now open to some visitors: iPhone users can add funds to Mobile T-money in Apple Wallet with a Mastercard, and Android users can use the Korea Tour Card app. Phone set-ups and supported cards change, so check before you go and carry a physical card as a back-up. Our cash and cards guide covers paying in shops.

Does it work in Busan, Jeju and other cities?

Yes. T-money and Cashbee cards work on the subway and city buses in Busan, Daegu, Daejeon and Gwangju, on Jeju's buses, and in most other cities, so one card will usually do for the whole trip. Top-up points and transfer discounts vary by region, so keep some cash and check the details in Naver Map or KakaoMap when you plan a route.

Quick checklist: buy a card at a convenience store, add ₩20,000 or so in cash, tap on and off on every bus and train, and get any leftover balance back (or spend it) before you leave.

FAQ

Where can I buy a T-money card in Seoul?

At almost any convenience store (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven, emart24), at card machines in many subway stations, and at convenience stores in Incheon Airport.

Can I top up T-money with a credit card?

Usually not for a physical card. Shops and station machines generally take cash for top-ups, so keep some won notes. Mobile T-money in Apple Wallet can be topped up with a Mastercard.

Is the Climate Card better than T-money for tourists?

It can be if you take many subway and bus rides a day within Seoul. It does not cover the Airport Railroad or rides outside its area, so a T-money card is still useful alongside it.

Can two people share one T-money card?

It is best not to. A subway gate lets one person through per tap, and transfer discounts only follow the card's own journey, so give each traveller their own card.

Does a T-money card expire?

No. The balance stays on the card, so you can keep it for your next trip to Korea.

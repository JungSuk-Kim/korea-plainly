Things to Do in Busan with Kids: An Easy Family Guide

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 3 November 2026

A practical family guide to Busan, with kid-friendly beaches, museums, parks, transport tips and local food.

Busan is a big, spread-out city, so family days work best when you explore one area at a time. Haeundae is easy to pair with an aquarium and beach train; Yeongdo makes a good museum day; and Gamcheon Culture Village is a colourful stop for older children. The Busan Metro and local buses cover most visitor areas, but allow time for transfers and hills.

Start with Haeundae Beach and the aquarium

SEA LIFE Busan Aquarium sits beside Haeundae Beach, a handy choice if you want an indoor activity and open space in the same outing. From Haeundae Station on Metro Line 2, walk towards the beach; the aquarium is along the waterfront. Inside, children can see marine animals and walk through an underwater tunnel. Give yourselves time for the beach too: there is room to run around, watch the waves and take a break from indoor attractions.

For a scenic ride, walk to Mipo and take the Haeundae Blueline Park Beach Train along the coast. It connects Mipo, Cheongsapo and Songjeong. The Sky Capsule runs between Mipo and Cheongsapo, not all the way to Songjeong, and seats are limited, so reserve ahead if you have your heart set on it. The Beach Train is a useful alternative when little legs need a rest. A light jacket is helpful on breezy coastal days.

Make space for play at Busan Citizens Park

Busan Citizens Park in Busanjin-gu is a good reset between busy sightseeing days. Its wide paths, lawns and play areas give children room to move while adults can sit for a while. It is also a pleasant place for a simple picnic: pick up kimbap (rice rolls) or snacks from a convenience store before you go. Bujeon Station on Metro Line 1 is a useful starting point for the park; check the walking route when you arrive, as the entrance you choose affects the distance.

Bring a ball or bubbles if you have them, and keep a spare change of clothes for younger children if they like playgrounds. The park is a better choice for an unhurried morning or afternoon than a tight stop between distant attractions.

Visit the National Maritime Museum in Yeongdo

The National Maritime Museum is on Yeongdo, the island district south of central Busan. Its ocean-focused galleries, ship models and aquarium make it a useful rainy-day option, especially for children who like boats and sea life. The museum is free to visit, though special exhibitions or activities may have separate arrangements. Some children’s programs can require advance booking, so look into those before travelling.

From Nampo Station on Metro Line 1, continue across to Yeongdo by local bus or taxi; this is not a short walk from the station. Use Naver Map or KakaoMap to check the best bus from your starting point. The museum is spacious, so plan a few hours rather than squeezing it into a quick stop. Its location also makes it easy to pair with a drive or bus ride around Yeongdo, rather than a trip back and forth across town.

Explore Gamcheon Culture Village at a child-friendly pace

Gamcheon Culture Village in Saha-gu is known for its brightly painted houses, small murals and stair-stepped lanes. Older children often enjoy spotting details and taking photos, but the steep streets can be tiring for toddlers and difficult with a stroller. From Toseong Station on Metro Line 1, use Exit 6 and catch a village bus towards Gamcheon Culture Village; the bus saves you from climbing the hill from the main road.

Once there, stick to the marked visitor paths and remember that people live in the houses around you. Keep voices low in residential lanes, and take care on steps, which can be uneven. A short visit is often enough for younger children. If everyone is still happy, stop for a drink or a sweet snack in one of the village cafés before heading back downhill.

Give everyone a taste of Busan

Busan has plenty of familiar foods alongside local favourites. Try milmyeon, cold wheat noodles served in broth or with a spicy sauce; ask for the sauce separately if a child prefers mild food. Dwaeji gukbap is pork and rice soup, and the rice can be served separately, which makes it easy to adjust for a young eater. At markets and snack shops, eomuk means fish cake, often served on a skewer in warm broth.

Bupyeong Kkangtong Market, near Nampo and Jagalchi, is a lively place to sample small bites, but it can feel crowded at busy times. Go earlier in the day with a stroller, or choose a quieter restaurant nearby for a sit-down meal. Useful words are “maepji anke” (not spicy) and “mul juseyo” (water, please). Showing the Korean phrase on your phone can help.

Keep the day simple

Use a T-money or Cashbee transport card for convenient subway and bus trips, and reload it at a convenience store. Busan’s lines make landmarks easy to identify: Line 2 serves Haeundae, while Line 1 reaches Toseong and Nampo. Check current tickets, transport routes and any booking requirements on Naver Map or the attraction’s official website before setting out.

For a first family visit, avoid pairing Haeundae with Gamcheon on the same day: they are on opposite sides of the city. A more relaxed plan is Haeundae and the coast on one day, then Nampo, Yeongdo or Gamcheon on another. Leave room for snack stops, bathroom breaks and the occasional bus ride that takes longer than expected.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the easiest way to get around Busan with children?

Busan has public transport, and the subway can be convenient for journeys near its stations. Buses and taxis may help when an attraction is farther away or little legs are tired. Check your route before setting out, and leave extra time for transfers and walking.

Can we plan activities for both sunny and rainy days?

Yes. Pair outdoor time at a beach or park with an indoor attraction, such as an aquarium or museum. Keeping your plans flexible makes it easier to adjust to the weather and your children’s energy. Check current visitor information before you go.

How many days should we spend in Busan with kids?

The right length depends on your pace and which places you want to visit. A few days can let your family enjoy a mix of outdoor sights and indoor activities without rushing. If you have less time, choose attractions in the same area for each outing.

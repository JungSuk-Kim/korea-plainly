Korean Skincare Shopping Apps for Visitors

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 12 November 2026

A practical guide to Olive Young, Hwahae and local shopping apps, with tips for checking ingredients, finding stores and paying as a visitor.

Korea’s skincare shops are easy to browse in person, but apps can help you compare products, look up ingredients and find a branch near your hotel. The catch is that many Korean shopping apps are designed for local residents: menus may be in Korean, and ordering can require a Korean phone number, payment method or delivery address. For a short visit, use apps mainly to research and navigate, then buy in a store.

Olive Young: the easiest place to shop in person

Olive Young is Korea’s best-known beauty and health chain, with branches in shopping areas such as Myeongdong, Hongdae and Gangnam. The Myeongdong shops are convenient from Myeongdong Station on Line 4; Hongdae branches are near Hongik Univ. Station on Line 2 and the Airport Railroad. Stores carry Korean skincare, makeup, hair care and travel-size items, so you can compare several brands in one stop.

The Korean Olive Young app can be useful for browsing promotions and product listings, but don’t assume its checkout or store information will be comfortable to use in English. Olive Young Global is a separate shopping service aimed at international customers; check its delivery terms rather than assuming an online order will arrive at your Seoul hotel. If you are shopping in person, open the product page or photograph the name and packaging so you can find the same item on the shelf.

Look for 할인 (discount), 기획세트 (value set) and 1+1 (buy one, get one) on shelf labels. Promotions vary by product and date. A large branch may have testers, but not every item will be available to try, and tester stock can differ from sellable stock. If a particular product matters, ask staff: 이 제품 있어요? means “Do you have this product?”

Hwahae: check ingredients and Korean reviews

Hwahae (화해) is a Korean beauty app known for product information, ingredient lists and user reviews. It is useful when you want to compare formulas rather than choose by packaging. Search a product name in Korean, or use your phone’s camera translation on the box. Helpful terms include 성분 (ingredients), 후기 (reviews), 민감성 (sensitive skin) and 무향료 (fragrance-free).

The app is primarily built for Korean users, so expect Korean text and reviews that may not translate cleanly. Treat ratings as one clue, not a guarantee that a product will suit your skin: look at the ingredient list and your own sensitivities. Google Lens or Papago can translate product names and short label text. A photo of the ingredient panel is also handy when comparing a product in a store near Hongik Univ. Station with one you saw in Myeongdong.

Naver Map: find a shop, not just a product

Use Naver Map to search for 올리브영 (Olive Young) around your accommodation or a subway stop. It is particularly helpful in Seoul, where several branches may be close together. Search near Gangnam Station on Line 2 or around Myeongdong Station on Line 4, then check the branch listing before setting off. Naver Map is also useful for saving a store location and planning a walking route between shops.

Map listings and app interfaces can be easier to use in Korean than English. Copying 올리브영 into the search box is often more reliable than voice search. If you need a specific shade or formula, don’t treat a map listing as proof that the item is in stock; ask the branch or choose a nearby larger store. Check current details on Naver Map or the official site.

Naver Shopping and Coupang: useful, with limits

Naver Shopping can help you see how a product is listed by Korean sellers and compare online prices. Coupang is a major local delivery app, but ordering often makes more sense for residents than for a visitor staying a few nights. Korean-language forms, account verification, payment setup and delivery instructions can all be obstacles, and a hotel may not be able to receive parcels on your behalf.

If you do order, ask your accommodation first whether it accepts deliveries and how to enter its Korean address. Confirm the product name, size and seller before paying; similar-looking listings can have different versions. For a weekend in Seoul, buying at an Olive Young branch is usually simpler than arranging a parcel. Naver Pay and Kakao Pay are common local payment services, but don’t assume you can register them with a foreign number or card.

Make the app research work in the shop

  • Save the exact name. Screenshot the product, size and Korean name before you leave your hotel. This avoids confusing products with similar packaging.
  • Check the label. 자외선 차단제 means sunscreen; SPF and PA ratings are printed on the package. For a fragrance concern, look for 무향료, but read the full ingredient list too.
  • Compare the offer. A set or 1+1 deal may be better value, but check how many items and what sizes are included.
  • Keep the receipt. It helps you identify the item later and is useful if you need to ask the shop about an exchange. Store policies differ, so ask before opening a product.

For most visitors, the useful combination is simple: use Hwahae or translation tools to research a formula, Naver Map to locate an Olive Young, and the store shelf to confirm the item and promotion. Treat Korean delivery apps as optional unless you have a local contact and a confirmed delivery plan.

Bottom line: Use Hwahae or a translation app to check ingredients, then find a nearby Olive Young with Naver Map and buy in person. Don’t rely on Korean delivery apps unless your accommodation confirms it can receive parcels.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use Korean skincare shopping apps if I don’t read Korean?

Some apps may have limited English support, so check the language options before creating an account. A translation tool can help with product descriptions, but ingredient names and product variants may not translate clearly. Compare the packaging and product name, and ask a store employee if you are unsure what you are ordering.

Can I have an app order delivered to my hotel?

That depends on the app’s delivery options and the hotel’s package policy. Before ordering, ask the hotel whether it accepts parcels for guests, and confirm the delivery address and recipient name format. Check the estimated delivery information in the app, and avoid relying on a parcel arriving before you leave.

How can I check whether a skincare product on an app is the one I want?

Compare the product name, packaging, size, and variant with the item you saw in a store or on the brand’s official channels. Read the ingredient list and seller information where available, and check recent reviews for useful details. If anything is unclear, confirm with the seller before placing an order.

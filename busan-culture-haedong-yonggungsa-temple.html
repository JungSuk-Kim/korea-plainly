Haedong Yonggungsa: Busan’s Temple Above the Sea

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 8 October 2026

Photo: Christian Bolz, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

At a glance: Haedong Yonggungsa is a Buddhist temple in Gijang, on Busan’s northeastern coast. Its seaside setting makes it very different from temples tucked into the mountains: visitors come for the coastal views, the temple grounds and the chance to experience a place of worship beside the water.

Address: 86 Yonggung-gil, Gijang, Busan

Why visit Haedong Yonggungsa?

Many Korean temples are set among wooded hills, but Haedong Yonggungsa sits beside the sea. That coastal position is the defining feature of a visit: temple buildings, Buddhist figures and traditional details appear against a backdrop of open water and rocky shoreline. The combination makes the site memorable even for travelers who have already visited other temples in Korea.

The temple is an active religious site as well as a popular destination. Its atmosphere can shift from quiet and contemplative to busy, especially when many visitors gather around the most photogenic viewpoints. Take time to look beyond the views: notice the arrangement of buildings, the devotional objects and the way the paths connect the different parts of the grounds.

Temple tradition traces Haedong Yonggungsa’s origins to 1376, during the Goryeo period. Whether you come for its religious significance, architecture or setting, it is worth approaching the visit with the understanding that people are here to pray as well as sightsee.

What to see on the grounds

Walk through the grounds at an unhurried pace rather than treating the temple as a single viewpoint. The approach and paths bring you past religious imagery and temple structures before opening onto views of the coast. The changing angles are part of the experience: pause where it is safe and unobtrusive, and look back toward the buildings as well as out to sea.

Photography is a natural part of many visits, but be considerate around worshippers. Avoid blocking narrow walkways or photographing people at close range without their permission. If a building is being used for prayer, keep your voice low and follow any signs or instructions on site. Some interiors may not be open to visitors, so enjoy them from the areas that are clearly accessible.

How to plan your visit

Haedong Yonggungsa is in Gijang, away from Busan’s central neighborhoods. Check the route from your hotel before setting out, and allow enough time for the journey and for walking around the grounds. Use the full address—86 Yonggung-gil, Gijang, Busan—in your navigation app, and confirm the current route and service information on the day of your visit. This is especially helpful if you are unfamiliar with the area or traveling during a busy period.

Wear comfortable shoes with a good grip. The visit involves walking around outdoor paths, and the route includes steps and changes in level. If mobility is a concern, check current access information with the temple before you go; the grounds may not be equally easy to navigate for every visitor.

Weather affects the experience. On a clear day, the sea is a dramatic part of the setting; in rain or strong wind, exposed coastal paths may feel less comfortable. Bring sun protection in bright weather and take care on wet surfaces. A quieter visit is more likely outside peak periods, though visitor numbers vary and no time is guaranteed to be calm.

Temple etiquette and photography

Visitors are welcome to appreciate the site, but remember that it remains a religious place. Keep your voice low around prayer spaces, avoid interrupting worship, and follow posted instructions. Ask permission before photographing people, especially anyone engaged in prayer. If a space is marked as restricted, respect the boundary even if it appears to offer a good photo angle.

For photos of the coastline and temple architecture, take care near steps and uneven ground. Keep pathways clear, and do not climb on walls or rocks for a better view. These small courtesies help protect both the site and the experience of other visitors.

Good to know

  • It is a place of worship. Keep conversations quiet near prayer areas and follow signs and staff guidance.
  • Plan for walking. Wear comfortable shoes and be prepared for steps and uneven outdoor surfaces.
  • Check details before leaving. Opening arrangements, access conditions and travel routes can change. Consult the official website and your map app for current information.
  • Be patient at viewpoints. Popular spots can get crowded. Wait your turn for photos and keep paths clear for others.
  • Bring what you need. Do not assume that facilities or refreshments will be available at a particular time; check ahead if you need specific services.

Is Haedong Yonggungsa worth the trip?

For many visitors, yes—particularly if you want to see a different side of Busan from its beaches, markets and urban districts. The temple’s setting beside the water is distinctive, and the visit offers a blend of coastal scenery and living religious culture. It may be less suitable for travelers seeking a completely quiet or effortless stop: the site can be busy, and walking around involves steps. Give yourself enough time to explore without rushing, and treat the temple as more than a photo stop.

FAQ

Where is Haedong Yonggungsa Temple?

It is at 86 Yonggung-gil, Gijang, Busan, on the city’s northeastern coast. Use the address in a map app to plan your route.

Is there an entrance fee?

Check the temple’s official website for current visitor information before your visit. Do not rely on old listings for details that may change.

Can I visit if I have limited mobility?

The grounds include steps and changes in level, which may make access challenging. Contact the temple ahead of time to ask about current accessibility conditions.

DMZ Tours from Seoul: What You'll See and How to Choose One

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 17 October 2026 · Updated 23 October 2026

DMZ tours from Seoul compared: coach tours, Paju's own shuttle from Imjingak and the DMZ train, plus the 2026 JSA status, passport rules and Monday closures.

Photo: Lance Vanlewen, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

The Demilitarised Zone, the 4-kilometre-wide strip that has divided North and South Korea since the 1953 armistice, is only about an hour's drive north of Seoul, which is why DMZ tours from Seoul are one of the most popular day trips in the country. A day there is sobering and strange: soldiers checking passports, a tunnel dug towards Seoul and a view into North Korea. This guide explains what you will actually see, the three main ways to go, what has changed with the JSA, and what to bring.

What a DMZ tour actually shows you

Most "DMZ tours" visit the Civilian Control Zone just south of the DMZ in Paju, not the zone itself. A typical half-day or full-day tour includes most of these:

  • Imjingak Peace Park: the starting point of almost every tour, with the Freedom Bridge, a bullet-scarred steam locomotive and fences covered in prayer ribbons left by separated families. It sits outside the restricted area, so anyone can visit.
  • The Third Infiltration Tunnel: a tunnel dug from the North and found in 1978. You walk (or, when it is running, ride a small monorail) down a steep slope and then stoop along the tunnel itself. Bags and phones go into lockers, and photos are not allowed inside.
  • Dora Observatory: a hilltop viewpoint where, on a clear day, you can see a North Korean village and the edge of Kaesong through binoculars. Spring haze often hides the view.
  • Dorasan Station: a modern station built for a railway to the North that has never run a regular service.

Read the itinerary closely: the cheapest tours often finish with a long stop at a ginseng or amethyst shop.

Three ways to do a DMZ tour from Seoul

1. A coach tour with pick-up in Seoul

This is what most first-time visitors book, and it is the easiest. A bus collects you from a hotel or a meeting point near City Hall, Myeongdong or Hongdae at around 7:00–8:00, an English-speaking guide explains each stop, and a half-day tour returns by early afternoon. In 2026 a half-day coach tour usually costs roughly ₩60,000–₩80,000 per person, with full-day tours and private cars costing more. Coach tours run most days; on Mondays, when the Third Tunnel is closed, many swap in other stops.

2. Paju's own DMZ shuttle from Imjingak

Paju city runs its own bus tour into the control zone from Imjingak. It is much cheaper (around ₩10,000 in 2026, a little more with the tunnel monorail) but needs more effort. You make your own way to Imjingak, by Gyeongui–Jungang Line train towards Munsan and then a short bus or taxi ride (check the route on Naver Map or KakaoMap). Only part of each bus is sold online; the rest of the seats go at the Imjingak ticket window from the morning, first come, first served, and popular weekends sell out. Everyone in your group must be at the window with their own physical passport. There is no English commentary on board, and the tour does not run on Mondays or on major holidays.

3. Korail's DMZ train to Dorasan

Korail runs a DMZ train from Seoul Station to Dorasan, with a coach for the restricted sites and a guide included. It runs only on a few selected days each month and needs a minimum number of passengers, so check the dates on the Korail website first.

The JSA question

The Joint Security Area at Panmunjom, with its famous blue huts where soldiers from both sides face each other, is a separate and much more restricted place. Public tours were halted in July 2023 after a US soldier crossed into North Korea during a visit. Since May 2025 the UN Command has allowed only special visits for officials and invited guests, arranged weeks ahead through the government. In practice, ordinary travellers cannot book a JSA tour in 2026. Be wary of any listing that promises one.

How to choose a DMZ tour

  • Half-day or full-day: a full day adds stops such as the Majang Lake suspension bridge or a talk with a North Korean defector, plus lunch.
  • Pick-up point: check how far the meeting point is from your hotel. Early pick-ups do not wait for latecomers.
  • Guide language: confirm the commentary is in English, not split between several languages.
  • What is included: entrance fees, the tunnel monorail and lunch are sometimes extras. Look for "no shopping" if you would rather skip the shop stop.
  • Cancellation policy: sites can close at short notice for military reasons, so choose a company that refunds or reschedules.

Before you go

  • Bring your physical passport. Soldiers check it at the checkpoint, and a photo or copy is refused. Residents can use their Korean residence card.
  • Check closing days. The Third Tunnel and Paju's shuttle are closed on Mondays and on Seollal and Chuseok, and the restricted area can close without warning. Confirm with your operator the day before.
  • Follow the photo rules. Photos of military posts are often banned, as are drones. Guides tell you where you can shoot.
  • Wear good shoes. The tunnel slope is steep and the walk back up is tiring, and the tunnel itself is low and narrow.
  • Dress warmly in winter. The area is open, windy and colder than Seoul.
Tip: Book at least a few days ahead in spring and autumn, when tours fill up. Check the meeting point on Naver Map or KakaoMap the evening before, and keep your passport in your day bag, not the hotel safe.

Is it worth it?

If you are curious about modern Korean history, yes. It is less dramatic than films suggest, but standing a few kilometres from North Korea and hearing how families were split stays with you. If you only have two or three days in Seoul and prefer food and neighbourhoods, it is fine to skip it; our 3-day Seoul itinerary works without it. With a week, a half-day tour fits well into a 7-day Korea itinerary. For other ideas, see our day trips from Seoul.

FAQ

Can I visit the DMZ from Seoul without a tour?

You can reach Imjingak Peace Park on your own by train and bus, but the Third Tunnel and Dora Observatory are in a restricted area. To see them you need either a tour from Seoul or Paju city's shuttle from Imjingak, with your passport.

Is the JSA at Panmunjom open to tourists in 2026?

No. Public tours stopped in July 2023, and since 2025 only special visits for officials and invited guests have been allowed. Ordinary DMZ tours from Seoul still visit the tunnel, observatory and Dorasan Station.

How long does a DMZ tour from Seoul take?

A half-day tour takes about six hours including the drive, leaving early in the morning and returning in the early afternoon. Full-day tours take eight to ten hours.

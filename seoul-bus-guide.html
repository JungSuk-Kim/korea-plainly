How to Use Seoul Buses as a Foreigner

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026

Learn how to plan a bus trip in Seoul, pay with a transit card, board and exit, and make transfers with less stress.

Photo: STA3816, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Seoul’s buses reach many parts of the city, including places that are a long walk from a subway station. They can feel busy at first, but a map app and a transit card make most trips straightforward. Here is what to know before you board.

  2. Get a transit card
  3. Board and pay

Plan your route

Use Naver Map or KakaoMap to check routes, bus stop locations, estimated travel time, and live arrival information. Both offer English-language options. Google Maps may be less helpful for public-transport directions in Korea, so it is useful to have a local map app ready.

Check the direction of travel as well as the route number. Bus stops often serve several routes, and buses with the same number can travel in opposite directions. In your app, confirm the stop name, the bus number, and how many stops you will ride. Save or screenshot your destination in Korean if you may need to show it to someone.

Bus stop signs usually show route numbers and destinations. Some stops also have electronic displays with arrival times. Information may appear in Korean and English, but the amount of English varies. If you are unsure, compare the stop name and route number with your map app.

Get a transit card

A T-money card is a convenient way to pay for regular city buses and the subway. You can usually buy or recharge one at convenience stores and at some subway stations. Look for the T-money sign, and check what payment methods the shop or machine accepts. Carry some cash for recharging because payment options can vary.

A foreign bank card that works for contactless payments may not work as a transit card on a bus. Do not assume you can simply tap your usual credit or debit card. If you plan to use a transit card, keep it separate from other contactless cards so the reader detects the one you intend to use.

Bus fares depend on the bus and route, so check the fare information in your map app or at the stop. If you need help buying or topping up a card, show its name on your phone or ask a staff member at a convenience store.

Board and pay

When your bus approaches, check the number before getting on. At a busy stop, stand where people are queuing. Buses usually board through the front door. Tap your transit card on the reader near the entrance and wait for the beep or other confirmation. If the reader does not respond, try again or ask the driver for help rather than walking on without checking.

At some stops, a bus may not pull in unless someone is waiting to board. Stand where the driver can see you and raise a hand as the bus approaches. Do this only when you want that bus; checking the number first helps avoid stopping the wrong one.

After boarding, move away from the entrance so other passengers can get on. Seats may be limited, and buses can start moving before you have found a place to sit. Hold a rail or strap if you are standing. Give older passengers, pregnant passengers, and people with disabilities space to use priority seating.

Know when to get off

Follow your route in the map app and watch the stop displays, rather than relying only on announcements. On many buses, passengers press a stop button to request the next stop. Press it after your stop has been announced or appears on the screen. If you are not sure which stop is yours, show the destination on your phone to a nearby passenger or the driver, but be aware that the driver may not speak English.

Buses usually let passengers exit through a rear or middle door. Before getting off, tap your transit card on the exit reader and check for confirmation. Keep your card ready so you do not hold up the line.

Transfers and bus colors

If you are changing to another bus or the subway, tap your card when you leave the first bus and again when you board the next service. Tapping off helps the fare system recognize your journey and may be needed for a transfer fare. Transfer rules and time limits can depend on the route and current fare system, so check the details for your trip. If you pay in a way that does not use a transit card, transfer benefits may not apply.

In Seoul, blue buses generally run along major routes, while green buses connect neighborhoods with larger transport hubs. Yellow buses serve circulation routes, and red buses commonly connect Seoul with nearby cities. These colors are a helpful guide, but always check the route number and destination. Airport limousine buses are a separate service, with their own stops and payment arrangements.

Travel comfortably

Keep your voice low, avoid blocking the aisle, and let passengers get off before you board. During busy periods, a bus may be crowded, so consider taking the next one if you are uncomfortable or have bulky luggage. If you use a wheelchair, travel with a stroller, or need step-free access, check the specific route and vehicle information in advance; accessibility features can vary.

For a first trip, choose a simple route, leave extra time, and keep your map open as you travel. Once you have used the card readers and learned how to follow your stop, Seoul’s buses become a practical way to explore the city.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

First Trip to Korea? Start Here: A Step-by-Step Planner

By Korea Plainly · Published 15 October 2026

Planning your first trip to Korea, step by step: when to go, how long to stay, entry rules, money, phones, getting from the airport, getting around and what to know on the ground.

Photo: Bgag, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons

Planning a first trip to Korea can feel like a lot: visas and arrival cards, which cities to see, how the money and transport work, which apps to download. This page puts it all in order. Work through the steps below, follow the links you need, and you will arrive knowing exactly what to do. Everything here is written for first-time visitors, in plain English.

Step 1: choose when to go

Korea has four very different seasons. Spring (April) brings cherry blossoms, autumn (October to early November) brings clear skies and red leaves, summer is hot and rainy, and winter is cold, dry and bright, with skiing and lights. Spring and autumn are the most popular, so book early for them.

Step 2: decide how long and where

Most first visits are one to two weeks. Seoul deserves at least three days; with a week you can add Gyeongju and Busan by fast train, and with ten days or more you can add Jeonju and Jeju.

Step 3: check the entry rules

Many nationalities can visit Korea without a visa for a short stay, but you may need a K-ETA (an online travel authorisation) depending on your passport and travel dates, and every visitor fills in an arrival card, which can be done online before you fly. Rules change, so check them for your passport a few weeks before you travel.

Step 4: sort out money and budget

Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but a little cash helps at markets and street stalls. Not every ATM takes foreign cards, so it helps to know which ones do. Shopping can come with a tax refund.

Step 5: get your phone ready

You will want mobile data from the moment you land, for maps, translation and messaging. Before you fly, choose a SIM, eSIM or pocket Wi-Fi, and install the apps Koreans actually use: Google Maps directions are limited in Korea.

Step 6: arrive and get into the city

Most international flights land at Incheon, about an hour from central Seoul. The airport train and limousine buses are easy and cheap; taxis cost more but go door to door.

Step 7: learn how to get around

Korea's public transport is cheap, clean and easy once you have a transit card. The subway covers most of Seoul and Busan, buses fill the gaps, and fast KTX trains link the big cities.

FAQ

Do I need a visa to visit Korea?

Many nationalities can visit visa-free for a short stay, but some need a K-ETA and the rules change. Check our entry guide and the official sites for your passport.

How many days do I need in Korea?

A week is enough for a good first visit to Seoul plus one or two other places. Ten days to two weeks lets you add Jeju or Jeonju at a relaxed pace.

Is Korea easy for first-time visitors?

Yes. It is safe, transport is excellent, and English signs are common in cities. The main things to prepare are mobile data, a Korean map app and a transit card.

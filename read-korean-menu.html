How to Read a Korean Menu Without Speaking Korean

By Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

Read a Korean menu without speaking Korean: the words for cooking methods and ingredients, price terms like 인분, spotting spicy dishes and translation apps.

Korea Plainly · 5 min read

A Korean menu with no English or pictures can look impossible, but most dish names are built the same way: a main ingredient followed by a cooking method. Learn a dozen of these building blocks and you can work out most of a menu. You don't even need to read Hangul fluently, although learning the alphabet takes an afternoon and helps a lot.

The easy way first: use your phone

Camera translation apps such as Papago and Google Lens can translate a menu as you point at it. Papago is made by Naver and is especially good with Korean. The translations are not always perfect ("soldier stew" for budae-jjigae, for example), so the word lists below help you make sense of the results.

Cooking methods: the end of the name

The last part of a dish name usually tells you how it is cooked:

  • -guk / -tang (국 / 탕): soup. Galbitang is a beef rib soup.
  • -jjigae (찌개): a thicker, often spicy stew. Kimchi-jjigae and doenjang-jjigae are everyday favourites.
  • -gui (구이): grilled. Saengseon-gui is grilled fish.
  • -bokkeum (볶음): stir-fried. Ojingeo-bokkeum is stir-fried squid.
  • -jjim (찜): braised or steamed. Galbi-jjim is braised short ribs.
  • -jeon (전): savoury pancake or fritter. Haemul-pajeon is a seafood and spring onion pancake.
  • -muchim (무침): seasoned and mixed, usually cold.
  • -bap (밥): rice dish. Bibimbap is "mixed rice".
  • -myeon / -guksu (면 / 국수): noodles. Naengmyeon is cold noodles.

Main ingredients: the start of the name

  • So / sogogi (소 / 소고기): beef
  • Dwaeji (돼지): pork. Samgyeopsal (삼겹살) is pork belly.
  • Dak (닭): chicken
  • Haemul (해물): seafood
  • Saengseon (생선): fish
  • Ojingeo (오징어): squid
  • Saeu (새우): shrimp
  • Dubu (두부): tofu. Sundubu is soft tofu.
  • Gyeran (계란): egg
  • Kimchi (김치) and doenjang (된장), a fermented soybean paste

Put them together and you can read a menu: dwaeji-gukbap is pork soup with rice, dak-galbi is spicy stir-fried chicken, and sundubu-jjigae is a soft tofu stew.

Words around the prices

  • 인분 (inbun): per serving. 1인분 means one portion; barbecue places often require at least two.
  • 소 / 중 / 대 (so / jung / dae): small, medium, large, for dishes to share.
  • 시가 (siga): market price, common for seafood. Ask the price before ordering.
  • 추가 (chuga): extra or add-on, such as extra noodles or cheese.
  • 공기밥 (gonggibap): a bowl of rice, often ordered separately with barbecue.
  • 세트 (seteu): a set meal.

Spotting spicy food

Look for 매운 (maeun, spicy), chilli pepper symbols, or a deep red colour in the photos. Words like 얼큰 (eolkeun, hot and hearty) also hint at spice. If unsure, ask "maewoyo?" (매워요?, is it spicy?).

A quick Hangul tip

Hangul, the Korean alphabet, has 24 basic letters and was designed to be easy to learn. Even being able to sound out words slowly helps you match a dish name to a word list (our learn to read Hangul guide takes about an hour) or a photo. Many visitors learn it on the flight.

FAQ

Which app is best for translating a Korean menu?

Papago and Google Lens both translate menus through your camera. Papago often handles Korean food names better.

What does 인분 mean on a menu?

It means "per serving". The price shown is for one portion.

Are Korean menus usually in English?

In tourist areas and chains, often yes. In small local restaurants, many menus are in Korean only.

How Much Does a Trip to Korea Cost? A Daily Budget Guide

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 15 October 2026 · Updated 17 November 2026

Korea travel costs in 2026: hotel, food, subway, KTX and sight prices in won, three sample daily budgets and how much a week costs.

Photo: Smiley.toerist, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Most visitors spend between about 80,000 won a day (hostel, convenience-store breakfasts, subway everywhere) and 300,000 won or more (a good hotel and Korean barbecue most nights). The gap comes almost entirely from where you sleep and how often you sit down to eat. Here is what things cost in 2026, in Korean won (₩), with three sample days. At late-2026 exchange rates, ₩10,000 is a little over 7 US dollars.

Where to stay: the biggest line in your budget

  • Hostel dorm or guesthouse (게스트하우스): roughly ₩25,000 to ₩45,000 a night in Seoul. Hongdae, Euljiro and around Seoul Station have the most choice, and many include toast and coffee.
  • Business hotel or small hotel: roughly ₩90,000 to ₩170,000 for a double room. Look near Myeongdong, Jongno or Seoul Station for a short walk to the subway and airport trains.
  • Four- and five-star hotels: usually ₩250,000 and up, more in Gangnam and at Haeundae in Busan.

Outside Seoul, Busan is a little cheaper, and Gyeongju, Jeonju and Sokcho cheaper again on weekdays. Prices jump at weekends, in cherry blossom season (early April), in autumn foliage season (late October to mid November) and around Seollal and Chuseok, when Koreans travel too.

Food: cheap if you eat like a local

  • Convenience store (편의점): a triangle gimbap (삼각김밥) is around ₩1,500 to ₩2,000 and a dosirak lunch box around ₩5,000. See our convenience store guide.
  • Everyday restaurant meal: kimchi-jjigae, sundubu, gukbap or bibimbap with free side dishes (banchan) and water costs about ₩9,000 to ₩13,000 in Seoul, a little less in other cities.
  • Market and street food: tteokbokki, eomuk (fish cake) skewers and hotteok cost a few thousand won each at Gwangjang Market or Busan's BIFF Square. See our street food guide.
  • Korean barbecue: samgyeopsal is priced per portion of about 150 to 200 g, often ₩16,000 to ₩20,000, and most places want at least two portions. Two people with soju usually spend ₩50,000 to ₩70,000. See our Korean BBQ guide.
  • Coffee: the surprise for many visitors. An americano at an independent café or Starbucks is around ₩4,500 to ₩6,500, about the price of lunch. Budget chains such as Mega Coffee, Compose and Paik's Coffee sell one for around ₩2,000.

There is no tipping and no service charge in ordinary restaurants, so the price on the menu is what you pay.

Getting around

Public transport is the best value in the country. With a T-money card, a Seoul subway ride starts at ₩1,550 and a city bus at ₩1,500, and transfers within 30 minutes are free or nearly free. If you will ride a lot, Seoul's Climate Card (기후동행카드) short-term pass covers unlimited subway and city buses, about ₩5,000 for one day or ₩20,000 for seven.

  • Airport: the AREX all-stop train from Incheon to Seoul Station is the cheapest way in, at under ₩5,000; airport limousine buses cost several times that but drop you near many hotels.
  • Taxis: a regular Seoul taxi starts at ₩4,800, so a short ride across town for two or three people is often ₩8,000 to ₩15,000. Fares rise 20 to 40 percent between 22:00 and 04:00. Call one with Kakao T.
  • Between cities: the KTX is usually the biggest single transport cost. Seoul to Busan is around ₩54,000 one way after the autumn 2026 fare cut; express buses take longer but cost roughly half. See our KTX guide.

Sights and activities

A lot of the best of Korea is free: Bukchon's alleys, Cheonggyecheon stream, the Han River parks at Yeouido and Banpo, Namsan's walking paths, markets and nearly all temples. The five Joseon palaces charge a small fee, ₩3,000 at Gyeongbokgung, and let you in free if you wear hanbok. Costs add up with observation decks such as N Seoul Tower and Seoul Sky, theme parks (a Lotte World or Everland day ticket is ₩60,000 or more at the gate), DMZ tours, cooking classes and K-pop shows.

Three sample daily budgets

Per person, per day, in Seoul, not including flights or shopping. Elsewhere, take off ten to twenty percent. For your own dates, try our trip cost calculator.

  • Backpacker, about ₩80,000 to ₩110,000: a hostel bed (₩35,000), a convenience-store breakfast (₩5,000), a gukbap lunch (₩10,000), market food and a beer for dinner (₩15,000), a ₩2,000 coffee, ₩7,000 of subway, and a free palace in hanbok or a Han River walk.
  • Mid-range, about ₩160,000 to ₩250,000: half of a ₩150,000 hotel room, a café breakfast, a restaurant lunch, half of a barbecue dinner with drinks, one paid sight, the subway and one taxi.
  • Comfortable, ₩300,000 or more: a four- or five-star room, taxis by default, a tasting menu or hanwoo beef dinner, and a show or day tour.

A typical first trip of a week in Seoul plus two nights in Busan comes to roughly ₩1.3 million to ₩2 million per person on a mid-range budget, before flights and shopping.

Easy ways to save

  • Eat your big meal at lunch. Many restaurants have a 점심 특선 (lunch special) that is a third cheaper than the evening menu.
  • Drink the free water from the self-service jug.
  • Claim the tax refund. Spend ₩15,000 or more in a tax-free shop such as Olive Young and show your passport for an instant discount. See our tax refund guide.
  • Pay by card, but carry ₩30,000 to ₩50,000 in cash for market stalls and topping up T-money. Our cash and cards guide explains which cards work.
  • Travel midweek and avoid the peaks. A Tuesday-to-Thursday hotel in March or late November can cost half the price of a Saturday in cherry blossom week.

Fares and entry fees change, often in January or July, so check the operator's site or Naver Map before you travel if a figure matters to your plan.

FAQ

Is Korea expensive to visit?

It is mid-priced. Transport and everyday Korean meals are cheaper than in Japan, Western Europe or the US; central Seoul hotels and café coffee are the main costs. A careful traveller can manage on about ₩100,000 a day.

How much money do I need for a week in Korea?

Roughly ₩700,000 per person on a backpacker budget and ₩1.2 million to ₩1.7 million mid-range, plus flights and shopping. Sharing a room makes the biggest difference.

How much cash should I bring to Korea?

Not much. Cards work almost everywhere, including taxis and convenience stores. ₩50,000 to ₩100,000 in cash covers market food, small stalls and transit card top-ups for a week, and you can withdraw more at a Global ATM.

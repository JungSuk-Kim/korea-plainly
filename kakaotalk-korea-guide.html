How to Use KakaoTalk in Korea

By Korea Plainly · Published 12 October 2026

A practical guide for visitors to setting up KakaoTalk, adding contacts, messaging, and using business channels in Korea.

Photo: chelsom tsai from TAIPEI, Taiwam, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

KakaoTalk is a popular messaging app in Korea. You can use it to chat with people you meet, share photos, make calls, and receive messages from some businesses. It is useful to have, but you do not need it for every part of a trip. Here is how to get started and use it comfortably.

Set up the app before you need it

Download KakaoTalk from the Apple App Store or Google Play Store. Check that the publisher is Kakao Corp. before installing. The app is available in English, though some menus or business messages may appear in Korean. The exact language options can depend on your device and app version.

To create an account, you will generally need a phone number that can receive a verification code by text message. A number from outside Korea may work, so you may be able to register before your trip. Enter the country code carefully and make sure the number can receive texts while you set up the account. A Korean SIM is not automatically required just to use KakaoTalk.

If you already have an account, use that account rather than creating another one for your visit. Keep access to the phone number and device connected to it, and check your login details before travelling. If you change phones or reinstall the app, you may need to verify your account again. Back up chats if you want to keep them; do not assume that every conversation will automatically appear on a new device.

Add friends and start a chat

You can add someone from your contacts, search for their KakaoTalk ID, or scan their personal QR code. The QR option is handy when you meet someone in person: open the friend or QR feature in the app and let the other person scan your code, or scan theirs. Menu names can change with app updates.

A KakaoTalk ID is different from a phone number. If you prefer not to share your number, you can give someone your ID or QR code instead, if your account settings allow people to find you that way. You can also control whether others can search for you by ID or phone number in the privacy and friend settings. Check these options before sharing your details.

For a group outing, ask the organizer which contact method they prefer. A group chat can make it easy to share a meeting point, photos, or last-minute updates. You can mute a busy conversation or leave it when you no longer need it. Before sending a message to a group, check that you have selected the right chat.

Use messages, photos, and calls

KakaoTalk supports text messages, photo and video sharing, and voice and video calls. Calls use an internet connection, so connect to Wi-Fi or use mobile data. If you are travelling with limited data, avoid sending large videos or making long calls over a mobile connection. You can review the app's data and media settings to manage downloads and notifications.

Check your phone's notification permissions if messages are not appearing on screen. Battery-saving settings can also delay alerts on some devices. In a quiet place, you may want to mute a group or switch off message previews on your lock screen so private messages are not visible to people nearby.

Follow businesses and local updates

Some shops, restaurants, attractions, and other organizations use KakaoTalk Channels to share notices or communicate with customers. A business may display a QR code or link at its counter, on a sign, or on its website. Follow a channel only if you want its updates, and check the name and profile before sharing information or responding to a request.

Do not assume that a KakaoTalk message means you have a confirmed booking, order, or appointment. Look for a clear confirmation and keep any relevant details, such as the time, location, and contact name. If a message is in Korean and you are unsure what it asks you to do, use a translation tool or ask the business to clarify before paying or sharing personal information.

Protect your account and personal information

  • Use your phone's screen lock and keep your KakaoTalk account details private.
  • Be cautious with unexpected links, payment requests, and requests for verification codes. Do not share a code sent to your phone with another person.
  • Review profile visibility, friend search, and chat settings. Share your phone number, ID, or QR code only with people you trust.
  • If you use a shared or borrowed phone, sign out when finished and avoid saving private messages or photos on it.

For public Wi-Fi, use networks you trust and avoid entering sensitive information if you are unsure about the connection. If you think someone else has access to your account, check the account and device settings and follow Kakao's official help guidance.

Know what KakaoTalk does not replace

KakaoTalk is a messaging app, not a guarantee that every Korean business or contact will use the same service. Some people may prefer a phone call, text message, email, or another app. Ask which method works for them. Transport, map, and payment services may have their own apps and sign-up requirements; having KakaoTalk does not automatically give you access to those services.

Before meeting someone, save the address and any important details somewhere easy to find. If you will be without internet access, agree on a backup way to contact each other. With the app installed, your account secured, and notifications set the way you like, KakaoTalk can make everyday communication in Korea much easier.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a Korean phone number to use KakaoTalk?

Not necessarily. Being in Korea does not automatically mean you need a Korean number, but signup or account verification may depend on your number and the options available to you. If you already have an account, check that you can log in and receive verification messages before your trip.

Can I use KakaoTalk over Wi-Fi while traveling?

Yes. KakaoTalk uses an internet connection, so you can send messages over Wi-Fi without relying on a Korean mobile data plan. Voice or video calls also need a suitable internet connection. If you leave Wi-Fi coverage, you will need mobile data or another way to connect.

Can I use KakaoTalk to contact Korean businesses?

Some businesses use KakaoTalk channels for inquiries or updates, but not every business offers this option. Search for the business by its name and check that the channel appears official before sharing personal details. For urgent questions, use the business’s other listed contact methods if available.

Visit Busan Pass 2026: Is the Busan Travel Pass Worth It?

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 26 October 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026

Visit Busan Pass 2026: 24H, 48H, BIG3 and BIG5 prices, which attractions are free, where to collect the card and when it beats paying at the gate.

Photo: bryan..., CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

The Visit Busan Pass is the city's official sightseeing pass for foreign visitors: one card or phone QR code that gets you into a set list of paid attractions without buying tickets at each gate. It can save money on a Haeundae-heavy trip and waste it on one built around beaches and markets. Here is how the four versions work and how to tell which one, if any, fits your plans.

The four passes and what they cost

There are two kinds of pass: time passes, which let you into as many listed places as you can reach before the clock runs out, and count passes, which give you a fixed number of entries over a much longer period. Approximate 2026 prices on the official site:

  • 24H: about ₩55,000. Unlimited listed attractions for 24 hours from the first one.
  • 48H: about ₩85,000. The same, for 48 hours.
  • BIG3: about ₩45,000. One attraction from Group A and two from Group B.
  • BIG5: about ₩65,000. Two from Group A and three from Group B.

Group A is the expensive end of the list: Busan X the Sky in Haeundae LCT, Lotte World Adventure Busan in Gijang, Skyline Luge Busan and the Busan City Tour Bus have all been in it. Group B is the cheaper end, with places like the Haeundae Blueline Park Beach Train, Songdo Marine Cable Car and Busan Tower in Yongdusan Park. Each attraction can be used once per pass, entry covers basic admission only, and the Blueline Park Sky Capsule (the little pod cars) is not on the free list. The pass is sold only to non-Korean passport holders, and staff may ask to see your passport.

Time pass or count pass?

The 24H and 48H clocks start when your first attraction ticket is issued, not when you buy, and they run in real hours. Start at 10:00 on Saturday and a 24H pass ends at 09:59 on Sunday. Most attractions close in the early evening, so a 24H pass you open at 15:00 gives you maybe three hours of use. Start it at opening time.

The BIG3 and BIG5 are more relaxed: they stay valid for 180 days after the first entry (within a year of purchase), so you can use one stop on Monday and the next on Thursday. For most first-timers with three or four days in Busan, a count pass is the safer buy, because a rainy morning or a long lunch at Jagalchi costs you nothing.

When the pass pays off

The pass pays off when you would have bought two or three of the big tickets anyway. Lotte World Adventure Busan on its own is one of the most expensive tickets in the city, so a BIG3 that includes it plus two Group B rides is close to a sure saving if you are going there. A Haeundae day that strings together Busan X the Sky, the Beach Train from Mipo to Cheongsapo, and the luge at Gijang suits a 24H pass well.

It does not pay off if your list is Gwangalli Beach, Gamcheon Culture Village, Huinnyeoul Culture Village, Haedong Yonggungsa Temple and the markets in Nampo. Those are free or cheap. Write down the attractions you would pay for, look up their gate prices on the official site or Naver Map, and add them up before you buy.

Plan by area, not by list

Busan is long and hilly, and the list spreads from Gijang in the north-east to Songdo in the south-west. Moving between the two ends takes over an hour on the Metro. Group your stops:

  • Haeundae and Gijang: Busan X the Sky (Haeundae Station, Line 2), the Beach Train from Mipo, Skyline Luge and Lotte World Adventure Busan (Osiria Station on the Donghae Line).
  • Nampo and Songdo: Busan Tower above BIFF Square (Nampo Station, Line 1), then a short taxi or bus to Songdo Beach for the cable car over the water.
  • Anywhere: the hop-on hop-off City Tour Bus starts near Busan Station.

Some venues ask you to book a time slot first, and several museums close on Mondays. If a stop needs a reservation, the official app tells you; do it the night before.

Buying, collecting and using it

Buy on the official VISIT BUSAN PASS website or app with a card issued outside Korea, or through a reseller such as Klook. Card and Mobile versions cost the same. Choose between them carefully:

  • Mobile PASS: a QR code in the app's MY PASS screen. Nothing to collect, but screenshots and printouts are not accepted, so keep your phone charged and your data or eSIM working. A mobile pass bought from a reseller cannot be added to MY PASS, so follow the reseller's own voucher instructions.
  • Card PASS: a physical card you collect with your voucher and passport. Pick-up points have included the counter on the second floor of Busan Station (KTX), the tourist information centre at Gimhae Airport arrivals, and Olive Young branches in Nampo and Gwangalli. Collect it before your first attraction.

At each place you show the pass at the ticket office (매표소, maepyoso) and they issue a normal ticket.

It is not a transport pass

No subway or bus rides are included. The Card PASS doubles as a rechargeable transport card: top it up with your own cash at a convenience store or a Metro ticket machine, then tap in on the Metro and buses as usual. Ask for 교통카드 충전 (gyotong kadeu chungjeon, "transport card top-up") at the till. That balance has nothing to do with your sightseeing entries. The Mobile PASS has no transport function, so you will still need a T-money card or similar; see the Korea transportation card guide.

Prices, the attraction list and pick-up points change during the year, so check the official site before you pay.

FAQ

Is the Visit Busan Pass worth it for one day in Busan?

Only if you start early and stay on one side of the city. A 24H pass used from opening time around Haeundae and Gijang can beat paying at each gate; one started in the afternoon rarely does. Our one-day Busan itinerary shows a route that does not need it.

Can I use the Visit Busan Pass on the subway?

Only the physical Card PASS, and only after you put your own money on it. No rides are included, and the Mobile PASS cannot be tapped on the Metro or buses at all.

Is it the same as the Discover Seoul Pass?

No. It is a separate pass run by Busan and works only in Busan. If you are also spending days in Seoul, read the Discover Seoul Pass guide and judge each city on its own list.

Seoul in December at Christmas: Lights, Markets and New Year's Eve

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 13 October 2026 · Updated 26 October 2026

Seoul in December at Christmas: where to see the lights and markets, a simple evening walk, Christmas Day in Korea and New Year's Eve at Bosingak.

Photo: Ken Eckert, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Seoul in December is cold, bright and festive. From late November the department stores light up their facades, Christmas markets open in the squares, and the month ends with a midnight bell in the old city centre. Christmas works a little differently in Korea, so this guide explains what to expect, where to see the lights, a simple evening route, and how to handle New Year's Eve. Christmas in Korea: what is different

Christmas Day (25 December) is a public holiday in Korea, but it feels more like Valentine's Day than a family holiday. Couples and groups of friends go out for dinner, walk around the lights and share a Christmas cake from a bakery. Families gather for Seollal and Chuseok instead, so you will not find the city shut down.

  • Shops and sights stay open. Malls, department stores, palaces, museums and most restaurants open as usual on 25 December, though some palaces close one weekday each week, so check before you go.
  • Christmas Eve is the big night out. Restaurants with a view, popular cafés and anywhere near the lights fill up from late afternoon.
  • Cakes are a thing. Bakery chains and hotel bakeries sell decorated Christmas cakes, and the popular ones are often pre-ordered. Walk into any bakery on the 24th and you will see the queue.
  • In 2026, Christmas falls on a Friday, so 25 to 27 December is a long weekend for locals. Expect busier trains out of Seoul and fuller restaurants all weekend. New Year's Day 2027 is also a Friday.

Where to see Christmas lights in Seoul

  • Myeongdong department stores. The Shinsegae main store and Lotte Department Store's main branch near Myeongdong turn their outside walls into huge light shows and video screens each winter. They have become one of Seoul's most photographed winter sights, and the pavement opposite gets crowded from about 6pm. See our Myeongdong street food guide for what to eat nearby.
  • Gwanghwamun Square. In recent winters the city has run a Christmas market and light displays here as part of its winter festival, usually from mid-December into early January.
  • Cheonggyecheon. The stream through central Seoul is usually lined with lights and lanterns in winter, and it is a pleasant, traffic-free walk. See our Cheonggyecheon guide.
  • Jamsil. The plaza by Lotte World Mall and Lotte World Tower has hosted a European-style Christmas market with a tall tree in recent years. Combine it with an evening at the theme park; see Lotte World or Everland.
  • The Hyundai Seoul in Yeouido. This mall has built an indoor Christmas village in recent winters. It is warm and very popular, and in some years entry has needed a timed booking through the store's app, so check ahead.
  • N Seoul Tower. Namsan is a good place to see the whole lit-up city on a clear, cold night.

Exact displays, markets and dates change every year and are usually announced in November. Check the Seoul city tourism website, and search the place's Korean name in Naver Map or KakaoMap for current details.

A simple Christmas lights walk

You can see the best of central Seoul's lights in one evening on foot, in about two hours with stops:

  1. Start at Myeongdong Station (Line 4) around dusk and eat your way through the street food stalls.
  2. Walk to the department store facades near Euljiro 1-ga Station (Line 2) and Hoehyeon for the light shows.
  3. Head north a few minutes to Cheonggyecheon and follow the stream west.
  4. Finish at Gwanghwamun Square (Gwanghwamun Station, Line 5) for the market and the view of the palace gate.

The whole route is roughly two kilometres. Pavements can be icy, so wear shoes with grip, and stop for a hot drink or street food like hotteok (sweet filled pancakes) and fish-cake skewers in broth to warm up.

New Year's Eve in Seoul

The biggest New Year's Eve event is the Bosingak bell ringing in Jongno, central Seoul. At midnight the historic bell is struck 33 times, and tens of thousands of people gather in the streets around it. If you go:

  • Arrive well before 11pm and dress very warmly; you will be standing outside for a long time, often below freezing.
  • Expect road closures. Jonggak Station (Line 1), right next to the bell, may be crowded or have trains passing through without stopping around midnight. Gwanghwamun or Jongno 3-ga can be easier.
  • Subway and bus services are usually extended past midnight on New Year's Eve; the details are announced each year.
  • For a calmer night, book a restaurant with a view or watch the countdown on TV from a cosy bar.

31 December is a normal working day in Korea; 1 January is a public holiday.

New Year's Day sunrise

Watching the first sunrise of the year is a popular tradition. People travel overnight to east coast beaches such as Jeongdongjin near Gangneung and Homigot in Pohang, or to Seongsan Ilchulbong on Jeju. In Seoul, hills such as Namsan and Achasan are popular and need only an early start. Trains and roads to the east coast are packed, so book early. Our New Year sunrise guide has times and spots.

The bigger family holiday comes later: Seollal, the Lunar New Year, which falls on 7 February in 2027. See our Seollal guide.

Tips for December in Seoul

  • Book early for hotels and dinners on 24, 25 and 31 December. Many restaurants offer set Christmas menus that need a reservation.
  • Dress for the cold. Daytime temperatures are often just above freezing and nights below it, with dry wind. A padded coat, hat, gloves and heat packs from a convenience store make evening light-watching far more pleasant. Our winter travel guide covers what to wear.
  • Go on a weekday if you can. The Myeongdong facades and markets are much calmer from Monday to Thursday than on Christmas weekend.
  • Use the subway. Taxis are hard to find in the evening crowds and roads around the lights get jammed.
  • Warm up indoors. Department stores and underground shopping arcades have winter sales and pop-up markets, and they are a good break from the cold.

FAQ

Is Christmas a holiday in Korea?

Yes, 25 December is a public holiday. It is mainly a day for couples and friends to go out, and most shops, restaurants and attractions stay open.

Is Seoul worth visiting in December for Christmas?

Yes, if you like crisp weather and festive lights and you dress warmly. The light displays, markets and street food make the evenings special, and sightseeing is quieter than in autumn apart from Christmas weekend and New Year's Eve.

What happens on New Year's Eve in Seoul?

The Bosingak bell in Jongno is rung 33 times at midnight in front of large crowds, and public transport usually runs later than normal. Many people also travel to the east coast to watch the first sunrise on 1 January.

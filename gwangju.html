Regional hub

Gwangju

The cultural capital of the southwest, known for its role in Korea's democracy movement, its art biennale and the generous Jeolla-style food.

Planning a trip to Gwangju

Getting there

The KTX runs from Seoul (Yongsan) to Gwangju Songjeong Station in about two hours.

How long to stay

One or two days for the Asia Culture Center, the May 18 memorial sites, Yangnim-dong's old houses and the food. Damyang's bamboo forest is an easy half-day trip.

Getting around

Gwangju has a subway line and a good bus network; taxis are inexpensive.

When to go

Spring and autumn are the most comfortable. Gwangju is known as one of Korea's best food cities at any time of year.

Where to stay

Stay downtown near the Asia Culture Center for walking to sights and restaurants, or near Songjeong Station for trains.

What to see first

Gwangju is the city of the May 18 Democratic Uprising of 1980. The May 18 National Cemetery and the memorial sites downtown tell that story, and the Asia Culture Center hosts exhibitions and performances. Yangnim-dong has early 20th-century missionary houses and the Penguin Village alley of folk art. Mudeungsan, a national park on the city's edge, offers rocky ridges and good hiking.

What to eat

The Jeolla region is known for generous tables, and Gwangju restaurants often serve many side dishes with every meal. Try tteokgalbi (grilled minced short rib patties), especially in nearby Songjeong or Damyang, and Gwangju's oritang, a hearty duck soup.

Good to know

Damyang's Juknokwon bamboo forest and its tree-lined Metasequoia road are an easy half-day trip by bus. The Gwangju Biennale, one of Asia's major contemporary art events, is usually held in the autumn of even-numbered years.

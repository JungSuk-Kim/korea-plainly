Useful Korean Phrases You Will Actually Use

Published 7 October 2026

The handful of Korean phrases you will really use in restaurants, shops and on the move, with simple pronunciation.

Korea Plainly · 6 min read

You can travel around Korea without speaking Korean, but a few polite phrases make every interaction warmer. The phrases below use the polite form, which is right for shops, restaurants and strangers of any age.

On this page

  1. Greetings and thanks
  2. Restaurants and cafes
  3. Shopping
  4. Getting around
  5. When you need help

Greetings and thanks

  • 안녕하세요 (an-nyeong-ha-se-yo) — Hello. Works at any time of day.
  • 감사합니다 (gam-sa-ham-ni-da) — Thank you.
  • 죄송합니다 (joe-song-ham-ni-da) — I'm sorry / Excuse me (for a mistake).
  • 저기요 (jeo-gi-yo) — Excuse me (to get someone's attention, for example a server).
  • 안녕히 계세요 (an-nyeong-hi gye-se-yo) — Goodbye, said when you are the one leaving.

Restaurants and cafes

  • 이거 주세요 (i-geo ju-se-yo) — This one, please. Point at the menu or picture.
  • 물 주세요 (mul ju-se-yo) — Water, please.
  • 계산할게요 (gye-san-hal-ge-yo) — I'd like to pay.
  • 안 맵게 해 주세요 (an maep-ge hae ju-se-yo) — Not spicy, please.
  • 포장해 주세요 (po-jang-hae ju-se-yo) — To go, please.

Shopping

  • 얼마예요? (eol-ma-ye-yo) — How much is it?
  • 카드 돼요? (ka-deu dwae-yo) — Can I pay by card?
  • 봉투 주세요 (bong-tu ju-se-yo) — A bag, please. Shops often charge a little for bags.

Getting around

  • ___ 어디예요? (___ eo-di-ye-yo) — Where is ___? For example 화장실 (hwa-jang-sil), restroom, or 역 (yeok), station.
  • 여기로 가 주세요 (yeo-gi-ro ga ju-se-yo) — Please take me here. Show the address on your phone to a taxi driver.
  • 여기서 내려요 (yeo-gi-seo nae-ryeo-yo) — I'll get off here.

When you need help

  • 도와주세요 (do-wa-ju-se-yo) — Please help me.
  • 영어 하세요? (yeong-eo ha-se-yo) — Do you speak English?
  • 천천히 말해 주세요 (cheon-cheon-hi mal-hae ju-se-yo) — Please speak slowly.

For emergencies, dial 112 for the police and 119 for fire and ambulance. The 1330 Korea travel hotline can help in English and other languages.

Tip: "주세요" (ju-se-yo, "please give me") after almost any noun gets you surprisingly far. Combined with pointing and a smile, it covers most everyday situations.

Kakao T and k.ride: Taxi Apps for Visitors to Korea

By Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 13 October 2026

Which taxi app works for visitors to Korea: Kakao T, k.ride, Uber and TABA compared, with fares, pickup tips and paying with a foreign card.

Korea Plainly · 5 min read

Photo: Ilya Plekhanov, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Kakao T is Korea's best-known taxi app, and most local taxis can be called through it. For visitors, though, the main Kakao T app can be hard to use, because it is built around Korean phone numbers and payment methods. The good news is that there are taxi apps made for visitors that work with a foreign phone number and a foreign card. This guide explains which one to use and how to get a smooth ride.

Kakao T or k.ride?

Kakao T is designed for people living in Korea. Signing up and adding a payment method usually requires Korean details, which many visitors don't have.

k.ride is Kakao Mobility's app for international visitors, launched in 2024. It uses the same large network of taxis as Kakao T, has an English interface, and lets you sign up with a foreign phone number and pay by foreign card. Its chat translates your messages for the driver, which helps when you are trying to agree on a pickup point.

Other options include Uber, which works in Korea with taxis but has fewer drivers outside central areas, and TABA, an app backed by the Seoul city government with English and other languages. Install one of these before you travel, and set it up while you are still on Wi-Fi.

How to book a taxi with an app

  1. Set your pickup point carefully. The app uses your location, but at big stations, malls and airports there can be several exits. Move the pin to the exact spot you are standing, or choose a nearby landmark or exit number.
  2. Enter your destination. Search in English, or paste the Korean address from your hotel booking for the most reliable result.
  3. Choose the taxi type. Regular taxis are the cheapest. Larger or premium options cost more.
  4. Check the car number. When a driver accepts, the app shows the car and its number plate. Check the plate before you get in.
  5. Pay through the app. With visitor apps your card is usually charged automatically at the end of the ride, so you just get out. Expressway tolls are added to the fare.

Fares and surcharges

Korean taxis use a meter. In Seoul the base fare for a regular taxi is ₩4,800, and the fare rises with distance and time. A late-night surcharge applies in the late evening and early morning, at its highest around midnight. App fees, premium taxis and busy-time pricing can add more, and the app shows an estimate before you book. Fares differ between cities and change from time to time, so treat these numbers as a guide.

Hailing a taxi on the street

You don't need an app. Taxis can be hailed on the street or found at taxi ranks outside stations and hotels. A lit sign on the roof means the taxi is free. To make the ride easy:

  • Show the driver your destination in Korean on your phone, or the name of a well-known landmark nearby.
  • Pay by card or with a transit card such as T-money; most taxis accept both, and many accept cash.
  • Fasten your seatbelt, including in the back seat.
  • Ask for a receipt (영수증, yeongsujeung) if you might need to contact the taxi company about lost property.

Tips for a smooth ride

  • Taxis are hard to find late on Friday and Saturday nights in busy areas, and during heavy rain. Allow extra time.
  • Cancelling after a driver has accepted may cost a fee.
  • Tipping is not expected.
  • For long trips, such as from Incheon Airport into Seoul, compare taxis with the train and airport buses in our Incheon Airport to Seoul guide.

FAQ

Can tourists use Kakao T?

The main Kakao T app is aimed at local users and usually needs Korean details. Most visitors find k.ride, Kakao's app for visitors, easier because it accepts foreign phone numbers and cards.

Do Korean taxi drivers speak English?

Some do, but many don't. Booking through an app or showing the address in Korean avoids most problems.

Can I pay for a Korean taxi with a foreign card?

Usually yes, both in the visitor apps and in the taxi itself. Have a backup, such as a T-money card or some cash, just in case.

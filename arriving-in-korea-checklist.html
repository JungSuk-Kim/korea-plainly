Arriving in Korea: A Checklist for Your First Day

Published 12 October 2026

Everything to sort out before you fly and in your first few hours after landing, in the order you will need it.

Korea Plainly · 8 min read

Photo: Maksym Kozlenko, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Korea is an easy country to arrive in, but a few things are much simpler if you sort them out before you board. Here is what to do, in order.

Before you fly

  • Check your entry requirements. Many nationalities can visit without a visa but need a K-ETA (electronic travel authorization) before departure. As of 2026, travellers from 22 countries are temporarily exempt from K-ETA until the end of December 2026. Check your own nationality on the official site, k-eta.go.kr, well before you travel.
  • Fill in the e-Arrival Card. Since January 2026 the paper arrival card is gone, and visitors without a valid K-ETA submit the free e-Arrival Card online at e-arrivalcard.go.kr, within the 72 hours before arrival. K-ETA holders do not need it. Avoid look-alike sites that charge a fee.
  • Sort out mobile data. Buy and install an eSIM at home, or reserve a SIM card or pocket Wi-Fi for airport pick-up. See Wi-Fi, SIM or eSIM.
  • Download the apps you will use. Naver Map or KakaoMap for directions (Google Maps is limited in Korea), a translation app, and a taxi app. See Naver Map vs Google Maps.
  • Save your accommodation address in Korean. Copy it from your booking or the hotel website. Taxi drivers and map apps work best with the Korean address.
  • Tell your bank you are travelling and check its fees for foreign card payments and ATM withdrawals.

At the airport

  • Immigration and customs: have your passport, accommodation address and return or onward ticket ready.
  • Get online: switch on your eSIM, or collect your SIM card or Wi-Fi router at the telecom counters in the arrivals hall.
  • Get some cash: withdraw a small amount from an airport ATM, or change a little at an exchange counter. Around ₩50,000–100,000 covers transit card top-ups and small purchases for the first few days. See Do you need cash in Korea?
  • Buy a transit card: convenience stores in the arrivals area sell T-money cards. Top it up with cash at the counter. One card per person. See How T-money works.

From Incheon Airport into Seoul

  • AREX train: the express train runs non-stop to Seoul Station in about 45 minutes; the cheaper all-stop train takes about an hour and connects with several subway lines, including Hongdae.
  • Airport limousine bus: slower in traffic but drops you close to many hotels, with space for luggage.
  • Taxi: the most direct option with heavy bags or late at night. Use the official taxi rank, or book with an app.

Your first evening

  • Find your nearest subway station and convenience store; both make the next days easier.
  • Check last-train times before going out. Seoul subway lines generally stop running around midnight.
  • Keep the emergency numbers handy: 112 for police, 119 for fire and ambulance, and the 1330 travel hotline for help in English and other languages.
  • Not sure what to do? See How to spend your first evening in Seoul.
Tip: Rules for entry change from time to time. Treat this page as a checklist, and confirm the details for your passport on the official K-ETA and e-Arrival Card sites before you fly.

Arriving in Korea: First-Day Checklist from Incheon Airport to Your Hotel

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 12 October 2026 · Updated 13 November 2026

What to do before you fly, at immigration and customs, and in your first hours in Korea: entry forms, data, cash, a T-money card and the trip into Seoul.

Korea Plainly · 8 min read

Photo: Maksym Kozlenko, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Landing in Korea for the first time is easier than most people expect: signs at Incheon Airport are in English, the trains are punctual and staff are used to confused, jet-lagged visitors. What slows people down is the small stuff: an entry form they did not know about, no data to open a map, no transit card, or an address they cannot show a taxi driver. This checklist puts everything in the order you will need it, from the week before you fly to your first evening.

Before you fly

  • Check your entry paperwork. Many nationalities can visit Korea without a visa, but most visa-free visitors need either an approved K-ETA (electronic travel authorisation) or the free e-Arrival Card. Citizens of a list of countries, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia and much of Europe, are temporarily exempt from K-ETA for arrivals until 31 December 2026; from 1 January 2027 they are expected to need one unless Korea extends the exemption again. Our Korea travel requirements guide explains each case. Check your own passport on k-eta.go.kr.
  • Submit the e-Arrival Card if you have no K-ETA. The paper arrival card has gone. Fill in the form at e-arrivalcard.go.kr within the 72 hours before you land. It is free: sites that charge a fee are agents, not the government.
  • Arrange mobile data. An eSIM installed at home works the moment you land. A physical SIM or pocket Wi-Fi can be collected at the telecom counters in arrivals; booking ahead is usually cheaper. See Korea eSIM, SIM card or Wi-Fi.
  • Download the apps. Naver Map or KakaoMap for walking and transit directions (Google Maps directions are limited in Korea), Papago for translation, and a taxi app such as Kakao T.
  • Save your accommodation address in Korean. Copy the Korean address from your booking confirmation into your notes. Map apps and taxi drivers find it far more reliably than an English hotel name.
  • Check your cards. Visa and Mastercard work almost everywhere; check your bank's foreign fees and bring a second card.

On the plane

Korea does not use a paper customs form for most travellers any more. If you carry nothing over the duty-free allowance (and no restricted items such as fresh fruit, meat or large amounts of cash), you simply walk through the green channel. If you do have something to declare, you declare it online or at the red channel; ask the cabin crew if you are unsure.

Landing at Incheon Airport

  1. Immigration. Follow the "Arrivals" signs to the foreigners' queue. Have your passport ready; most foreign visitors have their fingerprints and a photo taken at the desk. Officers may ask where you are staying and how long, so keep your address and return ticket to hand.
  2. Baggage and customs. Collect your bags from the carousel shown on the screens, then leave by the green or red channel.
  3. Arrivals hall. Here you will find tourist information desks with English-speaking staff, telecom counters, currency exchange, ATMs and convenience stores. Incheon has two terminals, and some airlines use Terminal 2; if you are meeting someone or catching a connecting flight, check which one, since moving between them takes a free shuttle bus or train.

Money, data and a transit card

  • Get online first. Switch on your eSIM or collect your SIM or router before anything else. You will need data for maps and translation.
  • Take out a little cash. Look for ATMs marked "Global" or with your card network's logo. Around ₩50,000 to ₩100,000 is plenty for the first few days: cards cover most spending, but you need cash to top up a transit card and at some market stalls. Airport exchange counters are convenient but usually give a weaker rate than banks or exchange shops in the city. More in Do you need cash in Korea?
  • Buy a transit card. Convenience stores in arrivals sell T-money cards; you top them up with cash at the counter or in station machines. Each traveller needs their own card. A WOWPASS combines a transit card with a prepaid payment card and is sold from machines at the airport. Full details in our T-money guide.

From Incheon Airport into Seoul

Incheon is about an hour from central Seoul. You have three main options, compared in detail in Incheon Airport to Seoul by AREX, bus or taxi.

  • AREX train. The Express runs non-stop to Seoul Station in roughly 45 to 50 minutes with reserved seats. The cheaper all-stop train takes about an hour, accepts T-money and connects with the subway at stations such as Hongik University (for Hongdae) and Gongdeok.
  • Airport limousine bus. Slower in traffic but stops near many hotels in Myeong-dong, Gangnam and Jamsil, with room under the bus for luggage. Buy tickets at the counters or machines in arrivals.
  • Taxi. Best with heavy bags, small children or a late arrival. Use the official taxi rank outside arrivals, or book through a taxi app. Show the driver your Korean address.

Landing late? The last trains leave before midnight, but some late-night buses run into Seoul after the trains stop. Check the current timetable on the airport website before you fly, and have a taxi as a backup.

Your first evening

  • Find your nearest subway station, its exit number and a convenience store.
  • Note the last train times for your line before you go out: most Seoul subway lines stop running around midnight.
  • Plugs are the round two-pin type (C/F) at 220 volts. Tap water is safe, though most locals drink filtered or bottled water. Tipping is not expected.
  • Save 112 (police), 119 (fire and ambulance) and 1330 (the tourist helpline, in English and other languages). Our pharmacies, hospitals and emergency numbers guide covers what to do if you feel unwell.
  • Keep the evening simple: a walk, a bowl of something hot and an early night. Ideas in How to spend your first evening in Seoul.
Tip: Entry rules, fares and timetables change. Treat this page as a checklist, confirm entry details for your passport on the official K-ETA and e-Arrival Card sites, and check routes on Naver Map or KakaoMap on the day.

FAQ

What do I need to do when I arrive in Korea?

Go through immigration with your passport (and an approved K-ETA or a submitted e-Arrival Card if you need one), collect your bags and walk through the customs channel. Then get online, take out a little cash and buy a T-money card before heading into the city.

Do I need Korean cash when I land?

Not much. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, including most taxis, but a small amount of cash is useful for topping up a T-money card and at markets. Airport ATMs accept most foreign cards.

What if my flight lands after midnight?

The trains will have stopped, so your choices are a late-night bus if one runs to your area, a taxi from the official rank, or a night at an airport hotel or capsule hotel. Decide before you fly and save the details offline.

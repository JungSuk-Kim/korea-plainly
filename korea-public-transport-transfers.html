How Korean Public Transport Transfers Work

By Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026

How Korea's transfer discounts work, why you must tap out of buses, and the small mistakes that cost visitors money.

Korea Plainly · 6 min read

Photo: Nesnad at en.wikipedia, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

In Seoul and the surrounding Gyeonggi and Incheon area, buses and the subway share one fare system. Pay with a transit card and a journey with changes counts as one trip: you pay a base fare plus distance, not a new fare every time you change.

On this page

  1. How a transfer works
  2. Always tap out of buses
  3. What you pay
  4. Common mistakes
  5. Outside Seoul

How a transfer works

  1. Tap your transit card when you board the first bus or enter the subway gate.
  2. Tap again when you get off the bus or leave the subway.
  3. Board your next bus or subway within the transfer window and tap in again.

In Seoul the window is about 30 minutes between getting off and getting on again, extended to about an hour late at night (roughly 9 pm to 7 am). Several transfers can be combined in one trip.

Always tap out of buses

On buses you tap the reader by the back door when you get off. If you forget, the system cannot calculate the trip, you lose the transfer discount, and your next ride may cost more. The reader will say "환승입니다" (hwan-seung-im-ni-da, "transfer") when a transfer is recognised.

What you pay

  • A base fare covers the first 10 km or so; longer trips add a small charge for each extra stretch.
  • As a guide, a basic Seoul subway ride with a card costs about ₩1,550 and a city bus about ₩1,500 (2026). Paying cash on a bus costs more and gives no transfer discount.
  • Express red buses, airport buses and a few private lines (such as the Shinbundang Line) charge more.

Common mistakes

  • Two people, one card. Each traveller needs their own card. Tapping one card twice charges a second fare, and transfers only work for the first person.
  • Leaving the station to look around. Stepping out of the gates ends the subway part of the trip. If you come back after the window closes, you pay a new fare.
  • Taking the same bus number again. Getting back on the same route is not treated as a transfer.
  • Low balance. Top up before a long trip; gates and readers refuse cards that cannot cover the fare.

Outside Seoul

Busan, Daegu, Gwangju, Daejeon and other cities also give transfer discounts with a transit card, but windows and fares differ. The habits are the same everywhere: one card per person, tap in, tap out.

Tip: Naver Map and KakaoMap show the fare and transfer points for a route, so you can see in advance how many changes a trip needs.

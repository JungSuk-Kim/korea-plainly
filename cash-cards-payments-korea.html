Do You Need Cash in Korea? Cards, ATMs and Payments

By Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026

Where your foreign card works, the few places that still want cash, and how to get Korean won without paying extra.

Korea Plainly · 6 min read

Photo: No machine-readable author provided. IGEL assumed (based on copyright claims)., CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Korea is one of the most card-friendly countries in the world. Most shops, cafes, restaurants, taxis and convenience stores take Visa and Mastercard, and many accept contactless payment. Still, a little cash saves trouble in a few situations.

On this page

  1. Where cards work well
  2. When cash still helps
  3. Getting Korean won
  4. Avoid paying extra
  5. Mobile payments

Where cards work well

  • Convenience stores, supermarkets, department stores and chain restaurants
  • Most cafes and sit-down restaurants, including small ones in big cities
  • Taxis, including those booked through apps
  • Hotels, museums, palaces and major attractions

When cash still helps

  • Topping up a transit card. Machines in subway stations and convenience store counters usually take cash only for top-ups.
  • Street food stalls and traditional markets. Many vendors take cards now, but some smaller stalls prefer cash.
  • Small rural guesthouses, temples and some local buses outside the big cities.

Around ₩50,000–100,000 in cash covers these situations for a few days for most travellers.

Getting Korean won

  • ATMs: look for machines marked "Global" or showing your card network's logo. Convenience store ATMs and bank ATMs in tourist areas are the easiest. See our guide to using Korean ATMs.
  • Exchange counters: airport counters are convenient for a small amount on arrival; licensed money changers in areas such as Myeongdong usually offer better rates.
  • Prepaid travel cards: several cards sold at the airport combine a transit card with a prepaid balance you load in foreign currency. Compare the rate and fees before choosing one.

Avoid paying extra

  • If a card machine or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, choose Korean won (KRW). Paying in your own currency usually comes with a worse exchange rate.
  • Check what your bank charges for foreign transactions and ATM withdrawals; one larger withdrawal often costs less than several small ones.
  • Tell your bank you are travelling if it blocks unusual foreign payments.

Mobile payments

Apple Pay works at many terminals that accept contactless cards. Local mobile payment apps such as Samsung Pay, Kakao Pay and Naver Pay are widely used by residents, but most of their features need a Korean bank account or phone number, so do not rely on them as a visitor.

Tip: Carry two cards from different banks or networks. If one is declined at an ATM or a shop, you have a backup straight away.

How Korean Recycling Works for Visitors

A practical introduction to recycling, food waste, and general rubbish in Korea — with the important rule that local instructions come first.

Korea has a detailed waste-sorting system, but visitors do not need to memorize every rule. The most important thing is to follow the instructions at the place where you are staying. Collection rules and bin arrangements can differ between cities, buildings, and accommodations.

Look at your accommodation first

If you are staying in a hotel, guesthouse, hostel, or apartment, check how the property wants guests to dispose of rubbish. Some accommodations have clearly labelled bins. Others may ask guests to leave rubbish in a specific place or may handle disposal themselves.

If the instructions are only in Korean, these words are useful:

  • 재활용 — recycling
  • 일반쓰레기 — general rubbish
  • 음식물쓰레기 — food waste
  • 종량제봉투 — designated general-rubbish bag

Common recycling categories

Depending on the local collection system, recyclables may be separated into categories such as paper, cardboard, plastic containers, cans, glass, and other packaging. Do not put everything with a recycling symbol into the same bin automatically. Read the label at the collection point.

  • Empty drinks and food containers before recycling them.
  • Rinse containers when they are dirty or contain food residue.
  • Separate lids or other parts when the local instructions ask you to.
  • Keep paper and cardboard as clean and dry as possible.
  • Do not put food-soiled rubbish into a clean recycling container.

Food waste is separate

Many residential areas separate food waste from general rubbish. If your accommodation has a food-waste bin, use it only according to its instructions. Some buildings use dedicated equipment or collection systems intended for residents.

Drain excess liquid from food scraps and keep packaging, plastic, and other non-food items out of the food-waste container. For items such as bones or shells, follow the local instructions rather than assuming one rule applies everywhere.

What is general rubbish?

Items that cannot be recycled or placed in the food-waste system normally go into general rubbish. In many residential areas, designated bags are used for this waste. The correct bag can depend on the local area, so visitors staying in an apartment should ask their host which bag and disposal point to use.

Do not leave a rubbish bag beside a collection area simply because you see other bags there. Your accommodation may have a specific place or collection time.

What should you do with batteries and electronics?

Batteries, small electronics, light bulbs, and other special items may have separate disposal arrangements. Do not put them into an ordinary recycling bin unless the local instructions specifically allow it.

For larger unwanted items, ask your accommodation or building manager about the correct disposal procedure instead of leaving them next to a rubbish area.

A simple routine for visitors

  1. Check the bins and instructions where you are staying.
  2. Empty food and drinks from containers before sorting them.
  3. Separate recyclable materials according to the local labels.
  4. Keep food waste separate when your accommodation provides a food-waste system.
  5. Ask before throwing away anything unusual or difficult to identify.
Bottom line: You do not need to learn Korea's entire waste system before your trip. Follow the signs at your accommodation, separate obvious recyclables, keep food waste separate when required, and ask when you are unsure.

FAQ

Do all parts of Korea use exactly the same recycling rules?

No. Local collection systems and building rules can differ, so the instructions at your accommodation should take priority.

Do I need a special rubbish bag?

Designated bags are commonly used for general household rubbish in Korea, but the exact requirement depends on the local area. Visitors should ask their host or accommodation.

Can I put all plastic into recycling?

No. Different types of plastic and packaging can be handled differently. Check the label at the collection point rather than relying only on the material.

How Korean Recycling Works for Visitors

A practical guide to sorting rubbish in Korea, from recycling and food scraps to local rubbish bags and hotel bins.

Recycling in Korea is part of everyday life, but the exact rules can vary by city, neighbourhood, apartment building, and accommodation. A bin setup in one place may not match the next. When in doubt, follow the signs where you are staying or ask your host, hotel desk, or building manager. A quick question is better than guessing.

Start with the bins where you are staying

Many homes and apartment buildings have separate collection points for recyclables, food waste, and general rubbish. Hotels, guesthouses, and serviced apartments may have their own arrangements. Look for labels on the bins or a posted guide; some instructions are in Korean only. If you cannot tell which bin to use, keep the item aside and ask staff rather than leaving it beside the bins.

These Korean labels may help:

  • 재활용 (jaehwalyong): recycling
  • 일반쓰레기 (ilban sseuregi): general rubbish
  • 음식물쓰레기 (eumsikmul sseuregi): food waste
  • 종량제봉투 (jongnyangje bongtu): designated general-rubbish bag

In some places, residents use designated bags for general rubbish. Bag requirements can depend on the area, so a bag sold for one neighbourhood may not be suitable in another. If you are staying in a private rental, ask your host whether you need a particular bag and where to put it. Do not assume an ordinary shopping bag is accepted for general rubbish.

Prepare recyclables before sorting

Common recycling categories include paper, plastic containers, cans, glass bottles, and some plastic film or bags. The categories and collection points vary, and packaging is not always recyclable just because it has a recycling symbol. Check the local signs and any instructions printed on the item.

  • Empty containers and remove leftover food or liquid. A quick rinse is useful when a container is sticky or dirty.
  • Separate packaging parts when the instructions ask you to—for example, a plastic lid from a bottle.
  • Flatten cardboard boxes and remove loose packing material when possible. Keep paper and cardboard clean and dry.
  • Put cans and bottles in the category shown at your building. Do not leave glass loose where it could break or injure someone.
  • Keep dirty, food-soiled items out of clean recycling. If you cannot clean an item or cannot identify its category, ask your accommodation how to dispose of it.

Plastic packaging can be confusing because rigid containers, wrappers, and soft film may be collected separately. Follow the labels at the collection point rather than putting every plastic-looking item in one bin. If a package has several materials, separate them when practical and when the local instructions allow it.

Food waste is usually separate

Many Korean homes and apartment buildings collect food waste separately from general rubbish. Use only the food-waste bin or disposal point identified by your accommodation. Some apartment systems have equipment or access procedures intended for residents, so do not use a machine or resident-only bin unless your host says it is okay.

Drain excess liquid before disposing of food scraps, and avoid putting packaging, cutlery, or other non-food items in the food-waste container. Bones, shells, tea bags, and other borderline items may be treated differently from place to place. Check the local guide instead of relying on a general rule. If your accommodation does not explain the system, ask staff where food scraps should go.

What about items that do not fit?

Batteries, small electronics, light bulbs, and other special items may have their own collection points. Do not put them into an ordinary recycling bin unless the local instructions say to. Ask your accommodation, a nearby community facility, or a shop that handles the item for guidance. Larger household items also have separate disposal procedures, so contact your host or building manager rather than leaving them by a rubbish area.

Public bins can be limited, and a street bin may not accept every kind of rubbish. When you are out, hold on to recyclables until you find an appropriate bin or return to your accommodation. Never leave bags beside a collection point unless a sign or your host specifically tells you to do so; collection areas often have set locations and instructions.

A simple routine for visitors

  1. Ask your host or hotel how to sort rubbish and where collection points are.
  2. Empty containers, separate materials where instructed, and keep paper clean and dry.
  3. Use the right food-waste container and follow any instructions for general-rubbish bags.
  4. Set rubbish out only at the place and time your accommodation recommends.
  5. If you are unsure, ask before throwing an item away.

You do not need to learn every local rule before arriving. The most useful habit is to check the signs at your accommodation and follow its system. Sorting carefully, keeping recyclables clean, and asking when something is unclear will help you dispose of everyday rubbish respectfully during your stay.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

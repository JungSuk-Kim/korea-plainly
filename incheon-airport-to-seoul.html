Incheon Airport to Seoul Station by AREX Train, Bus or Taxi

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 13 October 2026 · Updated 24 October 2026

Incheon Airport to Seoul Station and your hotel: AREX Express vs All-Stop train, airport buses and taxis, with 2026 fares and journey times.

Photo: calflier001, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Most first-time visitors land at Incheon International Airport, about 50 kilometres west of central Seoul on Yeongjong Island. Getting from Incheon Airport to Seoul Station is the easiest trip to plan, because the AREX airport train runs straight there, but Seoul Station is only the right stop if your hotel is nearby or on Line 1 or Line 4. Here is how to pick the option that leaves you closest to your bed with the fewest stairs and transfers.

The quick answer

  • Staying near Seoul Station, or flying home with Korean Air and similar airlines: the AREX Express Train.
  • Staying in Hongdae, Gongdeok, Yeouido or anywhere on the subway: the AREX All-Stop Train with a transit card.
  • Staying in Myeongdong, Gangnam, Jamsil or near a big hotel: check for an airport limousine bus that stops at the door.
  • Arriving late, in a group or with lots of luggage: a taxi from the official rank.

AREX Express Train: non-stop to Seoul Station

The Express Train runs without stops between the airport and Seoul Station. In 2026 the journey takes about 43 minutes from Terminal 1 and about 51 minutes from Terminal 2, with departures roughly every 30 to 40 minutes. Every passenger has an assigned seat, there is luggage space at the end of each carriage, and the adult fare at the station is about 11,000 won (2026).

Buy the ticket from the AREX machines or the counter in the Transportation Centre, which you reach by following the purple-and-blue "Train" signs from arrivals. The machines take cash and most foreign cards. The ticket is a QR code or a paper ticket, and it goes through its own Express gates: you cannot simply tap a T-money card here.

Seoul Station's AREX platforms are deep underground. Allow around ten minutes, with long escalators, to reach Line 1, Line 4 or the KTX concourse.

AREX All-Stop Train: cheaper and more useful than it sounds

The All-Stop (commuter) Train runs every few minutes and takes about an hour from Terminal 1 to Seoul Station, a little longer from Terminal 2. It costs roughly 4,000 to 5,000 won (2026) depending on the terminal, paid by tapping a T-money or similar transit card at the ordinary gates. You can buy and top up a card at the convenience store in arrivals; our transit card guide explains how.

The useful part is where it stops. Along with Seoul Station, it calls at:

  • Gimpo Airport for Line 5 and Line 9 (fast trains to Yeouido and Gangnam).
  • Digital Media City for Line 6 and the Gyeongui-Jungang Line.
  • Hongik University for Hongdae and Line 2.
  • Gongdeok for Line 5 and Line 6, handy for Mapo and Yeouido.

If your hotel is in Hongdae, the All-Stop Train is often faster door to door than the Express, because you skip the backtrack from Seoul Station. It fills with commuters near Seoul at rush hour, so keep suitcases out of the doorway. For changing lines after you arrive, see our Seoul subway guide.

Airport limousine buses: door to hotel without the stairs

Airport buses are the quiet favourite of locals with heavy bags. The driver loads your suitcase into the hold, you sit in a reclining seat, and many routes stop outside large hotels in Myeongdong, Jongno, Gangnam, Jamsil and other districts that are awkward to reach by train from the airport. Buses to Seoul mostly use four-digit route numbers starting with 6.

Ticket counters and machines are in the arrivals hall, and departures leave from numbered stops just outside on the same floor. Show your hotel name at the counter and ask which bus to take. The fare is more than the All-Stop Train and far less than a taxi, and some routes take a transit card or bank card on board, but check before you queue.

The weakness is traffic. Allow 70 to 90 minutes to central Seoul, more on Friday evenings and holiday weekends. If you have a timed booking, the train is safer.

Taxi: simplest, but the most expensive

Use the marked taxi rank outside arrivals and ignore anyone inside the building offering a ride. A regular metered taxi to central Seoul typically comes to roughly 60,000 to 80,000 won (2026) including the expressway toll, more late at night when a surcharge applies. Split four ways, a taxi can cost little more per person than the Express Train.

Have your hotel address in Korean ready to show the driver,. Taxi apps such as Kakao T also work at the airport if you set them up before the trip; see our guide to taxi apps in Korea.

Arriving late at night

The last trains leave the airport towards Seoul at around 11pm to midnight, the Express a little earlier than the All-Stop, and both start again around 5am. If your flight lands after about 10:30pm, look up the exact last departure for your terminal on the AREX website before you fly. A few late-night buses run to Seoul after the trains stop, but they are infrequent; a taxi is usually the simplest answer.

Going back: check in at Seoul Station

On the way home, Seoul Station's City Airport Terminal lets passengers on participating airlines (Korean Air among them) check in, drop their bags and clear immigration in the city, then ride the Express Train with only hand luggage. You need a same-day Express ticket, and check-in closes around three hours before departure. Check with your airline first.

Before you land: save your hotel's nearest station and address in Korean, load a transit card at the airport, and check which terminal you arrive at. It is printed on your ticket, and a free shuttle links the two. Our arrival checklist covers the rest.

FAQ

How long does it take from Incheon Airport to Seoul Station?

About 43 minutes by AREX Express Train from Terminal 1 and about 51 minutes from Terminal 2. The All-Stop Train takes about an hour from Terminal 1, a little more from Terminal 2.

Can I use a T-money card on the AREX train?

Yes on the All-Stop Train, where you tap in at the normal gates. The Express Train needs its own ticket from the machines or counter, although you can pay for it with cash or a card.

Is the Express Train worth the extra cost?

It is if you are staying near Seoul Station or want a guaranteed seat for your luggage. If your hotel is near Hongdae, Gongdeok or Gimpo Airport stations, the All-Stop Train is cheaper and gets you there without a transfer.

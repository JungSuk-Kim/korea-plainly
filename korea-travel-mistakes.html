Korea Travel Mistakes That Are Easy to Avoid

Published 7 October 2026

16 common Korea travel mistakes and how to avoid them, from relying on Google Maps and missing palace closing days to tipping and rubbish rules.

Korea Plainly · 5 min read

Photo: by Tom@HK, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Korea is an easy country to travel in, but a few habits from home can trip you up. None of these mistakes will ruin a trip, but each costs time, money or a little embarrassment. Here are the most common ones and how to avoid them.

Before you go

1. Not checking entry requirements early

Depending on your nationality, you may need a visa or a K-ETA (Korea Electronic Travel Authorization), and arrival paperwork has moved online. Rules and exemptions change, so check the official Korean government websites a few weeks before you fly rather than relying on an old blog post. Our arrival checklist covers what to sort out.

2. Relying only on Google Maps

Google Maps has long had limited walking and driving directions in Korea because of rules on exporting map data. Korea approved Google's request with conditions in 2026, but until full directions work, install Naver Map or KakaoMap before you arrive. See Naver Map vs Google Maps.

3. Forgetting the plug adapter

Korea uses 220V electricity with round two-pin plugs (types C and F). Bring an adapter, and check that your chargers accept 220V.

4. Travelling over a major holiday without knowing it

During Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (the autumn harvest festival), millions of people travel home. Trains and buses sell out, and many small restaurants and shops close for a few days. Palaces and big attractions often stay open. Check the dates before you book.

Getting around

5. Not getting a transit card

Buying single tickets is slower and costs more. A rechargeable transit card such as T-money works on subways, buses and many taxis, and gives transfer discounts. See our transit card guide.

6. Trying to see too much in one day

Seoul is big, and crossing it can take an hour. Group sights by area, as in our 3-day Seoul itinerary, and you will see more with less travelling.

7. Sitting in the priority seats

Seats at the ends of subway carriages are for older people, disabled passengers and pregnant women, and the pink seats in the middle are for pregnant women. Locals tend to leave them empty even when the train is busy.

8. Missing the last train

The subway stops running around midnight. After that, taxis are in high demand in nightlife areas. Check the last train time on your map app, or have a taxi app ready; see our taxi app guide.

Sightseeing

9. Turning up at a closed palace

Gyeongbokgung Palace closes on Tuesdays, and Changdeokgung and several other sights close on Mondays. Many museums close one day a week too. Check before you go.

10. Expecting to walk into everything

Popular experiences, such as the Changdeokgung Secret Garden tour and some restaurants, sell out or need booking. Book the few things that matter most to you in advance.

Eating and shopping

11. Arriving in the afternoon break

Many restaurants close between lunch and dinner for a break time, often for an hour or two in the afternoon. Eat lunch a little earlier, or check the hours on a map app.

12. Waiting for the bill at the table

In most Korean restaurants you pay at the counter by the door. Staff won't bring the bill unless asked. See how to order Korean food.

13. Carrying no cash at all

Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but traditional markets, street stalls and some small shops prefer cash. Keep a small amount with you. See our guide to cash, cards and payments.

14. Forgetting about tax refunds

Visitors can claim tax back on qualifying purchases at participating shops, and some refunds are taken off at the till. Carry your passport when you go shopping.

Everyday etiquette

15. Ignoring the rubbish rules

Public bins are scarce, and rubbish is sorted carefully. Carry a small bag for rubbish, and separate recyclables where bins are provided. See our recycling guide.

16. Tipping

Tipping isn't expected in restaurants, cafes or taxis, and can cause confusion. Good service is included.

FAQ

Does Google Maps work in Korea?

Partly. Public transport directions and place search work, but walking and driving directions have been limited. Use Naver Map or KakaoMap as well.

Is Korea expensive for tourists?

It depends on your style. Public transport, convenience store meals and local restaurants are good value, while hotels in central Seoul can be pricey.

Do I need cash in Korea?

A little. Cards are widely accepted, but markets and street food stalls often prefer cash.

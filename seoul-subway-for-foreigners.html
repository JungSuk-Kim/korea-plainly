Seoul Subway Map in English: Lines, Apps, Fares and Exits for Foreigners

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026

You only need Lines 2, 3 and 4, an app in English and three numbers: the station, the next stop and the exit. Here is how to ride the Seoul subway without a wrong turn.

Photo: LERK, CC BY 2.5, via Wikimedia Commons

Seoul's subway map has more than twenty lines, but a first trip uses a handful. Every station name is in English, every station has a number, and announcements come in English after the Korean. What trips visitors up is the wrong platform, the wrong branch or the wrong exit.

The lines you will actually use

Lines 1 to 9 are numbered and colour-coded on signs, floor arrows and trains. For a first visit these matter:

  • Line 2 (green) is the loop that links most of the places on a first itinerary: Hongik University (239) for Hongdae, City Hall (201), Dongdaemun History & Culture Park (205), Seongsu (211), Jamsil (216) for Lotte World and Gangnam (222).
  • Line 4 (sky blue) runs through Seoul Station (426), Myeongdong (424) and Dongdaemun.
  • Line 3 (orange) serves Gyeongbokgung (327), Anguk (328) for Bukchon and Insadong, and Express Bus Terminal (339) for buses out of Seoul.
  • Line 6 (brown) goes to Itaewon (630); Line 5 (purple) to Gwanghwamun (533) and Gimpo Airport.
  • Line 9 (gold) crosses the south bank from Gimpo Airport through Yeouido to the Gangnam area.
  • Named lines: AREX (light blue) from Incheon Airport to Seoul Station, Shinbundang (red) from Sinsa through Gangnam to Pangyo, and Gyeongui–Jungang (teal), whose trains come less often.

The three-digit station number is worth noting next to each stop in your plans. Checking for 205 on a platform sign is quicker than reading "Dongdaemun History & Culture Park". Interchanges have one number per line: Seoul Station is 133 on Line 1 and 426 on Line 4.

English subway map apps

The wall maps are in English, but a phone is far easier. Two apps cover everything:

  • Naver Map: set the language to English, type the place rather than the station, and it gives the line, direction, exit number and often the best door for a quick transfer (shown like "3-2": car 3, door 2). Our Naver Map vs Google Maps guide shows how to switch it to English.
  • Kakao Metro: a clean, zoomable map of the whole network with first and last trains for each station and direction.

Google Maps shows subway routes but its walking directions in Korea are poor, so treat it as a backup only.

Paying: T-money and fares

Buy a T-money card at any convenience store (CU, GS25, 7-Eleven) and top it up with cash there or at the yellow-and-blue reload machines in every station. A basic adult ride in Seoul cost about 1,550 won on a card in 2026, with a small extra charge for longer distances; Shinbundang and some airport routes add a surcharge on top. WOWPASS and the Korea Tour Card work the same way.

  • Tap out every time, including when you change to a bus: the fare is worked out by distance.
  • Transfers are cheap. Subway-to-bus or bus-to-subway within 30 minutes of tapping out (60 minutes late at night) is charged as one journey on distance.
  • Single journey tickets from the station machines include a 500-won deposit. Feed the card into the deposit refund machine (보증금 환급기) by the gates when you are done.
  • Unlimited rides. The Climate Card (기후동행카드) short-term pass only pays off if you ride many times a day.

Tapping a foreign Visa or Mastercard directly on the gate is not possible yet, so do not count on it.

Choosing the right platform

At many stations each direction has its own entrance on opposite sides of the road, and once through the gate you cannot cross without paying again. The sign above the stairs shows the next station and the final one, marked 방면 (bangmyeon, "towards"). Match the next station with your app.

  • Line 2 loop: signs say 내선순환 "inner circle" (clockwise) or 외선순환 "outer circle" (anticlockwise). Just check the next station. A train showing Kkachisan or Sinseol-dong is going down one of the two short branches.
  • Line 1 and Line 5 branches: Line 1 splits south of Guro (towards Incheon or towards Suwon and Cheonan), and Line 5 splits after Gangdong (towards Hanam or Macheon). Check the destination on the platform screen.
  • Line 9 express: trains marked 급행 (geuphaeng, express) skip most stations. They are fast between Gimpo Airport, Yeouido and Sinnonhyeon but packed at rush hour.

Transfers and exits

To change lines, follow the signs for 환승 (hwanseung, transfer) in the colour of your next line. Some are a few steps; others, like Line 5 to Line 9 at Gimpo Airport or Lines 1 and 4 at Seoul Station, are long walks. With a suitcase, follow the lift symbol and allow ten minutes.

Exits matter more than in most cities. Gangnam and Hongik University have around ten exits (출구, chulgu) each, coming up on different streets. Naver Map and most hotel websites give the exit number; yellow signs inside lead you to it.

Hours, crowds and manners

  • Trains run from roughly 5:30 am until around midnight. The last train (막차, makcha) differs by station and direction, and Kakao Metro shows it; after that, use a taxi.
  • Weekday rush hours (about 7:30-9:30 am and 6-8 pm) are packed on Line 2 near Gangnam and Sindorim and on Line 9 express. Move big luggage outside those times.
  • Seats at each end of the carriage (노약자석) are for older people, disabled passengers and pregnant women; the pink seats are for pregnant women. Leave both free even on an empty train.
  • Queue beside the doors, let people off first, keep calls quiet and do not eat on the train.

Arriving by plane? Our Incheon Airport to Seoul guide compares AREX with the airport buses, and the Seoul neighbourhoods guide shows which lines serve each hotel area.

Bottom line: Plan in Naver Map or Kakao Metro, note the station number, next station and exit, and tap out at the end.

FAQ

Is there an English Seoul subway map?

Yes. Station names, signs and wall maps are in English as well as Korean, and Kakao Metro and Naver Map both work in English. An app is easier to read than the wall map and includes new extensions.

How many subway lines does Seoul have, and which do tourists need?

Nine numbered lines plus over a dozen named ones. Most visitors only need Lines 2, 3 and 4, AREX from Incheon Airport, and sometimes Line 6 for Itaewon or Line 9 for Gimpo Airport and Gangnam.

Can I pay for the Seoul subway with my credit card?

Not by tapping a foreign card at the gate. Buy a T-money card or a tourist card such as WOWPASS and top it up with cash, or buy a single journey ticket from the station machine and get its 500-won deposit back afterwards.

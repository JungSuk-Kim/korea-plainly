Seoul Subway Map and How to Use It: A Guide for Foreigners

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 18 October 2026

The Seoul subway map looks complicated, but you only need a line, a direction and an exit. Here is how to read the map, pay, change lines and find your way out.

Reading the Seoul subway map

The Seoul subway map looks frightening the first time you see it on a station wall: more than twenty coloured lines, hundreds of dots and several lines that run far out into Gyeonggi-do and Incheon. The trick is that you never need to read the whole thing. You only need three pieces of information for any ride: the line, the direction and the station where you change.

  • Line numbers and colours. Lines 1 to 9 are numbered, and each has a fixed colour that is used on the map, on platform signs and on the floor arrows inside stations. Line 2 (green) is the loop through central Seoul, Line 4 (light blue) serves Myeongdong and Seoul Station, and Line 9 (gold) runs along the south of the Han River. Other lines have names instead of numbers, such as AREX (the airport railroad), Shinbundang, Gyeongui-Jungang and Suin-Bundang.
  • Station numbers. Every station has a three-digit code beside its name, for example 201 for City Hall on Line 2 or 424 for Myeongdong on Line 4. The first digit usually matches the line. If you cannot pronounce a station name, the number is an easy way to check you are in the right place.
  • Transfer stations. Stations where lines meet are drawn as larger white circles. Big ones such as Seoul Station, Jongno 3-ga and Express Bus Terminal connect three or more lines.

The best subway map apps

Route planning is much easier on a phone than on a wall map. Naver Map and KakaoMap both have English menus, show the subway lines in their real colours, and tell you which carriage door is closest to the exit or transfer you need. Subway apps such as Subway Korea show a clean, zoomable Seoul subway map. Google Maps shows subway routes but walks you badly, so keep it as a backup. Paying for the subway

The simplest way to pay is a rechargeable transit card such as T-money, which you can buy and top up with cash at convenience stores and at the machines in every station. Some travel cards and foreign-issued contactless bank cards are also accepted at the gates, but acceptance has been changing, so a T-money card with a few thousand won on it is the safest choice.

  • Fares. A basic adult ride in Seoul cost about 1,550 won with a card in 2026, with a little extra for longer trips. Paying with a card is cheaper than a single journey ticket.
  • Single journey tickets. The station machines (with an English button) sell a reusable card ticket that includes a small refundable deposit. Get the deposit back at the deposit refund machine near the gates when you finish.
  • Tap in and tap out. Always tap your card when you leave, not only when you enter. The fare depends on distance, and leaving without tapping can cost you more next time.

If you change from the subway to a bus within a short time, the transfer is discounted or free as long as you tap out of each vehicle. The details are in our guide to public transport transfers.

Finding the right platform

This is where most first-time mistakes happen. The same line runs in two directions, often from separate entrances on opposite sides of the road. Before you tap in, look at the signs above the stairs: they show the line colour and the next station or the end station in each direction. Compare that with the next stop on your app. At some stations the wrong gate means paying again to cross over.

Line 2 is the exception that confuses people: because it is a loop, the signs say "inner circle" (clockwise) and "outer circle" (anticlockwise). Just check the name of the next station instead.

Transfers and exits

Follow the coloured signs marked "Transfer" to change lines. Some transfers are a short walk across the platform; others, such as at Gimpo Airport or Gangnam-area interchanges, can take five to ten minutes through tunnels and stairs. Allow extra time if you have luggage, and look for lifts marked with a wheelchair symbol. On Line 9, express trains skip many stations, so check that yours is served.

Exit numbers matter more than in many cities. Large stations have ten or more exits that come out on different streets, sometimes several hundred metres apart. Your map app and many shop websites give the exit number ("Exit 8"), and yellow signs inside the station point the way.

Hours, crowds and etiquette

  • Trains usually run from about 5:30 in the morning until around midnight. Last trains vary by line and day, so check your app before a late night out.
  • Rush hours (roughly 7:30 to 9:30 and 6:00 to 8:00 pm on weekdays) are very crowded on Lines 2, 7 and 9. Travel outside these times with big suitcases if you can.
  • Let people off first, then board. Queue at the marked lines on the platform.
  • The seats at the ends of each carriage are for older passengers, pregnant women and people with disabilities. Pink seats are reserved for pregnant women. Leave them free even when the train is quiet.
  • Speak quietly, use headphones, and avoid eating on the train.

For journeys the subway does not cover well, the Seoul bus guide explains how the buses work, and our airport guide covers AREX from Incheon.

Bottom line: Use the Seoul subway map in an app, check the direction before you tap in, note your exit number, and tap out at the end. After a couple of rides it becomes the easiest way to see the city.

FAQ

Is there an English Seoul subway map?

Yes. Station maps and signs show station names in English as well as Korean, and Naver Map, KakaoMap and subway apps such as Subway Korea can be set to English. Saving the map on your phone is easier than reading the large wall maps.

Can I use the Seoul subway without speaking Korean?

Easily. Ticket machines, signs and announcements are in English, and station numbers help you check where you are. Plan the route in an app before you go, and ask station staff if you get lost.

How late does the Seoul subway run?

Most lines run until around midnight, with slightly earlier last trains on weekends and holidays on some lines. Times differ by station and direction, so check the last train in Naver Map or KakaoMap and use a taxi after that.

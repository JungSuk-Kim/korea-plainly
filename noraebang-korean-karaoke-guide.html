Noraebang: How Korean Karaoke Rooms Work

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 17 October 2026

A practical guide to visiting a Korean noraebang, from choosing a room and finding songs to handling the bill and following local etiquette.

A noraebang is a Korean karaoke venue where you sing in a private room, usually with friends or family. The word means “singing room.” Instead of performing in front of strangers, your group gets its own space, a screen, microphones, and a song-selection system. Some venues welcome walk-ins, and some let you reserve a room, so it is an easy activity to fit into an evening out.

Choose the kind of karaoke venue

Many noraebang venues rent a room to a group for a set amount of time. They are often found near busy shopping streets, restaurants, and nightlife areas. You may also see coin noraebang, where you pay for a short singing session, often by the song or in small blocks of time. These venues usually have compact rooms and can suit one or two people who want a quick turn. Payment methods and room arrangements vary, so check the signs or ask staff before you start.

Look for the word 노래방 on a sign. If you are unsure whether a venue is a private-room noraebang or a coin karaoke place, show staff your group size and ask whether a room is available. You can say “noraebang” or use a translation app. A little Korean is not required, but knowing how many people are in your group makes the conversation easier.

Getting a room and understanding the bill

At the entrance, tell the staff how many people are in your group and ask how much time is included. The venue may charge by the room, by the number of guests, or according to its own time packages. Do not assume that every place uses the same pricing system. Before heading to the room, confirm the total cost, how long you can stay, and whether food or drinks are included or ordered separately.

Some venues take payment at the counter before you sing; others may settle the bill afterward. Ask if you are not sure. When your time is nearly over, staff may offer more time if the room is available, but extensions are not guaranteed. If you have a reservation, arrive close to the agreed time and check in with staff. For a spontaneous visit, be ready to wait when the venue is busy.

Find a song and start singing

Most rooms have a songbook, a remote control, or a touchscreen system. Enter a title, artist, or song number to search. Some systems offer English menus or English-language song searches, while others work mainly in Korean. The available language options differ by venue. If the system is unfamiliar, ask staff for help or search for a song number online before your visit. Korean song titles may be easier to find in Hangul, but an English title or artist name can work too.

Choose a song from the list and use the control to add it to the queue. The order may be visible on screen, so check before adding another track. The system usually provides a microphone, and many rooms also have a second one. You can adjust the music and microphone volume with the controls. If the sound is too loud or a microphone does not work, ask staff rather than trying to change equipment settings you do not understand.

English-language songs are common in many song libraries, but the selection and how recently it is updated can vary. Korean pop, older Korean hits, and songs in other languages may also be available. A song’s lyrics often appear on screen, sometimes with a moving guide that helps you follow along. Some machines display a score after a performance. Treat it as a game rather than a measure of your singing ability.

Room etiquette

Noraebang is usually a social activity, so take turns and leave time for everyone to choose a song. You do not need to be a strong singer: joining in on the chorus or choosing a group favorite is perfectly fine. Tambourines may be provided for keeping time and cheering people on. Use them with consideration for the people in the room, especially if someone is singing a quieter song.

Keep the room tidy and treat the microphones and equipment carefully. Follow posted rules about food, drinks, smoking, and other items; policies can differ between venues. If you order refreshments, check how to do so and whether there is a service button or a phone in the room. Do not assume that every venue serves the same food or allows outside items.

If you are visiting with children or anyone who may be subject to venue restrictions, check the rules with staff before entering. Some venues may have limits based on age or time of day. When in doubt, ask rather than relying on a rule you heard about another karaoke place.

Tips for a comfortable visit

  • Decide on a few songs before you arrive, especially if the group has different language preferences.
  • Confirm the room time and total charge before you begin.
  • Keep an eye on the screen or clock so you know when your session is ending.
  • Ask staff for help with the song system, sound, or ordering. A translation app can make the request easier.
  • Check the venue’s rules instead of assuming that every noraebang works the same way.

For many visitors, a noraebang is a relaxed way to spend time with companions and experience a familiar part of Korean social life. Pick songs your group will enjoy, share the microphone, and do not worry about getting a perfect score. The main point is to have fun together.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if I don’t speak Korean?

Many noraebang rooms have a touchscreen or remote for choosing songs, but the language options differ by venue. Try searching by song title or artist, or show the staff the name written on your phone. If you need help using the controls, ask politely; staff may be able to guide you.

Will I sing in a private room or in front of strangers?

Noraebang usually means karaoke in a room reserved for your group, rather than singing on a public stage. Room types can vary, so check when you arrive if privacy matters to you. If you are visiting with friends, tell the staff how many people are in your group.

How do I know how long I can use the room?

The room time and payment arrangements can differ between venues. Before you start, confirm how much time is included and how to request more. Keep an eye on any timer or screen in the room, and ask staff if you are unsure whether your session is ending or how to settle the bill.

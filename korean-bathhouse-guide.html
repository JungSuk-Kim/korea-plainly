Jjimjilbang Guide: How to Visit a Korean Bathhouse

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 7 October 2026 · Updated 16 October 2026

First time at a Korean bathhouse or jjimjilbang? How a visit works step by step, bath etiquette, the body scrub, prices and sleeping over.

Korea Plainly · 5 min read

A Korean bathhouse is one of the most relaxing, and most local, things you can do on a trip to Korea. There are two kinds. A mogyoktang (목욕탕) is a neighbourhood bathhouse with hot and cold pools, showers and a sauna. A jjimjilbang (찜질방) adds a large shared lounge with heated rooms, snacks and places to rest, and many stay open late or around the clock. This guide walks you through a first visit so nothing comes as a surprise.

How a visit works, step by step

  1. Shoes off at the door. Put your shoes in a small locker by the entrance and keep the key.
  2. Pay at the front desk. Hand over your shoe key if asked. You pay an entry fee and usually receive a locker key on a wristband, a set of loose clothes (a T-shirt and shorts) and one or two small towels.
  3. Go to the bathing area for your gender. The bathing areas are separated by gender and bathing is done naked. Undress at your locker and leave everything there.
  4. Shower first, properly. Wash your whole body with soap at the shower stations before you get into any pool. This is the one rule everyone notices.
  5. Soak and rest. Move between the hot and cold pools and the sauna at your own pace. Drink water.
  6. Dress for the shared area. If you are in a jjimjilbang, put on the clothes you were given and go up to the shared lounge, where men, women and families mix.
  7. Pay extras on the way out. Snacks and services are often charged to your wristband and settled at the desk when you leave.

Bath area etiquette

  • Tie long hair up so it stays out of the water.
  • Keep your small towel out of the pools; people often fold it on their head.
  • Don't swim, splash or sit on the edge of the pool with your feet in for long periods.
  • Phones and cameras stay in the locker. Taking photos in the bathing area is not acceptable.
  • Rinse your stool and basin after using a shower station.

Nudity can feel awkward at first, but nobody is looking. Locals of every age bathe this way, and the atmosphere is matter-of-fact.

What's in the shared jjimjilbang area

The shared floor is where a jjimjilbang becomes a day out. You will usually find several heated rooms kept at different temperatures, sometimes lined with salt, clay, jade or charcoal, and often a cool room to recover in. There are open floors with mats and wooden headrests for lying down, TV areas, and a snack bar. Many places also have a small restaurant, a PC or reading corner and massage chairs.

Two snacks are a tradition: sikhye (식혜), a cold, sweet rice drink, and maekbanseok gyeran, eggs baked slowly until the whites turn brown. Try them at least once.

The body scrub

Many bathhouses offer a full-body scrub (세신, seshin) in the bathing area, done by a staff member with a coarse scrubbing mitt. It is vigorous and leaves your skin very smooth. Book it at the counter inside the bathing area; prices are usually posted on the wall. Soak in a hot pool for at least 15 to 20 minutes first so the scrub works properly. You can also buy a scrubbing mitt and do it yourself.

Prices and opening hours

A neighbourhood bathhouse is inexpensive, and a jjimjilbang costs a little more. Large spa complexes such as SPA LAND Centum City in Busan cost more again but are bigger and more polished. Some jjimjilbang charge extra after a certain hour for an overnight stay. Prices, hours and night rules change, so check the board at the entrance or the place's listing on Naver Map.

Can you sleep in a jjimjilbang?

Some travellers do, and many jjimjilbang open 24 hours. It is cheap, but expect lights, snoring and people coming and going, and only a mat on the floor. Keep valuables in your locker. Treat it as a back-up or an adventure, not a substitute for a hotel. Some places don't let minors stay overnight without a guardian.

Tips for first-timers

  • Bring a hair tie and your own toiletries if you are particular. Basic soap and shampoo are usually provided, but not always.
  • Bigger towels may cost extra. The small towels you are given are normal.
  • Rules on tattoos vary by place. Ask at the desk if you are unsure.
  • If you have a heart condition or high blood pressure, or are pregnant, take it easy in the hot rooms and pools.
  • Go on a weekday if you can. Weekends and holidays are busy, especially with families.

FAQ

Do I have to be naked?

In the bathing area, yes. Swimwear is not worn in a traditional Korean bathhouse. In the shared jjimjilbang area everyone wears the clothes provided.

Can couples or families go together?

Yes. You will separate for the bathing areas and meet again in the shared jjimjilbang area.

Is it OK to visit if I don't speak Korean?

Yes. The process is simple, and pointing and copying what others do works fine. Staff are used to visitors at the bigger places.

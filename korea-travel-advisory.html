Korea Travel Advisory: Safety and Official Updates

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 13 November 2026

Know where to check official safety notices in Korea, how to get help in an emergency, and what to do during severe weather, crowded events, or travel disruptions.

South Korea is straightforward to travel around, but conditions can change quickly during heavy rain, major events, or transport disruptions. A travel advisory is a reason to check the details and plan around them—not automatically a reason to cancel a visit. Use official Korean sources for local updates, and your own government’s embassy or consular service for advice specific to your nationality.

Where to check official updates

For disaster alerts and practical safety information, look for the Emergency Ready App, published by Korea’s Ministry of the Interior and Safety. It offers information in English, including emergency alerts and locations of evacuation shelters. Search for the exact app name and confirm the publisher before downloading. Phone alerts may arrive in Korean, so ask a hotel receptionist or a Korean-speaking companion to explain an urgent message if needed.

For weather warnings, use the Korea Meteorological Administration at weather.go.kr. For air-quality readings, check AirKorea at airkorea.or.kr; its readings can help you decide whether to spend a day outdoors in places such as Seoul Forest or along the Han River. The Korea Tourism Organization’s 1330 Travel Helpline can help with visitor questions in several languages. It is not an emergency number. For embassy notices, use your country’s official website or verified embassy account rather than reposted screenshots or unofficial social-media claims.

What to do in an emergency

Call 112 for police and 119 for fire or an ambulance. These are Korea’s main emergency numbers. Tell the operator the district, nearest subway station, street name, or a visible landmark. If you are in a large complex, give the building name and floor. For example, “Exit 3 of Hongik University Station, near the main road” is more useful than saying only “Hongdae.” If you cannot explain the location in Korean, say “English, please” and show the address on your phone.

For non-urgent visitor questions, call 1330 from within Korea. If you are hurt or in immediate danger, call 119 or 112 first, not the tourism line. At a subway station, alert a station employee or use the help intercom on the platform. On Seoul Metro, staff are usually near the gates or customer-service office; do not enter the tracks to retrieve a dropped item.

Weather, earthquakes, and air quality

Check the forecast before day trips to places exposed to weather, such as Namsan, Bukhansan National Park, or the coast in Busan. Heavy rain can flood low-lying roads and underpasses, while strong winds or snow can affect flights, ferries, and mountain paths. Follow official warnings and obey barriers. Do not walk through floodwater or enter a closed trail because a map still shows it as a route.

If an earthquake happens, drop, cover, and hold on; move away from windows and objects that could fall. Once shaking stops, follow local instructions and use the Emergency Ready App to look for designated shelters. For poor air quality, AirKorea provides readings by area. If levels are high, shorten outdoor exercise and consider a mask; a Naver Map or KakaoMap route through underground shopping areas can reduce time outside, though it may add walking.

Crowds, demonstrations, and transport changes

Large gatherings can close streets or make a familiar station exit difficult to use. In Seoul, check for notices around Gwanghwamun, City Hall, Itaewon, and Hongdae before heading out, especially on weekends or public-holiday evenings. Keep clear of police lines and do not try to cross a packed crowd against its movement. If an area feels too congested, leave by a side street and agree on a meeting point away from the crowd.

For subway delays, check notices from the operator or ask station staff before changing plans. A disruption on Line 2 can affect a journey through Hongik University Station or Gangnam, while Line 1 connects central Seoul with major rail hubs. Naver Map and KakaoMap are useful for comparing routes, but official station announcements take priority during an incident. If you are separated from your group, send the name of the station and exit number—not just a pin that may be hard to open.

Health and everyday safety

For a medical emergency, use 119. For a less urgent illness, ask your hotel to help locate a nearby clinic or hospital; show the staff your symptoms in a translation app if necessary. Carry your passport or a photo of its identity page and any important medication information. If you have a serious allergy, write it in Korean as well as English. Common menu words include “매운” (spicy) and “땅콩” (peanut), but check with restaurant staff rather than relying on a dish name alone.

Use marked pedestrian crossings and look both ways: scooters and delivery motorcycles may approach from directions you do not expect, including along narrow streets in Myeong-dong or Seongsu. Keep bags and phones secure in busy markets and on crowded trains, but do not assume every theft report online describes a current trend. If an offer, payment request, or safety claim seems suspicious, verify it with your accommodation or an official service.

A simple check before you go out

  • Check the KMA forecast and any official disaster alerts before a hike, coastal trip, or long outdoor day.
  • Save your accommodation’s Korean name and address, plus the nearest station and exit.
  • Remember 112 for police and 119 for fire or ambulance; use 1330 for visitor information, not emergencies.
  • During a major event or disruption, follow official instructions and station staff, even if a map app suggests another route.

For changing local notices, check current details on Naver Map or KakaoMap and the relevant official site; use the map for route planning, not as a substitute for emergency instructions.

Bottom line: Save 112 for police and 119 for fire or ambulance, and keep your accommodation address in Korean on your phone. Check official weather and disaster alerts before outdoor trips, and follow station staff or local instructions during disruptions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find reliable safety updates while traveling in Korea?

Check official travel advisories from your home country and updates from Korean government or local authorities. Your embassy or consulate may also share alerts for citizens abroad. For immediate local conditions, follow instructions from emergency services and other responsible authorities rather than relying only on social media or forwarded messages.

What should I do if a safety advisory changes during my trip?

Read the full update and check when it was issued, which area it covers, and what actions it recommends. Follow directions from local authorities, and contact your airline, accommodation, or tour provider if your plans may be affected. Keep your embassy’s contact details available in case you need assistance.

Who should I contact if I feel unsafe or need urgent help?

For an immediate emergency, contact the appropriate local emergency service and follow instructions from responders. If you need help as a visitor, your hotel staff or tour operator may be able to assist with communication and next steps. For consular support, contact your country’s embassy or consulate in Korea.

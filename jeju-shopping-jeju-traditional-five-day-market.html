Jeju Traditional Five-Day Market: A taste of local life

What the market is

Jeju Traditional Five-Day Market is a large traditional market in Jeju City, at 26 Oiljangseo-gil. Its name refers to the repeating market-day schedule: the main market days fall on dates ending in 2 or 7, such as the 2nd, 7th, 12th and 17th of the month. Check the calendar before you go so your visit lines up with a market day.

On those days, local residents and visitors come to shop, eat and catch up amid rows of stalls. The scene is quite different from a polished shopping mall: expect busy walkways, sellers calling out, and a mix of shopping and food. It offers a glimpse of everyday commerce on Jeju, as well as the chance to browse goods from around the island.

Stalls typically cover fresh produce, seafood, snacks, clothing and household items, though the selection changes with the season and the day. The market is a good fit if you enjoy browsing without a fixed shopping list. Google rates it 4.2 out of 5 from 6,988 reviews.

What to see, do and try

Take a slow lap before buying anything. The variety is part of the experience: look at the produce, notice what shoppers are choosing, and see how the food stalls fit into the market’s rhythm. You do not need to speak Korean to browse. A friendly smile, a point, and a number typed into a phone calculator can help with simple purchases.

Food is an easy reason to pause. Browse what is being cooked or served on the day, and choose a stall that looks appealing and busy. Market menus and offerings can change, so treat the visit as an opportunity to discover what is available rather than arriving with one specific dish in mind. If you are unsure what something is, a translation app can help you ask.

Look beyond edible goods, too. Clothing and practical household items make the market feel like a place where people shop for daily needs, not just a visitor attraction. If you plan to buy fresh food, think about whether you can store or carry it comfortably for the rest of your day.

How to plan your visit

First, choose a date that ends in 2 or 7. A visit on another date may not give you the full five-day market experience, so check the schedule before setting out. The busiest, liveliest atmosphere is naturally on market days. Arriving earlier in the day is a sensible choice if you want to browse before the walkways become more crowded; later visits can suit you if you prefer a more relaxed pace, but stall activity varies.

Allow around one to two hours for a first visit. You can spend less time on a quick walk-through or longer if you want to eat and browse several sections. Wear comfortable shoes, keep your belongings close in busy areas, and bring a reusable bag if you expect to shop. Carrying some cash is useful for small purchases, though payment options vary from stall to stall.

The market works well as part of a Jeju City day. Pair it with a visit to Yongduam Rock or the Jeju Folklore and Natural History Museum, then plan your route around the market date and the other places you want to see. If you also want a second market experience, Dongmun Traditional Market offers another chance to browse in the city. Avoid trying to fit too much in: the market is most enjoyable when you have time to wander and stop for a bite.

Good to know

Market-day crowds can make the walkways feel tight, so keep bags close and step aside before stopping to take photos. Ask stallholders before photographing them or their goods. Bring a translation app if you want help reading signs or asking about an item, and remember that stallholders may be busy serving customers. Check current hours on Naver Map before you go.

FAQ

Which days does Jeju Traditional Five-Day Market take place?

The recurring market days fall on dates ending in 2 or 7, including the 2nd, 7th, 12th, 17th, 22nd and 27th of the month.

How long should I spend at the market?

Plan for around one to two hours to browse at a comfortable pace and stop for food. A quick walk-through can take less time.

Can I visit if I do not speak Korean?

Yes. You can browse without speaking Korean. A translation app, a friendly gesture and a calculator can help with questions and simple purchases.

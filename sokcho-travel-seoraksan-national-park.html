Seoraksan National Park: Sokcho’s mountain gateway

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 14 October 2026

Photo: Ayan Bardhan via Google Maps

At a glance: Seoraksan National Park’s Seorak-dong district is a scenic mountain gateway, with temple grounds, hiking trails and access to dramatic peaks.

What Seorak-dong is

Seoraksan National Park covers a vast stretch of mountains in northeastern Korea, and Seorak-dong is its best-known entrance area on the Sokcho side. Come here for more than a quick photo stop: the district brings together forest paths, Buddhist heritage and access to some of the park’s celebrated rocky scenery. The park has a Google rating of 4.6 from 10,052 reviews.

Seorak-dong is a convenient choice if you are staying in Sokcho and want to experience the mountains without planning a long-distance trek across the park. The main visitor area is commonly called Sogongwon. From here, paths lead toward Sinheungsa temple and onward into the mountains; the cable car also departs from this area when it is operating.

What to see and do

Make time for Sinheungsa, a historic Buddhist temple set among the wooded foothills. The approach passes the large bronze statue known as Tongildaebul, or the Great Unification Buddha. You do not need to be a hiker to enjoy this part of the park: the temple grounds and surrounding mountain views make a rewarding visit in their own right. Walk quietly around the temple, and follow any signs about visitor etiquette or areas reserved for worship.

For a broad view without committing to a full-day climb, the Seoraksan cable car travels from Sogongwon toward the Gwongeumseong area. From the upper station, paths lead to rocky viewpoints over the surrounding peaks. The ride is popular, and operation can be affected by weather, so treat it as one possible part of your day rather than the entire plan. Wear shoes with a good grip if you plan to walk among the rocks.

If you would rather explore on foot, choose a trail that matches your fitness and available time. Paths toward Biryong Falls offer a forest-and-waterfall outing, while the route toward Ulsanbawi is a much more demanding mountain hike, with a steep climb and stair sections. Check trail signs before setting out, and do not start a challenging route late in the day. Seoraksan’s scenery changes quickly as you gain height, but mountain paths require more care than a stroll through the entrance area.

How to plan your visit

Allow at least a few hours for the temple, the main visitor area and an easy walk. A cable-car outing or a longer hike can turn the visit into a half-day or full-day plan. Decide on your main activity before arriving: combining a strenuous hike, a cable-car ride and a relaxed temple visit may make for a rushed day, especially when the park is busy.

Morning is a good choice if you want a quieter start and more room to adjust your plans. Autumn brings vivid foliage and is especially popular, so expect heavier crowds then, particularly on weekends. Spring and summer offer lush forest scenery, while winter brings a different, often snow-covered mountain landscape. Conditions vary by season, so dress for the weather and be prepared for cooler temperatures higher up.

Seorak-dong pairs naturally with other Sokcho stops. Spend the morning in the park, then head back toward the city for the coast, a beach walk or a visit to Abai Village. If you have more time, make Sokcho your base for exploring the wider area rather than trying to fit every attraction into the same day.

Good to know

Bring water, comfortable walking shoes and a light extra layer; even a warm day in town can feel cooler in the mountains. Stay on marked paths, take your rubbish with you and follow signs about trail closures or wildlife. If you are hiking, tell someone your plan and turn back if weather or energy levels change. The cable car may stop in unsuitable weather, so check its current operating notices before building your itinerary around the ride.

The Seorak-dong entrance is in Sokcho, at Seorak-dong, Sokcho-si, Gangwon-do. You can reach the area from Sokcho by local transport or taxi; allow extra time on busy foliage-season days. If you are driving, follow signs for the park and use the designated parking areas. Arriving earlier can make it easier to settle in before the busiest part of the day.

FAQ

Can I visit Seoraksan without hiking?

Yes. You can enjoy the Sogongwon visitor area, walk to Sinheungsa and take in the mountain scenery without attempting a long trail. The cable car is another option when it is operating.

How much time should I set aside?

Plan a few hours for a relaxed visit to the entrance area and temple. Add more time for the cable car or a longer hike, and consider a full day if you want to explore at an easy pace.

Is Seorak-dong a good base for visiting Seoraksan?

Yes. It is the main gateway on the Sokcho side, with access to the temple, mountain trails and cable car. Staying in Sokcho also makes it easy to combine the park with coastal sights around the city.

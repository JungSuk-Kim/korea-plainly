What Kampungku is

Kampungku Restaurant brings halal dining to the busy Myeongdong area, a convenient choice if you want a meal that fits halal dietary requirements while exploring central Seoul. It is identified as a Malaysian restaurant, and Korean fried chicken is also among the dishes recommended here. That mix gives you the option of seeking familiar Malaysian flavours or trying a popular Korean-style meal in a halal setting.

The restaurant is at 16-4 Namsan-dong 2(i)-ga, Jung District, Seoul, in the wider Myeongdong neighbourhood. Myeongdong is known for shopping streets, food stops and easy access to other central Seoul sights, so Kampungku can fit naturally into a day out rather than needing a special trip across the city. Its Google rating is 4.7 from 3,909 reviews.

For first-time visitors, a halal restaurant can make a busy sightseeing day easier to plan. Korean menus often feature meat, seafood and sauces in combinations that may be difficult to judge at a glance. Choosing a restaurant that serves halal food gives you a clearer starting point; if you have particular dietary needs, explain them to the staff when ordering.

What to try

Look to the Malaysian side of the menu if you want food that connects with the restaurant’s roots. If Korean flavours are what you came to Seoul for, Korean fried chicken is another dish to consider. It offers a way to try a familiar part of Korean food culture while dining at a halal restaurant.

If you are visiting with other people, compare menu choices before ordering so everyone can find a meal that suits them. If you are unfamiliar with a dish, ask the staff what it includes and whether it is served with rice or other sides. This is a useful habit in Korea, where menu descriptions may be brief or written mainly in Korean. You do not need to know the name of every dish in advance: pointing to a menu item or showing its name on your phone can help.

Many Korean meals are served with side dishes, and rice is a common accompaniment, though what comes with your order depends on the dish. Take a moment to check how a meal is presented before adding anything from a shared table. When you have a strict dietary requirement, ask before accepting sauces or extra sides rather than assuming they are included in the halal offering.

How to plan your visit

Allow enough time to sit down and eat rather than treating the restaurant as a quick stop between attractions. A meal can be a welcome pause after walking through Myeongdong’s shopping streets, especially if you have spent the day browsing shops or exploring central Seoul. Going during a break in your sightseeing also gives you time to decide what to order without feeling rushed.

Kampungku works well as part of a Myeongdong day: explore the neighbourhood, stop for a meal, then continue towards another central Seoul destination. You could pair lunch or dinner with shopping in Myeongdong, or make the meal part of an outing around Namsan. The area is also a useful base for connecting different parts of central Seoul, so consider your next stop before setting out.

Use the street address in your map app to navigate, and check the route from your starting point before you leave. Myeongdong’s busy streets and smaller roads can make it easier to follow a pin than to rely on a verbal description. If you are meeting friends, share the address with them so everyone has the same destination.

Good to know

Halal dining is the main reason to choose Kampungku, but it is also a practical option if your group wants Malaysian food or is curious about Korean fried chicken. If members of your party have different preferences, check the menu together and ask about ingredients or preparation where needed. Dietary standards can matter in specific ways, so be clear about what you can and cannot eat.

Bring the restaurant name and address up on your phone before you start walking, and keep the address handy in case you need to ask for directions. For current hours or prices, check Naver Map before you go.

A festival calendar for Korea, updated every week: what is on now and coming up across the country, from food and lantern festivals to fireworks, flowers and ice fishing.

Photo: Benjamin Krause, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Korea holds hundreds of festivals a year, from huge fireworks shows and lantern festivals to small-town food fairs, flower festivals and ice fishing on frozen rivers. This calendar lists what is on now and coming up across the country, and it is updated every week from the Korea Tourism Organization's official listings. Korea's festival seasons

  • Spring (March to May): cherry blossom and flower festivals, and Buddha's Birthday lantern parades. See our cherry blossom guide.
  • Summer (June to August): beach and mud festivals, music festivals and night markets.
  • Autumn (September to November): the busiest season, with food, lantern, fireworks and autumn leaf festivals. See Korea in November and our autumn foliage guide.
  • Winter (December to February): lights, Christmas markets, ice fishing and snow festivals. See our winter festivals guide and Korea in December.

Tips for festival days

  • Check the dates before you go. Festivals can be postponed or cancelled because of weather or other events, and programmes change from year to year.
  • Go early or on a weekday. Big festivals get very crowded at weekends and in the evening.
  • Use public transport. Roads and car parks near big festivals fill up; extra buses or trains often run. See our transit card guide.
  • Bring some cash for food stalls, though cards work in most places. See cash and cards.
  • Use a Korean map app for routes. See Naver Map vs Google Maps.

FAQ

Where does this festival list come from?

From the Korea Tourism Organization's official tourism data, which we check every week. Always confirm dates on the festival's own page before you travel.

When is the best time for festivals in Korea?

Autumn has the most festivals, followed by spring. Winter is the season for lights, ice fishing and snow festivals.

Are Korean festivals free?

Many are free to enter, especially street, lantern and food festivals. Some activities, such as ice fishing or paid seats at fireworks shows, cost extra.

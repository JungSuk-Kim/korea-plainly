Korea Shopping Districts: Where to Shop in Seoul on Your First Trip

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 28 October 2026 · Updated 22 November 2026

Seoul’s shopping districts compared: Myeongdong for K-beauty, Seongsu for pop-ups, Hongdae, Gangnam’s underground malls, Dongdaemun and the markets.

Photo: Republic of Korea from Seoul, Republic of Korea, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Seoul has no single shopping centre. Each district sells something different, so the trick is to pick by what you want to bring home, then stay in one part of the city for half a day. This is how the main areas compare, which station and exit to use, and the small habits that make shopping here easier.

Quick pick: which district for what

  • K-beauty and skincare: Myeongdong, or Olive Young N Seongsu in Seongsu-dong.
  • Korean fashion labels and pop-ups: Seongsu-dong, then Hongdae.
  • Cheap clothes, phone cases and accessories: the Gangnam underground arcades and Hongdae.
  • Malls and late shopping: Dongdaemun, COEX in Samseong, The Hyundai Seoul in Yeouido.
  • Everyday goods and market food: Namdaemun Market.
  • Traditional gifts, tea and ceramics: Insadong and Ikseon-dong.

Myeongdong: K-beauty and department stores

Take Line 4 to Myeong-dong Station (exits 5 to 8 open onto the shopping streets) or Line 2 to Euljiro 1-ga. Within a few blocks you have Olive Young’s large Myeongdong stores, almost every Korean cosmetics brand, the Lotte Department Store main branch with Lotte Duty Free upstairs, and Shinsegae’s main store towards Hoehyeon.

The street-food carts set up from late afternoon: gyeranppang (egg bread), hotteok, tornado potatoes and grilled cheese lobster tails. Compare prices on sunscreen and sheet masks between Olive Young and the brand shops, because brand shops often run “1+1” (buy one, get one) offers. Our Olive Young guide covers what to buy, and the Myeongdong guide covers the food stalls.

Seongsu-dong: pop-ups and Korean labels

Ride Line 2 to Seongsu Station and walk out of exit 3 or 4 towards Yeonmujang-gil, the lane that runs through the old shoe-factory blocks. This is where Korean fashion and beauty brands open flagship stores and short pop-ups in converted warehouses, alongside Musinsa’s own shops and Olive Young N Seongsu, a big concept store aimed at visitors. Prices are mid-range, not cheap.

Popular pop-ups use a waiting list. You scan a QR code or type a phone number on a tablet and get a text when it is your turn, so a Korean number or eSIM helps. Weekday mornings are calmest.

Hongdae and Yeonnam-dong: young fashion

Hongik University Station is served by Line 2, AREX and the Gyeongui-Jungang Line, so it is easy straight from Incheon Airport. Exit 9 leads to the main streets of cheap clothing, socks, phone cases, character goods and photo booths; AK& Hongdae sits beside the station. Exit 3 takes you along Gyeongui Line Forest Park into Yeonnam-dong, where the shops are smaller and the cafés quieter. Evenings bring buskers and crowds.

Gangnam: underground malls and COEX

South of the river, some of the best-value shopping is below ground. GOTO Mall runs under the Express Bus Terminal (Lines 3, 7 and 9) with hundreds of small shops selling clothes, shoes, bags and homeware, and the Shinsegae Gangnam department store is directly above. The Gangnam Station underground arcade along Line 2 is similar but smaller. Further east, Starfield COEX Mall at Samseong Station (Line 2) has the Starfield Library and is a good choice on a rainy or freezing day. For boutiques above ground, Garosu-gil near Sinsa Station (Line 3 and the Sinbundang Line) has Korean labels and cafés.

Dongdaemun: malls and the DDP

Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station (Lines 2, 4 and 5) puts you beside the curved Dongdaemun Design Plaza. Doota Mall and the other retail towers around it sell clothing floor by floor and stay open later than most of the city. Many of the neighbouring buildings are wholesale markets that trade overnight with shop owners, not tourists; you can look, but sellers may not sell single items.

Namdaemun and Insadong: markets and gifts

Namdaemun Market starts at Hoehyeon Station exit 5 (Line 4). It is Seoul’s big general market: kitchenware, children’s clothes, ginseng, socks and cheap souvenirs. Eat at the kalguksu alley, where a bowl of knife-cut noodles often comes with a side of bibimbap. Many stalls close on Sundays. See our traditional markets guide for other markets worth the trip.

For gifts with a Korean look, take Line 3 to Anguk Station (exit 6) and walk down Insadong-gil to Ssamziegil, a spiral building of small craft shops. You will find hanji paper, celadon-style ceramics, wooden spoons and chopsticks, and tea. Ikseon-dong’s hanok alleys, near Jongno 3-ga Station, are a five-minute walk away.

Paying, tax refunds and useful Korean

  • Cards: Visa and Mastercard work almost everywhere, including most market stalls. Carry some cash for street food, and a prepaid card such as WOWPASS if your card charges foreign fees.
  • Tax refund: look for “Tax Free” or “Tax Refund” signs and bring your passport. Purchases of 15,000 won or more in one shop qualify, and many shops take the tax off at the till for purchases under 1 million won each, up to 5 million won per trip. Details are in our tax refund guide.
  • Bags: shops may charge a small fee for a bag, so bring a foldable one.
  • Sizes: Korean clothing often runs small, and many cheaper shops are “free size” only. Ask “ibeobwado dwaeyo?” (Can I try it on?) before taking anything to a fitting room; some shops do not allow trying on tops.
  • Prices: “eolmayeyo?” means “How much is it?”. Haggling is rare outside markets.

Shop hours vary by building and pop-ups come and go, so check the shop on Naver Map or KakaoMap before you set out.

Bottom line: For a first trip, do Myeongdong and Seongsu-dong, add Hongdae or the Gangnam underground malls for cheap fashion, and save Insadong for gifts.

FAQ

What is the best shopping district in Seoul for a first-time visitor?

Myeongdong is the easiest: everything is within a few blocks of Myeong-dong Station, staff are used to visitors and most shops offer tax refunds. If you care more about Korean fashion brands than cosmetics, start at Seongsu-dong instead.

Where can I find cheap clothes in Seoul?

GOTO Mall under the Express Bus Terminal, the Gangnam Station underground arcade, the streets around Hongik University Station and Namdaemun Market all have clothes at lower prices than the department stores.

Can I combine two shopping districts in one day?

Yes, if they are close. Myeongdong and Namdaemun are a ten-minute walk apart, Insadong and Ikseon-dong are next to each other, and Seongsu and Dongdaemun are both on Line 2. Crossing from Hongdae to Gangnam takes about 45 minutes by subway, so give that a separate half-day.

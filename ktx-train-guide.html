KTX Train from Seoul to Busan: Tickets, Times and How to Ride

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 15 October 2026 · Updated 22 October 2026

How to take the KTX train from Seoul to Busan: stations, journey times, rough fares, booking in the app, Seoul Station vs Suseo and finding your seat.

Photo: calflier001, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

The KTX train from Seoul to Busan is the easiest way to cross Korea: city centre to city centre in roughly two and a half hours, with no airport security and no check-in. The same steps work on every other KTX route, such as Daejeon, Daegu, Gyeongju and Gangneung.

KTX Seoul to Busan at a glance

  • Journey time: about 2 hours 15 minutes on the fastest trains, closer to 2 hours 45 minutes or more on trains with extra stops. The timetable shows the length of each departure, so compare a few.
  • Stations: trains leave from Seoul Station (서울역) or Suseo Station (수서역) and arrive at Busan Station (부산역). Some Seoul Station trains also stop at Gwangmyeong, south-west of the city.
  • Frequency: trains run from early morning until late evening, often several an hour at busy times.
  • Rough fare: about ₩54,000 one way for a standard seat from Seoul Station since fares were cut in September 2026, and more for first class. Treat this as a guide and check the price shown when you book.

Flights from Gimpo to Gimhae are shorter in the air, but once you add getting to the airports and waiting, the train usually wins for central Busan. Our guide to domestic flights and budget airlines compares the two, and Seoul or Busan? helps if you are still deciding how to split your days.

Seoul Station or Suseo: one KTX, two Seoul starts

Until August 2026 Korea had two high-speed brands on this line: the KTX from Seoul Station and the SRT from Suseo Station, in south-east Seoul near Gangnam, each with its own app. On 1 September 2026 the two operators merged, so the former SRT trains now run as KTX and you can search trains from both stations in one place. Older blog posts and videos that tell you to download a separate SRT app are out of date.

The choice now is simply where you start. Seoul Station suits hotels in the old city, Myeongdong, Hongdae and anywhere on the airport railway. Suseo, on Line 3 and the Suin–Bundang Line, can save a long subway ride if you are staying in Gangnam or Jamsil. When you search, enter “Seoul” or “Suseo” as the departure station and compare both.

How to book KTX train tickets

You have three options, and the first is the easiest for most visitors:

  1. Korail’s app or English website. The app was renewed as Korail+ (코레일+) with the 2026 merger; download it from your phone’s official app store, choose English, enter Seoul (or Suseo) and Busan, pick a date and a train, then pay by card. Foreign cards usually work, though some are declined; trying another card or booking at the station are the fall-backs. Your ticket appears in the app.
  2. Ticket machines at the station. They have an English option and take cards.
  3. The ticket office. Show the staff your destination and time written down, for example “부산 10:00”.

Tickets usually go on sale about a month ahead. Weekday trains often have seats on the day, but Friday evenings, Sunday afternoons and holiday periods such as Seollal and Chuseok sell out early. On those dates, book as soon as you can and keep the booking screen open: seats freed by refunds often reappear.

If no seat is left, some trains sell standing or unreserved tickets for the cars marked 자유석 (free seating) or for the spaces between cars.

Is the Korea Rail Pass worth it?

The Korea Rail Pass (often called the KR Pass) is sold only to foreign visitors and gives unlimited Korail travel for a set number of days. Since the merger, check on Korail’s site whether your pass covers trains from Suseo before relying on it. It pays off if you take several long trips in a short time, such as Seoul to Busan, then Gyeongju, then back to Seoul, all within a few days, as in our 7-day Korea itinerary. For a single return trip to Busan, ordinary tickets are usually cheaper. Add up your planned fares first, and remember that you still need to reserve a seat with the pass.

Choosing a seat

  • Standard (일반실): four seats across, two either side of the aisle. Perfectly comfortable for two and a half hours.
  • First class (특실): three seats across with more legroom. Worth it on a long day or if you want a quieter car.
  • Direction: on some older trains half the seats face backwards, and the booking screen may label them as reverse direction (역방향). Pick a forward seat (순방향) if that bothers you.

Travel day: from the station to your seat

Seoul Station is a large complex shared by the subway (Lines 1 and 4), the airport railway and Korail. Follow the signs for Korail or the KTX up to the main hall, where big departure boards list each train by number, destination and time. Aim to arrive 20 to 30 minutes early the first time.

There are no ticket gates. When your platform number appears on the board, usually about 10 to 15 minutes before departure, walk down. Car numbers are marked on the platform floor or signs, so stand by your car and board there rather than walking the length of a full train. Conductors check tickets on a handheld device, so you only need the ticket on your phone, or the printed one, if asked.

Large suitcases go on the racks at the end of each car; smaller bags fit overhead. There is no restaurant car, so pick up lunch, coffee or a gimbap roll at the station shops before boarding.

Arriving in Busan

Busan Station is on Busan Subway Line 1. Nampo and Jagalchi Market are a few stops south; for Seomyeon, ride Line 1 north and change there to Line 2 for Haeundae and Gwangalli, roughly 40 to 50 minutes in total. A taxi to Haeundae avoids the change if you have heavy bags. A T-money card works on Busan’s subway and buses (see our T-money card guide). For a ready-made plan once you arrive, try One Day in Busan.

Changes and refunds

You can usually cancel or change a ticket in the app where you bought it. Refunds made well before departure have little or no fee, and the fee rises as departure gets closer and again after the train has left. If you miss your train, go to the ticket office straight away rather than boarding the next one: your seat is for one train only, and the app shows the current refund conditions.

Bottom line: book the KTX in Korail’s app a few days ahead (weeks ahead for holidays and Friday evenings), and compare trains from Seoul Station and Suseo, whichever is closer to your hotel.

FAQ

How long is the KTX train from Seoul to Busan?

About 2 hours 15 minutes on the fastest trains and up to around 2 hours 50 minutes on trains with more stops. The booking screen shows the exact time for each departure.

How much is a KTX ticket from Seoul to Busan?

Roughly ₩54,000 one way in standard class from Seoul Station after the September 2026 fare cut, and more in first class. Fares can change, and the Korea Rail Pass may work out cheaper if you take several long trips in a few days.

Can I buy KTX tickets at the station on the day?

Yes, from machines with an English option or at the ticket office. On weekdays there are often seats, but weekends and holidays can sell out, so book in Korail’s app if your time is fixed.

Using Papago and Other Translation Apps in Korea

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 18 October 2026

Learn how to use Papago, Google Translate, and other translation tools for menus, signs, conversations, and everyday tasks while visiting Korea.

Translation apps can make everyday tasks in Korea easier, from checking a menu to asking where to find a train platform. They are useful tools, but their results are not always exact. A little preparation and a few simple habits can help you get clearer translations and communicate more smoothly.

Choose an app that suits the situation

Naver Papago is a popular option for Korean and is designed with translation between Korean and other languages in mind. Google Translate is another useful choice, especially if you already use it or want to switch between different languages. Both apps may offer text, camera, voice, or conversation tools, though features can change and may vary by device or app version.

You do not need to rely on just one app. If a translation seems confusing, check it in another tool or try a different method. For example, type a short sentence instead of using voice input, or show the original Korean text to someone who can help. Treat the results as a guide rather than a guaranteed, word-for-word answer.

Prepare before you need a translation

  • Install the app and try a few translations before your trip. This gives you time to learn where the text, camera, and voice controls are.
  • Check whether the app offers an offline language option. If it does, download Korean and your preferred language while you have a reliable internet connection. Offline tools may have fewer features than online ones.
  • Allow camera or microphone access only if you want to use those functions. You can also type text instead.
  • Save useful information, such as your hotel name and address in Korean, in a note on your phone. An address written in Korean can be easier to show than a spoken attempt.
  • Keep your phone charged. You may also want a screenshot of important directions or reservation details in case you cannot get online.

Use camera translation carefully

Camera translation can help with menus, notices, labels, and signs. Hold your phone steady and make sure the Korean text is in focus. Good lighting helps. If the app struggles with a whole page, move closer to one section or type a few words manually.

Do not assume that every word on a menu has been read correctly. Decorative fonts, handwritten notes, small print, and text arranged vertically can confuse translation tools. A translated dish name may also sound strange because food names often include regional references, cooking methods, or ingredients that do not have a simple English equivalent.

For food allergies or dietary needs, do not depend on a machine translation alone. Prepare a short, clear message in Korean and show it to restaurant staff. Keep the wording specific, and ask for confirmation if the answer is important to your safety. You can use an app to draft a message, but consider checking it with another tool or a Korean speaker when possible.

Make voice conversations easier

Voice translation works best when people speak one at a time, slowly, and in short sentences. Background noise, accents, and unfamiliar place names can lead to errors. Pause after each sentence so the app can finish listening, and check that the recognized words match what was said before relying on the translation.

Keep requests simple. Instead of asking a long question with several details, ask one thing at a time. You might first ask where a station entrance is, then ask whether the entrance is nearby. If the app cannot recognize speech, type the question or show a translated phrase on your screen.

When speaking to someone, briefly explain that you are using a translation app. This gives the other person a moment to wait for the result. Be patient and polite, especially when someone is helping you at a busy counter or station.

Write short, clear messages

Translation apps often handle plain sentences better than slang, jokes, abbreviations, or long paragraphs. Write one idea at a time and use common words. If the translated sentence looks unusually formal or has an unexpected meaning, simplify the original and try again.

Korean speech levels reflect politeness and relationships. An app may produce wording that is understandable but sounds too casual, stiff, or unnatural for the situation. When in doubt, use a polite greeting and a short request. You do not need to master honorifics to be courteous; a calm tone, a smile, and a brief thank-you can help.

Protect personal information

Before entering text into an app, consider whether it contains sensitive details. Avoid translating passwords, payment information, personal identification numbers, or private messages unless you understand how the app handles that information. For hotel or transport details, show only what is needed to solve the immediate problem.

Keep a backup plan

Translation apps are most useful when paired with practical backups. Save important addresses in Korean, take screenshots of directions, and keep the original text visible when asking for help. If an app gives an unclear result, try another translation tool, type fewer words, or point to the item you mean. For an urgent or complicated issue, ask a staff member, your accommodation, or a trusted local contact for help rather than continuing to guess.

Used this way, Papago and other translation apps can reduce everyday stress without needing to replace human help. Prepare a little in advance, check important translations, and keep your requests simple.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a translation app to read Korean menus and signs?

Yes. Many translation apps can translate text through your phone’s camera, which is useful for printed menus, signs, and notices. Results may be less reliable with handwriting, decorative fonts, glare, or unusual layouts. If a translation seems unclear, try taking a sharper photo or ask staff to confirm the dish or direction.

Should I trust a translation app for important conversations?

Treat translations as a helpful aid, not a perfect interpretation. Short, simple sentences usually work better than slang, jokes, or long explanations. For important details such as an address, allergy, or reservation, check the translated text carefully and show it to the other person so they can correct misunderstandings.

Will translation apps work without an internet connection in Korea?

Some apps offer offline language options, but available features can differ, and camera or voice translation may need a connection. Before traveling, check the app’s settings and download any Korean language data it offers while you have Wi-Fi. Keep important addresses or messages saved as text or screenshots as a backup.

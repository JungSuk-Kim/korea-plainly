Korean Café Menu Basics: What to Order and How

Learn common Korean café menu terms, drink and dessert options, and what to expect when ordering.

Korean cafés range from small neighborhood coffee shops to large chains with extensive drink and dessert menus. Many serve familiar options such as espresso and tea, alongside drinks with names or ingredients that may be new to visitors. A little vocabulary can make ordering easier, especially when a menu is mostly in Korean.

Menus and ordering systems vary by café. Some have a staff member at the counter, while others use a touchscreen kiosk. You may also find a printed menu, a display of baked goods, or a menu board above the counter. If you are unsure, pointing to an item on the menu or showing its name on your phone is usually a practical way to communicate.

Start with the coffee basics

  • 아메리카노 (americano): Espresso diluted with hot or cold water. It is a common choice if you want coffee without milk. Look for 핫 (hot) or 아이스 (iced).
  • 카페라떼 (café latte): Espresso with steamed milk when served hot, or milk and espresso over ice when served cold. It is smoother and less intense than an americano.
  • 카푸치노 (cappuccino): Espresso with steamed milk and foam. The balance and serving style can differ between cafés.
  • 카페모카 (café mocha): A coffee drink made with chocolate and milk. It is generally sweeter than a plain latte.
  • 바닐라라떼 (vanilla latte): A latte with vanilla flavoring, often sweetened. If you prefer less sweetness, ask whether the café can reduce the syrup.

Many menus offer drinks hot or iced, but not every drink is available both ways. Some cafés let you choose a size or add an extra espresso shot. Options differ, so check the menu or ask before paying.

Useful words on menus

  • 핫 (hat): Hot. This may appear as a Korean spelling of the English word.
  • 아이스 (aiseu): Iced.
  • 디카페인 (dika페인): Decaf or decaffeinated. Availability can vary, and not every coffee can be made decaf.
  • 샷 추가 (syat chuga): Add a shot of espresso.
  • 시럽 (sireop): Syrup. Ask if you want to know whether a drink is sweetened or whether the amount can be changed.
  • 우유 (uyu): Milk. Alternative milks may be available, but the choices depend on the café.

If you have an allergy or dietary restriction, do not rely only on a drink’s name or appearance. Ingredients and preparation can vary. Ask staff about the specific ingredient, and use a translation app if needed. For a basic question, you can show the Korean name of the ingredient you need to avoid.

Tea, fruit drinks, and other choices

Tea menus may include 녹차 (green tea), 홍차 (black tea), 허브티 (herbal tea), or 밀크티 (milk tea). Some drinks are served plain, while others may be sweetened or combined with milk. Matcha drinks are often listed as 말차 (matcha), and may be made with milk or sweetener. Ask about ingredients if you want a less sweet option.

You may also see drinks labeled 에이드 (ade). In many cafés, an ade is a cold, often sparkling drink flavored with fruit or citrus. Lemon, grapefruit, and other fruit flavors are common possibilities, but menus differ. A smoothie may appear as 스무디 (smoothie), while blended drinks can have their own café-specific names. Check the description or picture rather than assuming every drink is made the same way.

Desserts and bakery items

Look for 케이크 (cake), 빵 (bread), 쿠키 (cookie), and 마카롱 (macaron). Some cafés keep pastries in a display case, so you can point to the item you want. The name, filling, or ingredients may not be obvious from the appearance; ask staff if you need details. Popular Korean café desserts can include 빙수 (bingsu), shaved ice served with toppings. It is often designed to share, though serving sizes and styles vary.

At some cafés, you choose a pastry at the counter before ordering. At others, you order everything together or select items from a self-service display. Follow the signs or watch how the queue moves, and ask if you are unsure whether to take an item to the register.

Ordering and collecting your drink

  1. Choose your drink and temperature. Check whether it is hot or iced, and whether the menu lists a size.
  2. Place the order. Tell the staff the drink name, or select it on the kiosk. If you want it to take away, look for 테이크아웃 (takeout) or 포장 (to-go/packed).
  3. Pay and keep your receipt. Some cafés give you a number or a buzzer. Keep it nearby in case staff call your number.
  4. Collect the order. Drinks may be placed on a pickup counter. Table service and pickup procedures vary, so follow signs or ask.

Useful phrases include 아이스 아메리카노 주세요 (aiseu americano juseyo), meaning “An iced americano, please,” and 포장해 주세요 (pojanghae juseyo), meaning “Please make it to go.” You can also show the item name on the screen and say 주세요 (juseyo), meaning “please” in this ordering context.

Before leaving the counter, check that you have the right drink and any food you ordered. If you are staying, look for signs about where to return trays or sort rubbish; arrangements differ by café. With the drink name, hot-or-iced choice, and a polite 주세요, you can order confidently even when the rest of the menu is unfamiliar.

Bottom line: A little preparation can make your time in Korea much easier. Keep this guide handy and enjoy exploring at your own pace.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can I do if I can’t read the café menu?

Look for English names or photos, and use a translation app if needed. You can point to the item you want and show the screen to the staff. If you have a preference, such as hot or iced, say it or indicate it clearly when ordering.

Do I usually order at the counter or at my table?

Many cafés have you order and pay at the counter, but the setup can vary. Check for signs, watch what other customers do, or ask a staff member where to order. After paying, follow the instructions for collecting your drink or waiting for it.

How do I ask for my drink to go?

Tell the staff you’d like the drink to take away, or say “to go” while ordering. If you want to sit in the café, make that clear too. Staff may ask whether you’re dining in or taking it away, so listen for that question.

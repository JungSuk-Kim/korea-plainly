Hyangilam Hermitage: A Sea-Cliff Temple in Yeosu

By Jungsuk Kim, Korea Plainly · Published 17 October 2026

Photo: 최영민_YOUNGMIN CHOI via Google Maps

At a glance: Hyangilam is a Buddhist hermitage perched above the sea, worth visiting for its rocky approach and sweeping coastal views. The uphill route is part of the experience, so wear shoes you can walk in comfortably.

What Hyangilam is

On the southern coast of Dolsan Island in Yeosu, Hyangilam Hermitage clings to a forested cliff above the water. It is an active Buddhist place of worship as well as a popular destination for visitors drawn by its dramatic setting. The name means “hermitage facing the sun,” and sunrise over the sea is a particular draw.

The hermitage’s origins are traditionally traced to 659, when the monk Wonhyo is said to have established a place of practice here. Today, the combination of temple buildings, steep rock and open sea makes the visit feel quite different from a stroll through a city temple. Google gives Hyangilam a rating of 4.5 from 6,232 reviews.

What to see and do

Take your time on the climb from the approach to the temple. The path rises through trees and rocky terrain, with steps and stretches that can feel steep. In places, the way passes between large rocks, creating narrow passages that are a memorable part of the walk. If you prefer not to squeeze through a tight gap, pause and see whether another route is available rather than rushing.

Once you reach the temple, look around its courtyards and buildings, then find a quiet spot to take in the coastline. The sea views are the main event: on a clear day, the water and distant shorelines open out below the cliff. Give yourself time to enjoy the contrast between the shaded temple grounds and the bright, expansive view.

You do not need to be Buddhist to visit, but remember that this is a working religious site. Keep your voice low, follow signs and barriers, and avoid interrupting people who are praying. If you take photos, be considerate of worshippers and temple activities.

Planning your visit

Allow around 1.5 to 2 hours for the uphill walk, a look around the hermitage and a few pauses for the views. The exact time depends on your pace and how long you stay at the top. The climb is not ideal for a rushed stop, and visitors with limited mobility may find the uneven, steep sections challenging.

Early morning is a lovely time to visit if you want to see the sunrise, but it means planning your transport and arrival carefully. Morning light also suits the sea views and can make the walk feel cooler. Later in the day, the scenery is still rewarding; choose a time that gives you enough daylight for the climb and return.

Hyangilam is in Dolsan-eup, on the southern part of Dolsan Island. Use “Hyangilam Hermitage” or the Korean name 향일암 in your map app, and check your route from Yeosu before setting out. You can make it part of a wider day exploring Dolsan and Yeosu’s coast, or pair it with the city’s waterfront sights on a separate part of your visit. Avoid trying to fit too many stops around the climb.

Good to know

Wear sturdy, comfortable footwear with a good grip; the approach includes slopes, steps and uneven ground. Bring water, especially on a warm day, and take breaks when you need them. Rain can make stone surfaces slippery, so walk carefully and pay attention to the path.

Dress respectfully for a temple visit and keep noise to a minimum around prayer areas. Before you go, check current hours or prices on Naver Map, as practical details can change. The address is 1 Hyangiram-ro, Dolsan-eup, Yeosu, Jeollanam-do.

FAQ

Is Hyangilam Hermitage worth visiting?

Yes, especially if you enjoy coastal scenery and do not mind an uphill walk. Its cliffside setting, rocky approach and sea views make it one of Yeosu’s distinctive temple visits.

How much time should I allow?

Plan for about 1.5 to 2 hours to make the climb, explore the temple and enjoy the view, adjusting the time to your pace.

Is the walk difficult?

The route includes steep sections, steps and uneven ground, so comfortable shoes and a steady pace help. It may be challenging for visitors with limited mobility.
